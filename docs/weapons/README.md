# Weapons, rated

Every weapon in the player's active mods, put through a fight by the game's own rules and rated from 1.0 to 10.0, with
what it is good and weak at, its special effects and a line in plain words. Built by `Tools/WeaponReview` from the
numbers `Tools/weapon_extract.py` reads out of the player's own def files (the game, its five DLCs and every active mod,
as RimWorld 1.7 loads them for the player's mod list, `MayRequire` and `MayRequireAnyOf` honoured as the game honours them),
with the same arithmetic the mod will use in the game (`Source/ColonyCouncil/Weapons/WeaponRateMath.cs`).
The design and the player's request are in `docs/design/weapons.md`; `weapons.json` beside this file holds everything
below for a tier-list page and for recommending weapons later.

**747 weapon defs**: 486 rated, 1 a colonist can wield but whose attack is wholly a mod's
own code (named below), and 260 left out because a colonist can't wield them (turret guns, mechanoids' and
animals' built-in weapons, weapons made by a spell; listed at the end). 72 of the rated ones are marked *(rough)*:
47 are unique weapons whose traits are rolled when made (rated as the plain weapon), the other 25 do
something the review can name but not value (see "Where the numbers are less sure").

## How to read a rating

- **The rating** says how much a weapon gets done in the fights it is made for, against the other weapons of its kind:
  guns, bows and launchers on one scale, melee weapons on another. 5.5 is the middle: about an assault rifle among guns,
  a steel longsword or warhammer among melee weapons. Near the middle, a point up is about a third more damage dealt for
  a gun and a fifth more for a melee weapon; the scale flattens toward both ends, so a mod's weapon three or ten times
  the best of the base game lands at 9-something rather than all of them sitting at 10.
- **What it is for** (its role) decides what counts: a sniper rifle is judged mostly at 25 and 40 cells and isn't marked
  down for being poor at five, a shotgun mostly within a dozen cells, a grenade on what it does to a group.
- **Sub-ratings** use the same scale. For guns: close (5 cells), medium (12), long (25) and far (40); then unarmoured,
  armoured (flak), heavy (marine armour) and mechanoids at the distances its role cares about; and a crowd. For melee:
  unarmoured, armoured, heavy and mechanoids.
- *(rough)* after a rating: part of what the weapon does is a mod's own code the review can't read, so it is rated on
  what it can read and the rest is named. Trust those numbers less.

## How the rating works

A colonist with the weapon fights the reference enemies by the game's own rules, read from the game itself:

- **Hitting.** A shot's chance to hit is the shooter's accuracy to the power of the distance in cells, times the weapon's
  accuracy at that distance (its close, short, medium and long accuracy at 3, 12, 25 and 40 cells, in between by a straight
  line), times the target's size. Open ground, clear weather, daylight, no cover. Melee: the fighter's melee hit chance,
  then the target's dodge.
- **Armour.** Each hit meets the armour on the part it strikes: armour minus the weapon's piercing is the chance to be
  halved, half of it the chance to be stopped outright, and nothing gets through from twice the armour. Sharp, blunt and
  heat each meet their own armour.
- **Firing.** A gun aims (its warm-up), fires its burst and rests (its cooldown); damage per second is over that whole cycle.
  Melee weapons strike with the attack the game picks: its best attacks three times in four, its middling ones (the fists
  among them) one in four, the weak ones never, exactly as the game weighs them.
- **Explosions** land within their scatter or near a miss and hurt whoever is inside the blast; a fuse lets a target on the
  move walk out (a fifth of the throws lost per second of fuse). Against a bunched group, a blast catches one more enemy in
  every eight cells (four at most), a missed bullet strays into someone else one time in seven, a beam strikes the one
  behind. A blast near your own line is a risk, so blasts lose a few hundredths per cell of radius.
- **Beyond damage.** A second an enemy spends stunned counts as 4 damage (8 for a mechanoid); EMP stuns a mechanoid for half a
  second a point, then it is immune for 37 seconds; a target set alight counts as 10 more damage; toxic buildup counts 40
  damage per full dose, a tox gas cloud 8. Heavier hits count a little more, up to a third more at 30 damage a hit, for what
  one blow can take out (a brain, a heart, a neck, a leg). Stopping power that staggers a man adds 3%, one that staggers
  big beasts 6%.
- **Reach.** A gun that can't reach as far as a raider's assault rifle (31 cells) has to close in under fire: a quarter off
  at 12 cells, nothing off from 31. Damage dealt from beyond 31 cells counts double: the usual raider can't answer it.
- **Each distance on one footing.** Hitting gets harder with distance for everyone; each distance is judged against what a
  good shot can do there (the reference shooter's own falloff is taken out), so a sniper's 40 cells and a shotgun's 5 compare.
- **One use.** A launcher spent in one volley spreads that volley over a 30-second fight.
- **What it does to its wielder.** Stats a weapon changes while held count where they belong: shooting accuracy and melee hit
  as more skill, aiming time and cooldowns in the cycle, move speed (a slower carrier: half its share for a gunner, all of
  it for a melee fighter), dodge, damage taken and pain. Stats with no part in a fight (work speed, psychic sensitivity,
  comfort) are named with their sign, not counted.
- **Abilities a weapon grants** count when their defs or code say what they do: a launched projectile, a beam or a burst
  of flame, rated like a shot and spread over a minute's fight (its charges, or as often as its cooldown allows), adding
  at most half again to the weapon's own worth (a few charges help a fight, they don't carry it). Abilities whose effect
  can't be read are named and marked, not counted.

### What the mods' own code does, and what is counted

The player's mods' code (their DLLs, decompiled to read, never run) was read wherever a weapon's effect lives there:

- **Vanilla Expanded Framework**: its flame streams and gauss slugs are a line from the gun that strikes whoever stands on
  it, once (`dealsDamageOnce`) or every so many ticks of its life; its laser capacitor, in quick-fire mode, takes a set
  share off the aim with each shot from the same spot (counted over a stand of ten shots), and at full heat each shot can
  blast the shooter (tagged *Can hurt you*); weapons with two firing modes are rated in their best; heavy weapons only go to
  someone in an exoframe or with the Tough trait (tagged *Restricted*) and lose hit points with each shot.
- **VFE Medieval 2**: a matchlock fires several balls at once (its `shotCount`), each twice as hard point blank and fading to
  nothing at its range, and leaves smoke around the shooter; the warbow's arrows arc over cover; the heraldic standard rallies
  its bearer (named, not counted).
- **A RimWorld of Magic**: the Staff of Blazing Power's bolt is a blast that ignores armour, and only a mage can fire it; the fire
  wand's blast burns through armour, the ice wand's shard pierces it, the lightning wand's sparks stun nearby; Light and Dark
  damage meet only the mod's own alignment armour, which ordinary armour doesn't give; a cleaving weapon strikes the target
  for 60% more and everyone beside it for 60%; a deflecting weapon turns aside its base chance plus a share per level of
  Melee (counted as defence). Winter's Reign launches its own flying objects and isn't rated.
- **VWE Bioferrite**: the heavy incinerator sprays like the game's incinerator, its abilities shoot like guns or spray like
  the incinerator, all counted. **VFE Settlers**: dynamite's fuse runs 20 to 666 ticks, and a quarter go off as they land.
  **VFE Tribals**' throwspikes, the Fairycore wands' orbs and the cryptoforge bolter fly as bullets do (the wands need someone
  who can channel; the orbs' and the cryptoforge's extra effects are named, not valued).
- **The game's own code**, for weapons whose class it names: the doomsday rocket adds three fire blasts around its impact;
  the fire arrow spreads fuel and starts a fire where it lands; the incinerator and beam weapons sweep a path across the
  target, striking it on its share of the burst's ticks with no roll to hit.

Not read, so named and marked *(rough)*: Rimsenal Federation's crucible damage, VWE Non-Lethal's tear gas, anaesthetic darts and
conditional stun, the cryptoforge's slowing, VFE Pirates' gravity hammer's push, VQE Ancients' scalpel, Alpha Genes' black hydra
bullet and the Fairycore wands' orb effects (their code wasn't in the upload or their effect is a hediff of their own).

For step 2, the weapon mods' own defaults (no settings file of theirs in the player's game, so they run on them): Simple
Sidearms' default preset (Basic) carries melee sidearms up to 1.9 kg and ranged ones up to 2.7 kg, two of each and three in
all; Dual Wield takes any weapon in the off hand but a bow, a blowgun, one over 3 kg or a melee weapon over 1.5 kg, and counts
bows, shotguns and anything over 3 kg two-handed. The *Sidearm*, *Offhand* and *Two-handed* tags follow these.

Not counted: cover, weather, darkness, quality (all rated at normal), bionics, moving while shooting (RunAndGun), Melee
Animation's moves, a mod's own code (named instead). Patches from other mods are not applied, as in the mod pool; the ones
that touch a weapon are listed below.

Where hits land is the human body's own part coverage. The mix the overall weighs: a quarter plain raiders, two fifths in
flak, a fifth in marine armour, the rest mechanoids, as a colony meets them after its first year (light melee weapons
lean a little more on everyday fights: 40/35/15/10). EMP weapons that harm no flesh are judged three fifths on mechanoids.

## Tags

Every weapon carries a few tags, given by rules on its own numbers (never by hand), each with a line saying why. A weapon
shows its most important few, at most seven, keeping its role, its first strength and its first weakness. Six categories,
each a colour from Rimfolk's own palette (readable at 4.5:1 or better on its window, panel and card grounds) and a symbol,
so the colour is never the only cue. The tier-list page and the game's weapon card use these same colours and symbols.
