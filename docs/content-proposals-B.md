# Content proposals B: illnesses, dilemmas, customs, wishes, moods and life moments

A list to choose from, as the rules ask. Every item is something new, not more text for something that exists, and
each starts from what the colony really does. Your save shows a stone-block bill kept at 430, a yayo bill, cigarettes
and joints, sixteen coffee and tea bills, Alpha Bees hives, lavish meals, and prosthetic limbs on the machining bench.
Events, world situations, visitors, camps and encounters are in the other list.

Rimfolk has 64 ailments, 189 dilemmas (22 of them seasonal customs) and 116 wishes. No ailment comes from cleaning,
animals, raw food or smoking. Research, prisoners, Ideology's precepts and slaves, and the weather have no dilemmas.
Summer has four customs, two of which need water or children. Nothing covers drug withdrawal or a new limb.

**On the map** is what you see and the existing piece that shows it. **[new: ...]** marks a small piece of code still
needed. Effort: S (defs and existing workers), M (a small new effect or check). Only unlocked content, nobody hurt
without a real cause, and no countdowns in any text.

The ailments: a colonist already has an everyday ailment 17% of the time in the balance runs, above the 10 to 15%
target. Any you choose would be short, mild and preventable, and paid for by trimming the commonest ones, so the total
doesn't rise.

## Illnesses and ailments

1. **Gout** (S). A hot, swollen big toe in someone 45 or older who eats well. It's rolled after fine or lavish meals and
   after drinks (both triggers exist) and halved when they've had fruit or vegetables lately (the diet record is already
   kept). **On the map:** a limp (Moving down 15%) and some pain for three to six days; it can come back.
   **Prevention:** simpler meals with vegetables, fewer drinks. The first ailment that comes from living well.

2. **Stone-dust cough** (S). From hours of mining or cutting stone blocks in a closed room. **On the map:** a dry cough
   when they speak, and Breathing down 8% for two to four days. **When:** a stonecutting bill is running, or there's
   mining under the mountain. **Prevention:** a large or open stone workshop, taking turns.
   [new: a counted trigger for mining jobs and stone-block bills]

3. **Sore knees** (S). From hours on the knees scrubbing floors or sowing. **On the map:** Moving down 6% for a few
   days. **Prevention:** more than one cleaner. [new: a counted trigger for cleaning and sowing jobs]

4. **Worms** (S). From eating raw meat or raw fish (Rimfolk already sees what is eaten), never from cooked food.
   **On the map:** they get hungry sooner (hunger rate up 25%) and eat more, until a tend with medicine clears it.
   **Prevention:** cook it. Anyone with the strong-stomach gene never gets it. [new: genes as a risk factor]

5. **Ringworm** (S to M). An itchy ring on the skin from handling animals (milking, shearing, training, taming).
   Children are likelier to get it. It passes to bedmates in a dirty room, by the rules lice already use. **On the map:**
   a small itch mood for a week, less with tending. **Prevention:** clean beds and barns, washing (Dubs Bad Hygiene).
   [new: a counted trigger for Handling work]

6. **Smoker's cough** (S). Rolled per joint or cigarette, as drinks are rolled now. **On the map:** a morning cough and
   Breathing down 5% for a few days, likelier with daily smoking. The game's own drug effects stay as they are.
   **Prevention:** fewer smokes, or the existing wish for help getting off a drug. [new: a per-smoke trigger]

7. **Bee stings** (S). From working the beehouses (Alpha Bees' own jobs, found by name, so without the mod it never
   comes). **On the map:** a swollen hand for a day (Manipulation down 5%, a little pain). A beekeeper with Animals skill
   is stung less. Nothing worse ever comes of it.

## Dilemmas

8. **Who gets the new leg** (M). **When:** a replacement limb or eye is in storage and two colonists are missing that
   part, and neither's beliefs rule it out. **Answers:**
   - Fit it to A, or fit it to B: the one passed over is hurt and trusts the council less, and the two cool towards
     each other.
   - Whoever's work needs it most (hands for a crafter or a doctor, legs for a hauler or a hunter): less bitterness, but
     the kind find it cold.

   **On the map:** the game's own operation appears on the chosen one's medical tab.
   [new: an effect that adds the operation bill]

9. **A bad batch** (S to M). **When:** the game's food poisoning hits two or more colonists from one cook's meals
   within a day. **Answers:**
   - Scrub the kitchen top to bottom: a work party cleans the kitchen's real filth, and the cook keeps the stove and
     their pride.
   - Someone else cooks for a while: the cooking goes to the next best and is handed back later. The cook is stung, and
     the meals may be plainer.
   - Blame the meat: nothing changes, and the sick grumble.

   [new: the kitchen as a place for work parties; a hand-over of work that undoes itself, using the planning helper's
   undo]

10. **The precept broken** (S to M). Ideology. **When:** someone has the game's own memory of breaking one of the
    colony's precepts (meat under a vegetarian creed, drugs where they're forbidden) and the devout are upset.
    **Answers:**
    - A quiet word: the moral guide or the leader sits with them. They settle, and the devout feel let down.
    - Penance: they clean the altar room and the shrines for a day. The devout grow more certain, and their own mood
      dips.
    - The council stands by them: the free spirits approve, and the devout trust the council less.

    [new: the altar as a place for work parties]

11. **The doctor is the patient** (S to M). **When:** the colony's only real doctor (the next best has half their
    Medicine or less) is ill or hurt and needs tending. **Answers:**
    - They tend themselves: the game's self-tend is switched on for them, at lower quality.
    - The next best tends them while the doctor talks them through from the bed: a bedside visit, and real Medicine
      practice for the helper.
    - Send for a healer from an ally or a camp (the visiting doctor already exists), for silver or goodwill.

    [new: a self-tend effect]

12. **The heat wave's hours** (M). **When:** the game's heat wave is on and people are working outdoors. **Answers:**
    - Work by night and sleep through the midday heat: timetables shift until the heat wave ends, then go back. Night
      work risks sleepless nights.
    - A midday rest in the shade: the hottest hours become recreation, and less gets done.
    - Carry on: the hard workers approve, and heatstroke stays the game's.

    [new: a timetable shift tied to the condition, reusing the decrees' timetable code]

13. **So close** (M). **When:** the current project is three-quarters done and its
    researcher has been at the bench into the small hours. **Answers:**
    - Let them work through tonight: their timetable for one night is work, and the progress is the game's own
      research. They sleep in next day and risk a sleepless night.
    - Send them to bed (the existing rest): they're restless.
    - A second pair of eyes: another researcher works beside them as a lesson. Progress is slower, but the helper learns
      and the two grow closer.

    [new: a one-night timetable change]

14. **Lessons through the bars** (M). **When:** a prisoner is far better (10 or more, five above them) at a skill a
    colonist loves. **Answers:**
    - Lessons at the cell: the colonist sits with the prisoner an hour a day and learns. The prisoner softens a little
      (the existing resistance effect), and the wardens are uneasy.
    - Recruit them first: the wardens are set to recruiting, and the colonist waits.
    - No contact: the colonist is disappointed, and the cautious approve.

    [new: a sit-down visit into a cell]

15. **The drug lab** (S to M). **When:** a bill for a hard drug (yayo, flake, go-juice, wake-up) is running and someone
    objects: a former addict, a parent, or someone whose faith forbids it. **Answers:**
    - Stop the hard drugs: those bills are suspended (you can resume them). Silver is lost, the objector is grateful,
      and the greedy grumble.
    - Stop every drug but medicine: stronger both ways.
    - Keep making them: the objector's mood and trust go down.

16. **A slave's freedom** (M). Ideology. **When:** a slave who has lived in the colony a season or more has a friend
    among the colonists, and the friend asks for them. **Answers:**
    - Free them into the colony: the game's own emancipation makes them a colonist, and supremacist minds object.
    - Free them to go home: they're walked to the gate and go, and their people think better of the colony.
    - Not now: the friend is hurt and trusts the council less.

    [new: an emancipation effect]

## Seasonal customs and festivals

17. **Spring cleaning** (S). **When:** spring, the first days over 10 °C, with real filth and clutter about.
    **Answers:**
    - All hands for a day: a work party of up to eight cleans, then hauls, then there's a toast. Other work waits, and
      the tidy are delighted.
    - The morning only: four people for a few hours.
    - Not this year: the tidy grumble.

18. **The bounds walk** (S to M). Summer, any land. **When:** a warm, dry day. **Answers:**
    - Everyone walks the colony's edge with the children: a stone is set at each corner the first year (the existing
      marker stones) and found again in later years. The children learn the land; a morning's work is lost.
    - The elders walk it alone: shorter, and the young miss out.
    - Not this year.

    [new: a daytime route round the edge, from the night watch's patrol, with stones at its stops]

19. **The star night** (S). Summer. **When:** a clear, warm night (14 °C or more, no rain or fog). **Answers:**
    - Everyone sits out late: a gathering in the open from nine, and a later start next morning.
    - The children and the stargazer for an hour.
    - Bed as usual.

20. **The fishing match** (M). Summer, with Odyssey's fishing zones (or Vanilla Fishing Expanded's on its own).
    **When:** a fishing zone with fish in it. **Answers:**
    - A match and a fish supper: a work party fishes with the game's own fishing job, and the catch is cooked by the
      existing feast cooking.
    - A quiet afternoon for two.
    - Not now.

    [new: fishing among the work party's jobs]

21. **The autumn hunt** (S). Fall. **When:** the meat in store won't last the winter, there's safe game on the map, and
    someone can hunt. **Answers:**
    - A big hunt: up to three hunters after several animals (the existing hunting party), and fresh meat for supper.
    - One hunter, one animal.
    - No hunt: the colony buys or slaughters instead.

22. **Telling the bees** (S to M). With beehives (Alpha Bees). **When:** a death, a birth or a wedding in the colony.
    **Answers:**
    - The family walks with the beekeeper from hive to hive to tell them the news.
    - The beekeeper goes alone.
    - It isn't done: the superstitious are uneasy. Nothing happens to the bees, and no text says it will.

## Wishes

23. **A grave for {PET}** (S to M). **When:** a named or bonded animal dies and its body lies whole at home. Its person
    wants it buried, not butchered. **On the map:** granted, they dig a grave near the colony and carry the animal into
    it (the enemy-dead burial's digging and hauling, for one animal). The journal lines about a small grave become
    true.

24. **Something from home in the fields** (S to M). In spring, someone misses a crop the colony could sow now but
    doesn't: a fruit, a vegetable, a flower (modded crops too). It's fulfilled when a growing zone of it is sown.
    **On the map:** the crop comes up. [new: a worker that picks a sowable crop nobody grows]

25. **Something green in winter** (S). **When:** fall or winter in a land with a real winter, with power, and with
    sun lamps or hydroponics researched. A grower, or someone who has gone without fresh food, wants a sun lamp over an
    indoor field. It gives the scurvy and fresh-food troubles a fix to ask for.

26. **A lamp at the bench** (S to M). **When:** someone has worked a bench or researched in the dark (those hours are
    already counted for eye strain). It's fulfilled when a lamp lights that bench. [new: a check that the bench is lit]

27. **A roof over the way** (M). **When:** in a wet land, someone got soaked between the bedrooms and the table (the
    game's own soaked memory). It's fulfilled when that way is roofed. [new: the way's open cells, worked out once]

## Moods, quirks and nuisances

28. **The butcher's table** (S). A nuisance: someone sleeps or eats in a room with a butcher's table or spot, and the
    smell gets to them. The fix is plain to see: move it. (The "clean hands" ambition already covers kitchens.)

29. **A draught at the bed** (S). A nuisance: below 5 °C outside, a bed a few steps from a door straight to the
    outside. Unlike a cold room, the room can be warm on average. The fix is an anteroom.

30. **Makes the first pot** (S to M). A quirk for an early riser: on mornings when there's coffee or tea in the stores,
    they take a cup to a friend or two (the existing rounds, serving a drink). It only comes with a mod that makes
    coffee or tea. [new: rounds that serve a drink]

## Life moments

31. **Getting clean** (M). An arc for someone in the game's own drug withdrawal:
    - A friend sits with them in the evenings (a visit).
    - The friend stays with them one bad night (a vigil at their side).
    - When the addiction is gone, the friend raises a cup at supper (a toast).

    If they take the drug again (the game's own craving), the arc ends without blame and can start over.
    [new: an arc worker that reads addictions]

32. **The new leg** (M). An arc after the game's own operation fits a replacement limb: first steps with a friend at
    their side (a short walk together), a frustrating day, the first full day's work, and pride. It feeds the existing
    "whole again" ambition.

33. **Morning coffee** (S). A moment: two colonists who drink coffee or tea at the same table in the same hour share a
    word (in the social log and the bubbles), and their friendship grows a little. It needs a coffee or tea mod.

34. **A reader's year** (S to M). An aspiration: read a number of different books from the colony's shelves (the
    game's own reading). [new: a count of different books read]

If you'd like only a few, these would change play the most: 8 (who gets the new leg), 9 (a bad batch), 1 (gout),
17 and 18 (spring cleaning, the bounds walk), 23 (a grave for a pet), 12 (the heat wave's hours) and 31 (getting
clean).
