# Peoples, the third round: eight more, and peoples whose age changes

The player's words (8 November 2026), in order:
- "I really love the factions. I want more of them. And it looked to me that the creative factions were all more ... not
  primitive, but not necessarily modern ... definitely not spacer level, but also not really industrial ... I just
  wanted kind of variety in that regard as well, kind of different generations or time spans represented in factions,
  or that the factions actually kind of scale also with time ... I just want to, you know, have all the ages covered
  with our factions, and just add some more factions or work on some additional ones as well."
- On the eight below: "i love all your faction proposals!"
- On ages that change: "also changing over time: do both" and "our older factions should also be able to grow and
  develop over time to reach higher research levels".

Where things stood: the game keeps every faction at the tech level it was made with, for good (raids grow with the
colony's wealth, not its tech). Rimfolk's fifteen peoples were 2 tribal, 7 medieval, 5 industrial and 1 spacer, each
fixed at its def's age; only peoples arising in play took another age, chosen once.

The design philosophy of `peoples-next.md` holds for every people here: a signature then variety, uniform where it makes
sense, gifts for a share, xenotypes and races from the whole pool, every age, flavour and lore as much as possible, the
colony is the family, harm only at the game's rate and shown. Everything is drawn from the player's enabled mods
(`docs/player-mods/`, active rows), softly, with a vanilla fallback.

## Ages that change over time (both ways the player chose)

1. **Over the years.** Every people drifts: as years pass, each may rise an age (tribal to medieval to industrial to
   spacer), at a pace set by its ways. A people that holds to its ways rises slowly and not past what it would accept
   (the Mossborn and the herders keep close to the land and rise late, if at all past medieval); a people of learning
   or trade rises sooner.
2. **Through the colony.** Peoples the colony trades with, gives books, techprints, components or tools to, teaches
   (its learned exchanges, apprentices, guests) or is allied with rise faster; peoples cut off from the world (their
   towns burned, at war with everyone, no trade for years) can fall back an age.
3. **Both at once**, every people old and new, with one exception that runs the other way: the Sunken Vault starts
   high and declines unless someone keeps it supplied.

What a change of age means, seen in play: their gear, weapons, kinds of people and gifts take the form of the new age
(the ages' kits each people already has), their towns' plans and new buildings follow it, their words and titles
shift where the age has its own, their traders' goods change, and a letter tells the colony when it happens ("the
Wardens' smiths have learned to work steel"). The people's page shows its age and which way it is heading. Saved per
people: its age now and its progress.

## The eight new peoples (approved as written; details are the builder's within this)

### 1. The Signal Line: keepers of the radio masts (industrial)
*"Every mast is a voice. Every voice is a road nobody has to walk."*
A chain of radio masts runs across the region, raised by a lineman who walked the hills with a coil of copper wire
after the long war cut every road. The Line keeps them standing, relays news and letters, and sells what it hears.
- Look: linesmen share oilskins with a copper aerial badge (the linesmen's kit); operators, clerks and families vary
  freely around the badge.
- Arms and gifts: rifles and climbing gear; a share are crafters and intellectuals; with Royalty a few hear the psychic
  hum in the static.
- Towns: a mast on the highest ground, a relay hut, a cable yard.
- At the gate: they ask to raise a relay mast on the colony's highest ground (letters reach the colony and its
  neighbours faster, and a mast warns of a raid a little before it comes); they sell news and buy components and
  copper; a lineman climbs the colony's mast in a storm to keep it singing.
- Titles: chief lineman, operator, relay-keeper, wire-trader.

### 2. The Deepwell Union: drillers of the boomtowns (industrial)
*"The ground keeps what it's given. We go down and ask for it back."*
Miners and drillers who struck it rich and then lost it all when the seam ran dry, and stayed together anyway. The
union's book holds every member and every shift; a strike is called by vote, never by one man.
- Look: hard hats, lamp and work clothes, varied by trade; signature: the union's red armband.
- Arms and gifts: picks, shotguns, dynamite (soft, from the player's mods where they have it); tough and strong
  miners; a few with the player's mining genes.
- Towns: a headframe, a boarding house, a slag heap, a union hall.
- At the gate: they offer to drill the colony's deep ground for a share of what comes up; they sell steel, components
  and blasting goods; a strike stalls their trade until the colony helps settle it; mining accidents only at the game's
  rate, through the shared harm budget.
- Titles: union boss, shift-captain, driller, assayer.

### 3. The Last Republic: what is left of the old government (industrial)
*"The Republic never fell. It simply has fewer citizens than it used to."*
A planetary government that collapsed everywhere but in a handful of towns that kept the archives, the ration cards
and the radio. They hold elections, keep a census and broadcast the news as they wish it were.
- Look: the Civic Guard wears one uniform (the clearest after the Ashen Banner); clerks and citizens in drab varied
  civilian clothes with a ribbon in the Republic's colours.
- Arms and gifts: the Guard's issued rifles and helmets; a share are bureaucrats and speakers; with Royalty a
  propaganda officer or two with a psylink.
- Towns: a town hall with a flag, an archive, a ration office, a guardhouse.
- At the gate: a census-taker walks the colony and counts its people; the Republic offers citizenship papers (better
  trade terms, a duty owed); it may ask the colony for a conscript for a season (the colonist goes and comes back, or
  doesn't, by the world's own dangers); its radio may praise or denounce the colony.
- Titles: the president, minister, census-taker, quartermaster.

### 4. The Hollow Fleet: the colony ship's crew, still at their posts (spacer)
*"We were promised a world. We are still waiting for orders to disembark."*
Their ship came down two hundred years ago and never flew again; the crew's descendants still keep its watches, ranks
and logs in the grounded hull, and still hope to lift.
- Look: ship's uniform for every rank (the uniform rule: a crew), worn and patched; officers with rank insignia.
- Arms and gifts: charge rifles and ship-issue gear; a share of mechanitors and psycasters; many non-baseliners from
  long-ago gene work.
- Towns: the grounded hull, a landing pad, hydroponics.
- At the gate: a shuttle lands; they buy ship parts, AI cores and ship chunks the colony salvages; they trade
  cryptosleep caskets and spacer goods; a sleeper from their caskets, woken too late, asks the colony for shelter.
- Titles: captain, first officer, quartermaster, deckhand.

### 5. The Helix Works: gene-crafters (spacer, with Biotech)
*"Every body is a draft. We are only editors."*
A guild of geneticists who outlived the lab that made them, and kept working. Most of them are their own work.
- Look: lab coats and clean suits, varied beneath; signature: a helix pin.
- Arms and gifts: needle guns and spacer pistols; mostly non-baseliners, drawn from every xenotype in the player's mods;
  mechanitors among them.
- Towns: a gene lab, a growth vat hall, a quiet garden.
- At the gate: they sell xenogerms and gene work; they buy genepacks; they ask for a sample from a colonist with rare
  genes (a small blood draw, shown); a batch of theirs gone wrong may ask the colony's help. Without Biotech they trade
  medicine and drugs and keep their lore.
- Titles: chief editor, sequencer, vat-keeper, sample-buyer.

### 6. The Lattice: mechanitors and their mechs (spacer, with Biotech)
*"A mind alone is a candle. A lattice is a lamp."*
A collective of mechanitors who link their mechs into one working whole. Quiet, exact, and stranger than they know.
- Look: mechanitor robes and bandwidth gear, varied; signature: a lattice pattern on the hood.
- Arms and gifts: mechanitors with their mechs; light weapons; a few psycasters.
- Towns: a mech gestation hall, charging yards, a control tower.
- At the gate: mech caravans; they repair the colony's mechs; a lost mech of theirs may wander onto the map (return it,
  or keep it at a cost to goodwill); they buy subcores and mech parts. Without Biotech they come as engineers with
  turrets and keep their lore.
- Titles: the node, link-warden, mech-tender, part-trader.

### 7. The Choir of the Spire: listeners at the archotech (beyond spacer)
*"The Spire is thinking. We are what it thinks about."*
A people living in the shadow of an archotech spire, changed by it over generations: calm, kind, and unsettling.
Their technology is beyond anything else in the region, and they barely use it.
- Look: pale robes and archite marks, varied; no armour.
- Arms and gifts: most are psycasters (Royalty and Vanilla Psycasts Expanded), archite genes with Biotech; they rarely
  fight, and when they do it is with the mind. With Anomaly they know things about the void that others don't.
- Towns: a ring of quiet halls round a monolith.
- At the gate: they come to "listen" to the colony (a psychic calm for some, unease for others, shown as moods); they
  trade psychic gear and rare archotech goods for art; one may stay a while to learn what it is to be ordinary.
- Titles: the first voice, cantor, listener, keeper of the hum.

### 8. The Sunken Vault: the shelter that opened (spacer, falling)
*"The doors opened. The world we were waiting for didn't come with them."*
A sealed shelter whose people came up after generations below, with spacer gear and failing power cells. They cannot
make what they carry, and every year a little more of it stops working.
- Look: vault suits with a number on the back (the vault's own), wearing out; later ages patch them with whatever is
  at hand.
- Arms and gifts: spacer weapons at first, then what they can make; a share of intellectuals and medics.
- Towns: a vault door in a hillside, a surface camp growing round it.
- Ages: they start at spacer and fall an age every few years unless supplied with components and parts (the colony can
  be that supplier, and they remember).
- At the gate: they trade their last spacer goods for components and food; they ask the colony to teach them a craft
  they have lost; a vault-born child who has never seen a sky asks to stay.
- Titles: the overseer, warden, technician, surface-scout.

Each comes with an emblem and town plan, a faith with Ideology, messengers, letters and talk lines in its own voice,
touchpoints at the gate and a mission, and its names for peoples that arise during play. None is required, all rarely
raid, and each is listed once in a new world.
