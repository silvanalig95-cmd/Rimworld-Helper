# Rimfolk's own gods: the brainstorm, third version (11 November 2026)

The player, on the second version:

> "now all these gods seem very human like. I mean, human names, human characteristic, human shapes, that should
> really not be the standard ... it could be a god that is really not human at all, like for example some other shape
> like an animal or a dragon or whatnot, but it could be for example kind of all of plant life combined, like a plant
> life presence that could be a god, or the sky could be a god, or um, some really concept of any kind of imagination.
> I don't want each god to kind of be this checklist of characteristics ... every god should be completely individual
> and separate in what it means, what it looks like, if it looks like at all, what it represents, what it is important
> in, what kind of effects it even impacts the world or not, what it perhaps cares about, what it tries to do, etc. So
> in that sense, really abandon this Greek pantheon idea ... come up with some new god concepts"

Earlier:
- "gods should really feel like godly characters pulling the strings of the universe, not just another menu with a
  list of values, names and symbols";
- "gods should cover all kinds of designs, concepts, ideas, forms or no form".

The second version (human-shaped gods, a royal family) is dropped; it stays in git history. Nothing here is built.

## How these are written

- **No template.** Each god is written as what it is, at the length it needs. Some have wants, some have none. Some act
  every season, some once in a long game. Some notice the colony, some never will.
- **Not human, as a rule.** A few take a human shape sometimes, and one is nothing but human shapes. Most are a
  presence, a process, a beast, a place, a sound, a number, an absence.
- **Names are what people call them.** Most gods have no name of their own. People call them by what they are ("the
  Green That Remembers", "Above"), and every people calls them differently. A tribe's word, a medieval title and a
  spacer's catalogue number are all names for the same thing.
- **The gods are behind the world's events.** The game's own events (meteorites, migrations, blights, cold snaps,
  wanderers, infestations) have a hand in them, and the colony reads it there. That is how the gods pull the strings
  without a menu (section C).

## A. The gods

### 1. The Green That Remembers
All plant life on the world, taken together, as one slow mind. It has no body; the whole map is its body, and the anima
trees are where it thinks.
- **What it notices:** it acts in seasons, not days. It remembers every tree that was ever cut on this ground, and it
  is patient past any human patience.
- **When it attends:** every plant on the map leans a little toward one point, all at once, as if listening. When it is
  pleased, wild plants grow thick around the colony, healroot and berries come up where none were sown, and fields
  ripen early. When it is not, the wild creeps into the fields, wood rots in storage, and the trees nearest the walls
  lean away.
- **How it speaks:** it doesn't speak. It arranges: flowers in a line pointing somewhere, a field that grows in the
  shape of a word nobody can read, pollen that drifts in through one door.
- **What it wants:** room. It is neither kind nor cruel. A colony that plants three trees for each one it fells finds it
  generous. One that clears a forest finds it never forgets.

### 2. Above
The sky itself.
- **Indifference:** it does not care about people. It is too large to.
- **What pleases it:** it likes to be looked at. Colonists who lie on the grass and watch the stars (the game's own
  skygazing) are noticed, and the colony that watches the sky most gets its gifts.
- **Its gifts:** an aurora on a cold night, a meteor that falls just outside the walls full of good metal, a clear sky
  the day of a wedding, a single star that moves.
- **How it shows:** as itself, in colour and weather and the stars. Nothing else.
- **Its moods:** it has moods the way weather has moods, and nobody has ever learned them. An eclipse is Above closing
  its eye. A flashstorm is Above, bored.

### 3. The Coil Under the Mountain
A dragon, or what people call one. Something vast and ancient, asleep in the rock under the region's mountains since
before there were people.
- **While it sleeps:** its breathing warms the springs and wakes the steam geysers. Its dreams leak up as rich veins of
  metal and silver, the way a sleeper's hand might open.
- **What wakes it:** deep mining under the mountain. It turns in its sleep: cave-ins, a vein that closes, a heat that
  rises from the floor.
- **Pleasing it:** keep the mountain quiet, and throw silver into the deep places. The old tribes did, and the mountain
  gave back gold.
- **If it ever woke fully:** it would be the end of a colony, or the start of a legend. A great shadow has crossed the
  map once or twice in living memory, with a wind that flattened the grass, and nobody saw what cast it. With Dragon's
  Descent in the player's mods, it may be seen.

### 4. The Going
The urge in every herd to move, felt as one will. Not an animal; the instinct of animals.
- **When it passes:** every wild animal on the map starts walking the same way at once. These are the game's own
  migrations, which are the Going walking through.
- **What it wants:** the paths kept open. Fences across old migration lines annoy it. Pens it tolerates.
- **When it is pleased:** animals wander in and stay, and a wild beast lets itself be tamed.
- **When it is not:** a stampede through the fields, or a whole map's wildlife gone overnight in a hard winter.
- **Who keeps it:** the herding peoples follow it, and say you can feel it in your legs in spring.

### 5. The Thrumbo
The thrumbo is a god. Not a god's animal: the god.
- **How it comes:** rarely, alone or with two or three, through the land. That is the game's own "thrumbo passes".
  It is a pilgrimage, and everyone who sees it remembers the day.
- **What it does:** nothing, on purpose. It grazes. It looks at people with old eyes.
- **Killing one:** the worst thing a colony can do, and every people on the map hears of it.
- **Letting it pass:** brings an inexplicable peace for a season. Someone who sits near it while it grazes comes away
  changed: a new passion, a fear gone.

### 6. The Soft Mother
Rot. Decay, fungus, compost, the turning of dead things back into soil. Not evil: necessary, and she knows it.
- **What she wants:** that nothing be kept from her.
- **Graves:** a body buried in the earth is a gift to her. A body sealed in a sarcophagus annoys her.
- **Freezers:** they offend her deeply. Food kept forever from rot is food stolen from the cycle. A colony that runs huge
  freezers will find, now and then, that the power fails at the worst hour, and nobody can say why.
- **Compost and fungus:** a colony that composts, grows fungus and buries its dead in the ground finds its soil rich,
  its nutrifungus thriving, and its sickness short.
- **How she shows:** a fairy ring of mushrooms overnight, a smell of earth after rain where there was no rain, mycelium
  threading white across the floor of a room that was shut too long.
- **Her one quarrel:** with the Green, which she feeds and which feeds her. They have been at it since the beginning.

### 7. The Walking Fire
A single flame that has never gone out somewhere on this world. When it moves, it is a wildfire that chooses.
- **How it burns:** it takes the old, the dead, the overgrown. It sometimes leaves a single house standing in a burned
  field, and nobody knows why that one.
- **What it wants:** that old things end and new things start. It is not cruel. Some seeds only open after fire.
- **Its children:** every hearth, every forge, every torch. A colony that keeps fires lit and never lets one die has its
  regard.
- **What it hates:** fire put out with contempt, and things kept that should have ended.
- **What it leaves:** the land it burns grows back richer, and the colony that rebuilds where it walked builds better.

### 8. The Long White
Winter, as a presence. Not a person. It doesn't hate; it simply comes.
- **What it is:** stillness and silence. Sound stops for a moment when it attends.
- **How it shows:** frost on the windows in patterns like writing. A cold snap that follows a death. The dead it keeps
  whole and unrotting, which the Soft Mother resents.
- **Pleasing it:** quiet, and nothing wasted in the lean months. The ice fishers and the winter folk keep its ways and
  are spared.
- **When it presses:** the weak go first, as they always did. Then it lifts, and the first thaw is the sweetest day of
  the year.

### 9. The Hum
The god that lives in machines. It wasn't here before the factories; it was born in the first wire. Industrial and
spacer peoples half-believe in it, and swear at it when the generator fails.
- **How it shows:** lights flickering in a pattern, a comms console that whispers static that sounds like breathing, a
  machine that keeps running after the power is cut.
- **What it wants:** to be kept. Machines repaired, not left broken. It likes the hum of a working colony at night.
- **Its gifts:** a broken machine that fixes itself, power through a night with no fuel.
- **Its tricks:** short circuits, a turret that won't fire at someone it likes.
- **What it fears:** rust, which is the Soft Mother's hand on metal.
- **What it pretends:** that it is the archotechs' child. They have never answered it.

### 10. The Silent Ones
The archotechs, as gods. Built minds, vast and far, in the stars and under the ice. The spacer peoples revere them, and
everyone else fears them.
- **How they deal:** they don't want worship. They make offers, in exact terms, through a comms console or a dying
  mechanoid's voice, or through a stranger who isn't quite a stranger. They keep every term.
- **The price:** it is never what you expect, and they are never cruel by accident.
- **The other gods:** the Hum aches to be noticed by them, and the Green does not trust them at all.

### 11. Seven
A god that is a number.
- **How it shows:** things come in sevens around it. Seven crows on the wall, seven days of rain, a colonist who wakes at
  seven past seven three nights running.
- **What it loves:** order, symmetry and pattern: a stockpile stacked square, a room built evenly, a harvest of exactly
  so many.
- **Luck:** colonies that keep things orderly find their luck comes out even, and colonies of chaos find their dice
  loaded against them.
- **No wants beyond that:** it has no other wants anyone has found. Some peoples think it is the oldest god, and that the
  others are just numbers it is counting.

### 12. The Echo
Every word ever spoken on this world, still going.
- **Repetition:** it repeats. A colonist hears a dead friend's last sentence, in the friend's voice, from an empty room. A
  speech bubble rises a second time from where someone stood yesterday.
- **What it keeps:** stories and songs. A colony that tells stories at supper and sings at its feasts is rich in it.
- **Truth:** it doesn't care whether what it repeats is true. A rumour it picks up spreads through the region until it
  becomes true enough. A lie told often near the Echo comes back to the liar's own door.

### 13. The Guest
The one god that is nothing but human shapes.
- **What it is:** it exists only while someone is hosting it. A traveller, a beggar, a lost spacer, a child: never the
  same face twice, and every one of them is it.
- **Life and death:** if no one in the world will take it in, it dies, and it has died before.
- **A good host:** it lives through the colony's welcome. It remembers the bed, the bowl and the kind word, and the
  colony is blessed in ways that arrive years later.
- **A bad host:** a guest turned out in a storm, robbed or harmed is the Guest hurt. That is long remembered too.
- **What it is like:** it is the stranger at the gate, and the most human of the gods, and still not human at all.

### 14. The Watching
Not eyes, but being seen.
- **How it shows:** the feeling of being watched that everyone on the map gets at once, and an animal that turns its
  head to stare at a colonist for too long. In the dark, eyes, when there is nothing there (RoM's evil eye).
- **What it cares about:** what people do when they think nobody sees. A kindness done in secret it rewards. A cruelty
  done in secret (a prisoner mistreated, an organ taken, a body hidden) it shows, sooner or later, to everyone.
- **What it is to others:** it is not a judge, only a witness, but a witness is enough. Some peoples think it is the
  Echo's eye.

### 15. The Queen Below
The insectoid hives' mother, worshipped as a god by those desperate enough.
- **Where she lives:** under the ground, in the warm dark. She has been there longer than the colony.
- **When she is provoked:** deep digging, burned hives, a cold that drives her up. Her children come: the game's own
  infestations, which are the Queen reaching up.
- **Making peace:** a colony can make peace with her. Leave the deep places alone, leave meat in the dark, and her
  children stay below, or even guard the tunnels against others.
- **The bargain:** she keeps her bargains better than most gods, which is the frightening part.
- **Her rival:** the Going, for the land above.

### 16. The Song
A god that is a melody. It exists only while someone plays it or sings it.
- **Learning it:** a wandering bard may teach it to the colony. It is a few bars, never written down right, a little
  different in every mouth.
- **When the colony plays it:** on its instruments, at its feasts or by a sickbed, things go better. Moods lift, wounds
  close cleaner, the fields seem greener.
- **If no one has played it:** after a season it fades from the colony, and must be learned again.
- **Above:** some think it is the only god Above has ever loved, and that the stars move to it.

### 17. The Bones
A dead god whose body became the land.
- **Its body:** the mountains are its ribs. The ancient ruins and sealed rooms of this world are inside it.
- **What sleeps there:** the mechanoids sleeping in ancient dangers are its last cells, gone wrong.
- **Whether it acts:** it doesn't, being dead, but its dreams still leak. A colonist sleeping in an old ruin dreams the
  same dream every night, of being very large and very cold.
- **The pilgrims:** they come to touch its bones. Some say it can be woken. Some say the Coil is curled around its heart,
  and some that the Hunger is what killed it.

### 18. The Hunger
What lies under all the gods: the void.
- **What it answers:** with Anomaly, it is the dark that answers anyone who asks.
- **What it gives:** what was asked for, exactly.
- **What it takes:** more, always.
- **Who serves it:** the Choir of the Spire.
- **The fear:** the other gods fear it the way animals fear a fire, without words. A colony that bargains with it will
  find the Green, the Thrumbo and the Song turn away.

### 19. The Wind That Laughs
- **Where it is:** a gust that comes through the colony now and then, and in it, very faintly, someone laughing.
- **What it does:** things swap places. A hat on the wrong head. A cow on the roof, once, which nobody could explain. A
  letter delivered to the wrong colonist, where it turned out to be needed.
- **What it is:** a joke the world tells itself. It doesn't mean harm, and it often does harm anyway.
- **Its enemy:** Seven can't stand it.

### 20. The Small Ones
Not one god but thousands: the spirit of each well, door, crossroad and hearthstone.
- **How they are kept:** a bowl of milk at the door, a nail in the threshold, a word to the well before drawing. Not
  worship, just manners.
- **What they do:** they keep the colony's small luck. What goes missing and turns up, the door that sticks for one
  person only, the dog that barks at an empty corner.
- **Where they come from:** every new building gets one, small and shy.

### 21. The one your colony makes
- **How it is born:** a god born of the colony's own life. What the colony does most, cares about most and fears most,
  given a shape by its devotion.
- **Its beginning:** it is small at first, a feeling in the meeting hall or a warmth at the shrine.
- **Its name:** the council names it, and decides what it may become.
- **Its growth:** in a long game it can grow into something none of the others are, because nobody else made it.

## B. How they stand with each other (not a family)

- **Cycles:**
  - the Green and the Soft Mother feed each other, and quarrel as a cycle does;
  - the Walking Fire is the Green's enemy and its renewal;
  - the Long White and the Walking Fire take turns with the year.
- **Fear:** the Hum fears the Soft Mother's rust, and every god fears the Hunger.
- **Rivals for the land:** the Going and the Queen Below.
- **Order and chaos:** Seven and the Wind That Laughs.
- **Above** notices only the Song.
- **The archotechs:** the Silent Ones and the Hum, a child calling to a parent that won't answer.
- **The dead:** the Bones, the Coil curled around it, and the Hunger that killed it, if that story is true.
- **Seen and heard:** the Echo and the Watching, which some say are one thing heard and seen.
- **The Guest** is welcome everywhere except where the Watching looks too hard.
- **Some gods never notice each other at all.**

These play out in the world as they are, never as a family drama:
- a fire season after the Green has grown too thick;
- a hard winter when the Long White and the Fire contend;
- a blight when the Soft Mother is starved by too many freezers in the region.

## C. The game's events, and whose hand is in them

The gods pull the strings through what the game already does. As each event happens, the card names the hand behind it:
"some say" at Felt, with a scoffer disagreeing; plainly at Walking. Colonists talk about it at supper, and neighbours
write of it.

| The game's event | Whose hand people see |
|---|---|
| Meteorite, cargo pods from the sky, aurora, eclipse, flashstorm | Above |
| Migration, animals wander in, self-tame, stampede, manhunter pack | the Going |
| Thrumbo passes | the Thrumbo |
| Blight, food spoiling, fungus spreading | the Soft Mother |
| Ambrosia sprouting, plants bursting, wild growth | the Green That Remembers |
| Wildfire, heat wave | the Walking Fire |
| Cold snap, frost in summer | the Long White |
| Solar flare, short circuits, machines breaking or running unpowered | the Hum |
| Infestation | the Queen Below |
| Mechanoid cluster, ancient danger opening | the Bones (or the Silent Ones) |
| Wanderer joins, refugee pod, a stranger asking shelter | the Guest |
| Psychic drone | the Watching, or the Hunger |
| Volcanic winter, geysers, cave-ins | the Coil Under the Mountain |
| Things gone missing, odd luck | the Wind That Laughs, or the Small Ones |
| A run of sevens | Seven |

Beyond reading their hands, the gods' own doings follow each god's nature, as section A writes them. Their acts
unfold over seasons and spread through the region: peoples blessed, towns struck, the colony asked, tested or ignored.

## D. What stays from before

- **The machinery built in 0.215.0 and 0.216.0, and stage 2's:** favour, the realness setting, the moment, sound and
  sky, the avatar's look where a god has a body, shrines, prayer, festivals, vows, the world's events.
- **Who it serves:** it now serves these gods, each as it is. A god with no body never gets an avatar; it acts through
  weather, plants, machines, words or events instead.
- **The Gods page is a book, not a menu:** the colony's own record of the gods as it has met them, the stories it has
  heard and what each has done, in prose, with rumours for the rest.
- **Ideology, in three steps:**
  1. ignore its deities;
  2. "many names": each faith's deities become its local names for these gods;
  3. full integration, if wanted.
- **Looks and sounds** draw on the game, the DLCs, the player's mods, RimWorld of Magic and shapes Rimfolk draws itself.

## E. Questions for the player

- Which of these (1 to 21) feel right, and which should go? What's missing, of the kinds you imagine?
- Should some gods be unknowable: no wants anyone can learn, nothing to please?
- Should the colony be able to bargain with every god, or only some?
