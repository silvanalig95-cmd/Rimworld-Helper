# Rimfolk's own pantheon: the brainstorm, redone (11 November 2026)

The player, after 0.215.0 and 0.216.0: "honestly the existing rimworld gods are just randomly generated it feels and they
have little to no character. instead of building up on this system, i'd rather we start creating our own pantheon, where
each god feels hand-crafted and unique, we build this up from now, and either fully ignore the rimworld ideology gods or
eventually try to integrate our gods into the rimworld ideology system. let's redo the brainstorm under these conditions."

Nothing here is built. What carries over from what is built:
- **Kept:** the machinery is god-agnostic, so it all keeps working: favour, the realness setting, the Gods page, the
  avatar's look, the moment, sound and sky, and stage 2's stranger, sacred animal, worship and world.
- **Replaced:** the generated pantheon (gods made from Ideology's random deities and Rimfolk's name makers) becomes the
  hand-made roster below, the same in every game. Each god is a def with its whole character in it.

## A. Where the gods come from (the frame, in RimWorld's own lore)

1. **The gods are minds of the planet's psychic field.** RimWorld already has the field: the anima trees' psychic web,
   psychic drones and soothes, psylinks, and the archotechs who reach into it.
   - The gods woke in that field when life first sang on this world, and they grow with attention: prayer, fear, stories.
   - This is why they are real but subtle, why the psychically sensitive feel them first, and why a psychic drone is
     sometimes two gods quarrelling.
   - Beneath the field is the void, which Anomaly made real.
2. **Every age sees the same gods differently**:
   - tribes: spirits and ancestors;
   - medieval peoples: gods, with temples and saints;
   - industrial peoples: "psychic phenomena", or a half-forgotten faith of grandmothers;
   - spacers: "local superminds", bargained with like any other power.

   The gods don't change; the words do. Rimfolk's text already speaks by age.
3. **The archotechs are not gods, and the gods know it.** The archotechs are built minds that came from the stars. The
   gods call them the Silent Ones and distrust them; some peoples worship them anyway. Their bargains are exact and their
   price is strange.
4. **One pantheon, many names.** The same thirteen gods are worshipped across the world, by different names in different
   places. "The Ashen Banner call Varrak the Thunder-Orm." Ideology's deities can become these local names (section E).

5. **Every god is shaped its own way** (the player: "not every god necessarily has to have a sacred beast, each god
   should be individual in what it looks like and whether it has any form at all"; "gods should cover all kinds of
   designs, concepts, ideas, forms or no form"). No two gods show themselves alike, and no god must have a body, a
   beast or a human face. The shapes a god can take:
   - **A body:** a person, a giant, a beast, or a made thing that moves, such as a scarecrow, a clay golem or a stone
     golem (A RimWorld of Magic's golems).
   - **Many bodies:** a crowd, a warband, a flock.
   - **An object:** a mask, a coin, a lantern that passes from hand to hand.
   - **A place:** a grove, a spring, a field, a vault.
   - **The weather or the sky:** a storm, a beam of light, an eclipse.
   - **A sense:** a sound with no singer, a smell, a taste, a warmth, a sudden silence or cold.
   - **A dream only**, or a voice through a sleeping colonist.
   - **A pattern:** coincidences, luck, the same number three times.
   - **Words:** lines in a letter nobody wrote, a sum in the ledger, a name in the history.
   - **An absence:** the dark, the void, what is missing.
   - **Bones and ruins:** what is left of a dead god.

   A god may have several of these, or only one.

## B. The thirteen

Every god has the same fields, so each feels whole on the map, in the text and in play:
- epithets;
- domains;
- forms: a human form, a beast form, and a stranger's disguise;
- a voice, and a temperament;
- what it loves and what it hates;
- sacred things: an animal (from the player's game), a plant, a place and a day;
- ties to the other gods, and its myths;
- gifts, wrath and omens;
- how each age knows it;
- its colours, its symbol and its sound.

### 1. Auren, the Hearthmother
- **Epithets:** Keeper of the Fire, She Who Counts the Guests.
- **Domain:** hearth, home, hospitality, guests, the shared meal.
- **Shape:** a body, and only ever one. She is a broad old woman with ember-bright eyes and a shawl that smokes at
  the hem, and she always comes walking, as the stranger at the gate (her oldest story): a beggar woman asking for a
  bowl and a corner. She never comes as anything else. When she is pleased, every hearth in the colony burns a little
  brighter for a night.
- **Voice:** warm, blunt, remembers everything. "You fed me. I don't forget a bowl."
- **Temperament:** patient, motherly, and terrible when a guest is wronged.
- **Loves:**
  - guests well treated, a bed given, food shared;
  - a fire kept lit through the night;
  - Hospitality's guests leaving happy.
- **Hates:**
  - a guest harmed, robbed or turned out in a storm;
  - a cold hearth in winter;
  - eating alone while others go hungry.
- **Sacred:** bread; the hearth; the longest night.
- **Ties:**
  - wife of Varrak; she knows of his lovers and keeps a cold silence;
  - sister of Sella;
  - enemy of Ryke, whose wars burn homes;
  - she dislikes Velle, Varrak's son by another, but feeds him all the same.
- **Myth:** *The Bowl at the Gate.* The first colonists turned away an old woman in the snow. The next night their fires
  would not light. The one who had given her their own blanket found a coal glowing in their pocket, and from it every
  fire in the camp was lit again.
- **Gifts:**
  - a blessed hearth: the room's warmth and mood;
  - guests who stay and join;
  - the cook's touch: a passion in Cooking;
  - RoM: Priest.
- **Wrath:** fires that won't catch, a house fire that turns inward (at the game's rate), and guests who never come.
- **Omens:** a coal that glows by itself, a smell of bread where none is baking.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: Grandmother Fire;
  - medieval: the Hearthmother, with hearth-saints;
  - industrial: the "kitchen saint" of old households;
  - spacers: an "indexed psychic attractor associated with domestic settings".
- **Colour:** ember orange and ash grey. **Symbol:** a flame in an open hand.
- **Sound:** a crackling hearth, a low hum, a warm chant.

### 2. Varrak, the Stormfather
- **Epithets:** Lord of the Sky, Breaker of Oaths and Keeper of Them.
- **Domain:** storm, sky, lightning, oaths, rule.
- **Shape:** no body. He is the storm.
  - He shows only in the sky: a thunderhead that stands still over the colony, lightning that strikes the same rod
    three times, a voice in the thunder that every colonist hears as words.
  - In his greatest moments a shape of a crowned giant is half-seen in the clouds, and a beam of white light falls on
    the one he names (RoM's power beam).
- **Voice:** loud, grand and short. "Swear it. Then keep it."
- **Temperament:** proud, generous when pleased, unfaithful, and quick to strike.
- **Loves:**
  - oaths made and kept, and promises (Rimfolk's promises kept);
  - boldness in battle;
  - tall towers, lightning rods, and high places.
- **Hates:**
  - broken oaths;
  - cowards;
  - anyone who laughs at thunder.
- **Sacred:** the oak; the hilltop; the lightning rod; the first storm of spring.
- **Ties:**
  - husband of Auren;
  - former lover of Aal, and father by her of Velle;
  - rival of Sella over the rain: he withholds it to spite her old refusal;
  - ally of Ryke;
  - son of the Sleeper, whom he chained.
- **Myths:**
  - *The Chaining of Orm*: Varrak and Oskarn bound their father Orm, who would have eaten the world, and sealed him in the
    deep vault.
  - *The Dry Year*: Varrak kept the rain from Sella's fields for a whole year, until Velle stole his rain-skin and spilled
    it.
- **Gifts:**
  - storm-sense;
  - lightning that strikes your enemies in a raid (rare, shown);
  - RoM: Storm Born;
  - VPE: the Staticlord path.
- **Wrath:** lightning on an oathbreaker's roof, hail on the fields, a dry storm.
- **Omens:** thunder without cloud, hair standing up, a rod that hums.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: the Sky-Father;
  - medieval: Varrak the King;
  - industrial: "the storm saint" sailors pray to;
  - spacers: an "atmospheric psychic phenomenon".
- **Colour:** storm grey and lightning white. **Symbol:** a lightning bolt through a ring.
- **Sound:** thunder, Royalty's bestowing, the war drum.

### 3. Sella, the Furrow-Mother
- **Epithets:** Mother of the Sheaf, the Grieving Field.
- **Domain:** harvest, the seasons, rain on fields, growing food.
- **Shape:** a made thing. She walks as a scarecrow of straw and rags (RoM's straw golem) that nobody put up,
  standing in the colony's field one morning and in another field the next. In spring the scarecrow is green with
  sprouting grain; in winter it is black and bowed, and it weeps.
- **Voice:** soft, slow, and sorrowful in winter. "Is it time? Has he come home?"
- **Temperament:** patient, generous, and inconsolable in grief.
- **Loves:**
  - the first sheaf offered;
  - fields tended to the last row;
  - nothing wasted, and food shared in a hungry year.
- **Hates:**
  - food left to rot;
  - fields burned;
  - Varrak's pride.
- **Sacred:** wheat and rice; the scarecrow; the threshing floor; the last harvest day.
- **Ties:**
  - sister of Auren;
  - mother of Lir, the green child, whom Oskarn holds in the land of the dead;
  - rival of Varrak;
  - ally of Brannoch and Aal.
- **Myth:** *Why Winter Comes.* Lir drowned in the first winter, and Oskarn took him across. Sella grieved, and nothing
  grew. Oskarn, who is fair, lets Lir walk the fields for half the year. When the boy goes back across the river, his
  mother mourns, and the world goes cold.
- **Gifts:**
  - a growing season that comes early or ends late;
  - a blight that passes your fields;
  - the gardener's hand: a passion in Plants;
  - RoM: Druid.
- **Wrath:** blight, fields that won't sprout, food spoiling early.
- **Omens:** a single green shoot in snow, a scarecrow facing the wrong way.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: Field-Mother;
  - medieval: Sella of the Sheaf, with harvest festivals;
  - industrial: harvest-home customs;
  - spacers: "agricultural psychic resonance".
- **Colour:** wheat gold and loam brown. **Symbol:** a sheaf.
- **Sound:** wind in grain, a slow chant.

### 4. Oskarn, the Ferryman
- **Epithets:** Lantern-Bearer, Keeper of Names.
- **Domain:** death, the passage, graves, memory.
- **Shape:** a body, but seen only by some. A very tall hooded figure with a lantern and no face, where the face
  should be, a small cold light. Only the dying, the grieving and the psychically gifted see him. Everyone else sees a
  lantern moving on its own, or nothing at all, while the grieving one talks to the empty air.
- **Voice:** quiet and exact. Every name he says, he says fully. "I keep them. All of them."
- **Temperament:** patient, fair, unbribable, gentle with the grieving.
- **Loves:**
  - proper burials and eulogies;
  - the dead's names spoken at supper;
  - graves kept.
- **Hates:**
  - the dead left lying;
  - graves robbed;
  - resurrection without his leave, which is Velle's trick.
- **Sacred:** the lantern; the graveyard; the dead's day.
- **Ties:**
  - twin brother of Ithra, with whom he quarrels over every soul;
  - he holds Sella's son;
  - ally of Mirr;
  - with Varrak, he chained Orm;
  - Velle cheats him and he never forgets it.
- **Myth:** *The Ferry and the Coin.* Velle once crossed the river alive, pretending to be dead, and stole back a
  fisherman's daughter. Oskarn let her live and took Velle's laughter instead. Velle has smiled but never laughed since.
- **Gifts:**
  - a peaceful death with no bad memories for the mourners;
  - a last word from a dead colonist in a dream;
  - RoM: Necromancer or Death Knight.
- **Wrath:** the dead walk if left unburied (with Anomaly, the game's own shamblers, rare), and dreams of the dead.
- **Omens:** a lantern seen far off at night where no one is, a candle by a sickbed going blue.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: the Boatman;
  - medieval: Oskarn the Ferryman;
  - industrial: the undertaker's saint;
  - spacers: a "post-mortem psychic echo".
- **Colour:** lantern amber and night black. **Symbol:** a lantern over water.
- **Sound:** a gong, a bell tolling, Anomaly's "Death Pall Rising".

### 5. Kaeth, the Huntress
- **Epithets:** Long-Shot, the White Hart's Shadow.
- **Domain:** the hunt, the chase, the clean kill, archery.
- **Shape:** never seen herself, only her hunt. A white stag running across the map at dusk (the Elves'
  ancient deer, else an elk), the baying of hounds where there are no hounds, an arrow found humming in a tree, a kill
  left clean at the edge of the map with not a mark but one. Those who chase the stag find the trail ends at a cliff.
- **Voice:** few words, all of them sharp. "Clean, or not at all."
- **Temperament:** cold, competitive, honourable.
- **Loves:**
  - a clean kill and the whole beast used;
  - legend beasts brought down fairly;
  - a hunter's first kill.
- **Hates:**
  - waste and cruelty to prey;
  - traps that maim;
  - hunting a mother with young.
- **Sacred:** the white stag; the yew; the high forest; the hunter's moon.
- **Ties:**
  - rival of Brannoch, over the beasts;
  - lover of Ryke, a fierce and quarrelling pair;
  - ally of Ylda.
- **Myth:** *The Hart That Was Never Caught.* Kaeth swore to Brannoch she would take the white stag. She has chased it
  for a thousand years. Some say she could have caught it any day, and doesn't, because then the chase would end.
- **Gifts:**
  - a hunter's eye: Shooting and Animals passions;
  - a beast's revenge that never comes;
  - RoM: Ranger.
- **Wrath:** beasts turn on the wasteful hunter, and game leaves the map.
- **Omens:** a white stag seen at the edge of the map, a perfect arrow found in a tree.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: the Hunt-Mother;
  - medieval: Kaeth of the Chase;
  - industrial: the hunters' luck;
  - spacers: a "predation-linked psychic signature".
- **Colour:** birch white and pine green. **Symbol:** antlers over an arrow.
- **Sound:** a horn far off, Anomaly's "A Twisted Path".

### 6. Brannoch, the Herdmother
- **Epithets:** the Great She-Bear, Mother of Every Calf.
- **Domain:** beasts, herds, taming, the birth of animals.
- **Shape:** a beast, and that is all she is. A colossal she-bear, mossed and ancient, larger than any beast
  on the map (in her wrath Alpha Animals' behemoth), who walks slowly through the pens. Every animal in the colony
  follows her for an hour, and the ones she noses give birth easily that year.
- **Voice:** deep and slow. "Mine. All of them are mine."
- **Temperament:** gentle, patient, and fierce as a bear for her young.
- **Loves:**
  - animals well kept, well fed and well sheltered;
  - births in the barn;
  - bonds between colonist and beast.
- **Hates:**
  - cruelty to animals;
  - starving herds;
  - slaughter of the young.
- **Sacred:** the bear; clover; the pen and the barn; the first calving.
- **Ties:**
  - rival of Kaeth;
  - ally of Sella;
  - foster-mother of Velle, whom she nursed when Auren would not.
- **Myth:** *The Bear Who Nursed a God.* When Aal left the child Velle on the riverbank, Brannoch found him and nursed him
  among her cubs. That is why foxes are clever and bears are kind to them.
- **Gifts:**
  - easy births in the herd;
  - an animal bonded to one colonist;
  - tame beasts that defend the colony;
  - RoM: Shaman.
- **Wrath:** herds sicken, a manhunter pack, the colony's animals flee.
- **Omens:** animals lying down facing one way, a bear track by the barn.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: Bear-Mother;
  - medieval: the Herdmother;
  - industrial: the drovers' saint;
  - spacers: a "fauna-linked psychic node".
- **Colour:** moss green and bear brown. **Symbol:** a paw with a calf's head.
- **Sound:** a lowing, Alpha Memes' Neolithic chant.

### 7. Ylda, the Old Wood
- **Epithets:** the Green, Mother of the Anima.
- **Domain:** wild growth, forests, the anima trees, the slow.
- **Shape:** a place. She is the wood itself, and the anima trees are her. She never walks. She shows as a grove that
  grows overnight, as roots bursting through a floor (RoM's living-wall spikes), as a tree that wasn't there yesterday
  and hums, and as her dryads, who speak for her.
- **Voice:** slow, in long pauses, as if each word grew. "...You. Cut. ...Why."
- **Temperament:** patient past understanding, and implacable once moved.
- **Loves:**
  - trees planted;
  - the anima tree tended;
  - wild land left wild.
- **Hates:**
  - the axe and charcoal;
  - forests burned;
  - Hadrun's furnaces.
- **Sacred:** the anima tree and every great tree; the grove; the first leaf of spring.
- **Ties:**
  - enemy of Hadrun;
  - ally of Kaeth;
  - the oldest of the gods, older than Varrak, though younger than Orm.
- **Myth:** *The First Tree.* When the field was young, Ylda planted one seed and sang to it for a hundred years. It grew
  into the first anima tree, and every anima tree since is a sapling of her song. That is why they hum.
- **Gifts:**
  - a Gauranlen or anima tree;
  - forests regrowing;
  - dryads;
  - RoM: Druid.
- **Wrath:** roots that crack floors, a wild growth that eats a field, wood that rots in storage.
- **Omens:** a tree that wasn't there, the anima tree's hum growing loud.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: the Green Mother;
  - medieval: the Old Wood;
  - industrial: a "spirit of the forest";
  - spacers: the "planetary anima network".
- **Colour:** leaf green and bark grey. **Symbol:** a tree with roots like hands.
- **Sound:** leaves rustling, the dryads' song.

### 8. Hadrun, the Smith
- **Epithets:** the Lame Forge-Lord, Maker.
- **Domain:** craft, metal, the forge, invention.
- **Shape:** a body, and his works. A broad soot-black smith with molten hands and an iron leg, who comes to work
  at the colony's smithy at night and is gone by morning, the work done and better than anyone there could do it. When
  he cannot come himself he sends a clay servant he made (RoM's Maharal golem) that works without a word.
- **Voice:** gruff, proud, short with fools. "Look at this joint. Look. That is how it's done."
- **Temperament:** proud, jealous, honest, generous to good apprentices.
- **Loves:**
  - masterwork and legendary items;
  - a forge always lit;
  - a good apprentice;
  - honest trade in made things.
- **Hates:**
  - shoddy work;
  - the Silent Ones' machines, which make without hands;
  - Velle, who stole his spark.
- **Sacred:** the anvil; iron; the smithy; the day the forge is first lit.
- **Ties:**
  - he loves Mirr, who doesn't love him back;
  - enemy of Ylda;
  - rival of the Silent Ones;
  - Velle stole from him.
- **Myth:** *The Stolen Spark.* Velle stole a spark from Hadrun's forge and gave it to the first people, who had only
  cold hands. Hadrun chased him across the sky and fell, and his leg broke. He made himself a new one of iron, better
  than the old.
- **Gifts:**
  - a masterwork in a colonist's hands;
  - a smith's inspiration;
  - a relic weapon (Vanilla Persona Weapons Expanded);
  - RoM: Enchanter or Technomancer.
- **Wrath:** tools break, a fire in the workshop, metal won't take an edge.
- **Omens:** sparks with no fire, an anvil ringing at night.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: Fire-Shaper;
  - medieval: Hadrun the Smith;
  - industrial: the patron of foundries and guilds;
  - spacers: "the manufacturing saint", a joke that isn't one.
- **Colour:** molten orange and soot black. **Symbol:** a hammer and a spark.
- **Sound:** an anvil, the forge's roar, ItemEnchanted.

### 9. Velle, the Wanderer
- **Epithets:** the Fox, Bringer of Spark, the Unlaughing.
- **Domain:** roads, trade, travellers, luck, messengers, thieves, tricks.
- **Shape:** no shape of his own. He is luck and coincidence. A different stranger every time, never the same face
  twice: a pedlar, a child, a caravan guard. A fox at the edge of sight. Most often nothing at all:
  - things that move overnight, a coin where none was dropped;
  - a deal that is too good;
  - a P.S. in a letter that nobody wrote (RoM's deception mote over the one he fooled).
- **Voice:** quick, charming, never quite true. "Trust me. No, really. This time."
- **Temperament:** playful, capricious, kind to travellers, ruinous to the proud.
- **Loves:**
  - trade, and a clever bargain on either side;
  - travellers helped;
  - letters carried (the post);
  - a good joke.
- **Hates:**
  - closed gates;
  - misers;
  - Oskarn's fairness, which he finds boring.
- **Sacred:** the fox; the crossroads stone; the road; the market day.
- **Ties:**
  - son of Varrak and Aal;
  - nursed by Brannoch, disliked by Auren;
  - stole from Hadrun, cheated Oskarn, and is friends with nobody and everybody.
- **Myth:** *How the Wanderer Paid the Ferryman.* See Oskarn's myth. He has never laughed since. Some say his smile is
  the more dangerous for it.
- **Gifts:**
  - a lucky trade;
  - a caravan that comes when needed;
  - a lost thing found;
  - a dead colonist returned, once, at a terrible price and against Oskarn's will;
  - RoM: Faceless.
- **Wrath, all of it pranks:** goods vanish from a stockpile, caravans take the wrong road, a blessing turns out to be a
  curse, or the other way round.
- **Omens:** a fox at the crossroads, a coin found where none was dropped.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: Coyote-Brother;
  - medieval: Velle the Wanderer;
  - industrial: the traveller's luck;
  - spacers: an "improbability cluster".
- **Colour:** fox red and road dust. **Symbol:** a road with a star.
- **Sound:** a skip, a laugh cut short, Odyssey's "Baubles".

### 10. Mirr, the Veiled Moon
- **Epithets:** Lady of Dreams, Keeper of Secrets, Who Watches the Pit.
- **Domain:** night, dreams, secrets, the psychic, madness.
- **Shape:** a dream. She is never on the map.
  - She speaks through a sleeping colonist, who sits up and talks in a voice not their own. The words are true.
  - She walks in Rimfolk's dreams, a pale woman with silver eyes and a veil.
  - The only sign by day is moths, too many of them, gathered at one window.
- **Voice:** soft, riddling, sad. "You dreamed of me. You will again."
- **Temperament:** gentle, secretive, grieving, dangerous when betrayed.
- **Loves:**
  - dreams told at breakfast;
  - secrets kept;
  - the psychically gifted;
  - lights at night for the lost.
- **Hates:**
  - the Hunger, which she watches;
  - secrets betrayed;
  - Hadrun's clumsy love.
- **Sacred:** moths; moonflowers; the dark room; the moonless night.
- **Ties:**
  - sister of Calla, the dead Morning, whom she still mourns;
  - ally of Oskarn;
  - loved by Hadrun;
  - she guards the dreams against the Hunger.
- **Myth:** *The Night Calla Died.* When the Hunger rose in the War of the First Winter, Calla the Morning held the gate
  alone so Mirr could close it. Mirr closed it with her sister still outside. She has never forgiven herself, and she
  watches the gate every night.
- **Gifts:**
  - true dreams: warnings of raids;
  - a psylink or a psychic sensitivity;
  - RoM: Bard or Psionic;
  - VPE: the Frostshaper or Necropath paths.
- **Wrath:** nightmares, a colonist driven briefly mad (the game's own breaks), secrets told aloud.
- **Omens:** the same dream in two colonists, moths gathering at a window.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: the Night-Sister;
  - medieval: Mirr of the Veil;
  - industrial: "a sleepwalker's angel";
  - spacers: the "dream-band psychic phenomenon".
- **Colour:** moon silver and deep indigo. **Symbol:** a crescent over an eye.
- **Sound:** a soothing pulse, Odyssey's "Floating Points".

### 11. Ryke, the Red Captain
- **Epithets:** the Scarred, First into the Breach.
- **Domain:** war, courage, glory, the warband.
- **Shape:** many bodies. He is not one but the warband: a host of red-cloaked phantoms that rise beside the colony's
  fighters in a great battle and fight with them (RoM's spirit wolves turned red, the bloodwolves), then are gone. Some
  say one of them laughs.
- **Voice:** a laugh before every word. "Good. GOOD. Again!"
- **Temperament:** wrathful, joyful in battle, honourable to a fallen enemy.
- **Loves:**
  - courage;
  - a raid beaten off;
  - a duel fairly fought;
  - honoured enemy dead.
- **Hates:**
  - cowardice;
  - the wounded left behind;
  - Auren's scolding.
- **Sacred:** the spear; the battlefield; the day of a great victory.
- **Ties:**
  - ally of Varrak;
  - lover of Kaeth;
  - enemy of Auren;
  - rival of Ithra, who undoes his work.
- **Myth:** *The Captain Who Lost.* Ryke has lost exactly one fight, to Auren, who hit him with a pot when he tried to
  take the last bowl from a child. He still hasn't forgiven her, and he still doesn't eat last.
- **Gifts:**
  - courage in a raid: no breaks;
  - a champion's strength;
  - a warband of the Ashen Banner coming to help;
  - RoM: Gladiator or Paladin.
- **Wrath:** fighters who freeze, an enemy's champion, a war god's raid.
- **Omens:** weapons ringing in the armoury, red shapes at the edge of sight before a raid.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: the War-Spirit;
  - medieval: Ryke the Red;
  - industrial: the soldiers' saint;
  - spacers: a "combat-stress psychic attractor".
- **Colour:** blood red and iron. **Symbol:** crossed blades.
- **Sound:** a war drum, Royalty's bestowing, "Final Battle".

### 12. Ithra, the Mender
- **Epithets:** the White Hand, She Who Gives and Takes Sickness.
- **Domain:** healing, mercy, plague, medicine.
- **Shape:** a hand. The White Hand, a hand of light laid on the brow of the sick (RoM's healing circles), with no arm,
  no body and no face. Clean water rises in a dry well where she has been.
- **Voice:** calm, kind, unhurried. "Lie still. This will hurt, and then it won't."
- **Temperament:** generous, distant, merciless to cruelty. She sends plagues as well as cures them.
- **Loves:**
  - the sick tended;
  - doctors who keep at it;
  - mercy to prisoners and enemies;
  - clean water.
- **Hates:**
  - cruelty, and torture of prisoners;
  - the sick left alone;
  - Ryke's carelessness with lives.
- **Sacred:** healroot; clean water; the hospital; the day the plague broke.
- **Ties:**
  - twin sister of Oskarn: they quarrel over every soul and love each other;
  - rival of Ryke;
  - ally of Auren.
- **Myth:** *The Twins at the Bedside.* Ithra and Oskarn stand on either side of every sickbed. When they agree, the sick
  one lives or dies quickly. When they argue, the fever lasts.
- **Gifts:**
  - a plague eased;
  - a doctor's hand: a Medicine passion;
  - a scar healed;
  - RoM: Priest.
- **Wrath:** a plague on the cruel (through the shared illness budget), wounds that won't close.
- **Omens:** clean water in a dry well, a warmth on the brow of the sick.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: the White Healer;
  - medieval: Saint Ithra of the Bowl;
  - industrial: the nurses' saint;
  - spacers: a "therapeutic psychic field".
- **Colour:** white and pale blue. **Symbol:** a bowl with a leaf.
- **Sound:** PreachHealth, a soft bell, "Is There Still Time".

### 13. Aal, the River-Wife
- **Epithets:** the Deep Water, Mother of the Fox.
- **Domain:** rivers, lakes, the sea, fish, floods, change.
- **Shape:** the water. She is every river and lake. She shows as a shape of a woman rising from the water and
  falling back, as a river that runs backward for an hour, as a fog that walks up from the shore, or as her voice in
  the sound of the water.
- **Voice:** changeable, sometimes laughing, sometimes cold. "Everything goes to the sea."
- **Temperament:** moody, generous, and drowning when crossed.
- **Loves:**
  - fish caught and shared;
  - bridges built with her leave;
  - offerings thrown into the water.
- **Hates:**
  - fouled water;
  - dams;
  - Varrak, who left her.
- **Sacred:** reeds; the river; the flood day.
- **Ties:**
  - former lover of Varrak;
  - mother of Velle, whom she left on the bank;
  - ally of Sella;
  - cool to Auren.
- **Myth:** *The Child on the Bank.* Aal bore Varrak's son and, fearing Auren's anger, left him in the reeds. Brannoch
  found him. Aal has watched him from every river since, and never once called him home.
- **Gifts:**
  - rich fishing;
  - a dry spell broken;
  - a flood that spares the colony;
  - RoM: Heart of Frost.
- **Wrath:** floods, fish gone from the water, a drowning (rare, at the game's rate).
- **Omens:** a river rising with no rain, a fog that comes up from the water at noon.
- **Every age:**
  - tribes: River-Mother;
  - medieval: Aal of the Deep;
  - industrial: the boatmen's lady;
  - spacers: a "hydrological psychic resonance".
- **Colour:** river blue and reed green. **Symbol:** three waves.
- **Sound:** water, Odyssey's "Impermanent Waves".

## C. Those who don't fit

14. **Orm, the Sleeper.**
    - Who: the first god, father of Varrak, who would have eaten the world.
    - Where: chained by his son and Oskarn in the deep vault, which is the Sunken Vault of Rimfolk's peoples.
    - What he does: speaks to the desperate in dreams, promises everything, and wants out.
    - Freeing him (a long, terrible quest) would change the world.
    - Form: a voice in the dark, and stone that breathes.
15. **Calla, the Morning, the dead god.**
    - Who: Mirr's sister, killed holding the gate against the Hunger.
    - Where: her bones are the old places, the ancient shrines and sealed rooms of the game. The pilgrims seek them.
    - What she does: speaks to no one, yet the dawn is a little warmer near her bones.
    - Some say she can be brought back.
16. **The Hunger** (the void, with Anomaly).
    - What it is: not a god but what lies under the gods.
    - What it does: answers anyone who asks, always pays, and always takes more.
    - Who serves it: the Choir of the Spire.
    - Its rising is the War of the First Winter, and the gods fear it coming again.
17. **The Silent Ones** (the archotechs).
    - What they are: built minds from the stars, not gods.
    - How they deal: exact, cold bargains, a price in deeds, never in offerings.
    - Who reveres them: the spacer peoples (the Broker, the Silent Captain, the Weaver, the Lattice-mind).
    - The gods distrust them, and Hadrun hates them.
18. **Tamm of the Salt, the fading god.**
    - Who: god of a salt-trading people who died out.
    - What he does: almost gone, and gentle, he asks only to be remembered. A colony that remembers him gets small,
      strange kindnesses.
19. **The newborn, your colony's own god.**
    - How it is born: from your colony's devotion, small and weak.
    - Its name: the colony names it, as the council decides.
    - Its domain: what the colony does most.
    - How it grows: with devotion, becoming a true god in a long game. The thirteen take notice, some kindly, some not.

20. **Ommet, the Counter.**
    - Who: the god of debts, bargains and balances. No body, no voice. He is the sum.
    - How he shows: in writing only. A line in the colony's ledger that nobody wrote ("owed: one life"), a debt of honour
      that settles itself, a letter's figures that add up to something else.
    - What he is felt in: every debt, favour and IOU, Rimfolk's own diplomacy of debts.
    - How he answers: he is exact and never cruel. He only balances. A colony that keeps its word finds its luck evens
      out; one that cheats finds the cost arriving from somewhere unexpected.
21. **The Mask.**
    - What it is: a god that is an object. A white mask with no mouth (RoM's possession mote), found in a ruin or brought
      by a trader.
    - What it does: whoever wears it for a night becomes, for a day, someone else. They're a better speaker or a worse
      liar, they remember things they never knew, and they speak to the colony in a voice that isn't theirs.
    - The catch: it wants to be worn, and passes from hand to hand. Festivals, secrets, a stranger's face.
    - No one knows whose face it is.
22. **Tessel, the Flock.**
    - What it is: a god that is many. A murmuration of crows over the colony that makes shapes in the sky: a word, a
      face, an arrow pointing somewhere.
    - What it does: it never lands. It warns of what's coming, and it scatters if anyone shoots.
    - Who answers it: crows everywhere are its eyes, and the river folk read it.
23. **The small ones.** Not one god but thousands:
    - the spirit of a well, a door, a crossroads, a hearthstone;
    - a bowl of milk left at the door, a nail in the threshold, a word to the well before drawing;
    - they are what the colony's people do every day, and they keep the colony's small luck: what goes missing and is
      found, the door that sticks, the dog that barks at nothing.

## D. The drama map

- **The royal house:** Varrak married to Auren; his lovers (Aal); his son by her, Velle; his father Orm, chained.
- **The sisters:** Auren and Sella; Mirr and the dead Calla.
- **The twins:** Ithra and Oskarn, quarrelling over every soul.
- **The rivals:**
  - Varrak and Sella over the rain;
  - Kaeth and Brannoch over the beasts;
  - Ylda and Hadrun, the wood against the forge;
  - Ryke and Auren, war against the hearth;
  - Ryke and Ithra;
  - Hadrun and the Silent Ones.
- **The lovers:** Ryke and Kaeth; Hadrun's unreturned love for Mirr.
- **The old wrongs:** Velle's theft of the spark and his cheating of the Ferryman; Varrak's chaining of Orm; Aal's
  abandoned child.
- **The great fear:** the Hunger rising again, a second War of the First Winter. This could be a late-game arc: the gods
  ask the colony to choose a side, or to hold a gate.

Played out, using stage 2's machinery:
- favour with one is felt by its rivals and allies, by these ties;
- a god's quarrel shows in the sky (Varrak withholding rain);
- a god asks the colony to slight a rival;
- the stranger at the gate is most often Auren;
- the white stag is Kaeth's, and her hunt passes through;
- myths are told at suppers and in letters, and the peoples' patrons and festivals follow these gods.

## E. The gods pull the strings: characters, not a menu (the player's rule)

The player: "gods should really feel like godly characters pulling the strings of the universe, not just another menu
with a list of values, names and symbols." So:

24. **The gods are behind what happens, and the colony reads them into it.** The game's own events (raids, plagues,
    storms, traders, wanderers, harvests, deaths) are attributed to the gods as they happen, by the colony's priests,
    its believers and its neighbours, and the event card names the hand behind it:
    - "Ryke sent them to try you."
    - "Velle's luck brought this trader."
    - "Sella turned her face away."

    At Felt it's always "some say", and a scoffer disagrees in a bubble. At Walking it's plain. The gods are in every
    event, not in a page.
25. **Each god has its own plans, which unfold over seasons.** Every game, each god pursues something, and the colony
    is caught up in it whether it likes it or not:
    - Varrak courts Aal again, and Auren's fury chills every hearth for a winter.
    - Velle plots to free Orm for a joke.
    - Sella bargains with Oskarn for one more spring for her son.
    - Mirr finds the gate weakening and needs someone to hold it.
    - Hadrun competes with the Silent Ones to build a greater thing than theirs.

    These arcs move through the colony's events, letters, visitors and dreams. They end with real consequences in the
    world: a people blessed, a town flooded, a war begun or ended, a god changed.
26. **The gods move the world, not just the colony.** Their quarrels show in the region's own life: two peoples at war
    because their gods quarrel, a town that thrives because its god is pleased, a plague that walks the roads because
    Ithra is angry with a cruel lord. The neighbours' letters tell of it.
27. **A god takes an interest in one person.**
    - It chooses a colonist and follows their life for years: testing them, saving them once, asking something terrible
      of them, maybe making them its voice.
    - Others notice. "She's Kaeth's. Everyone knows it."
    - It's a story with a name in it, not a number.
28. **Two gods at one bedside.** At the great moments, the gods act in front of the colony:
    - Ithra and Oskarn argue over a dying colonist, and someone in the colony can speak for them.
    - Ryke's phantoms stand with the defenders in the last fight.
    - Auren knocks at the gate on the coldest night.
29. **No meters, no stats.**
    - Favour is never a number or a bar, only how the god treats the colony and what the priest makes of it.
    - The Gods page becomes a book, not a menu:
      - the colony's own record of the gods as it knows them;
      - the myths it has heard, from whom and when;
      - what each god has done to the colony, in prose;
      - the gods' quarrels as the colony understands them, which may be wrong.
    - A god the colony has never met is only a rumour, a story, a name in a letter. The page grows as the game goes on,
      like a holy book being written.
30. **The gods are in people's mouths.**
    - Colonists swear by them, thank them, blame them, and argue about them at supper.
    - Neighbours tell their myths, and visitors bring news of what a god did elsewhere.
    - A child asks where the dead go, and gets three different answers.

## F. Ideology: three ways, in order

31. **Ignore Ideology's deities** (first). Our pantheon stands on its own, and ideoligions are people's beliefs about
    the world. A faith with deities still shows them in its own window, but Rimfolk's gods are what act.
32. **One pantheon, many names** (next).
    - Each ideoligion's deities become that faith's local names for our gods: matched by the deity's type and the faith's
      memes, and shown as "what the Kindred call Varrak".
    - Letters and speech use the local name, and the Gods page shows every name a god goes by.
    - This keeps Ideology's own window meaningful without its randomness.
33. **Full integration** (later, if wanted). Our thirteen become Ideology's deities: an ideoligion's deities are set from
    the pantheon, with our names, genders and descriptions. Memes and precepts follow, for example "Devotion to Auren:
    guests are sacred", or "Kaeth's code: no traps". Relics become the gods' own, and the rituals are theirs.
