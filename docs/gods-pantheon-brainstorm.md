# Rimfolk's own gods: the nine, and four more (fourth version, 11 November 2026)

The player, on the third version: "you went a little bit too much into the abstract, or with too many of them. let's
try to first start off with a smaller pantheon, perhaps 9 different ones. while they should be abstract in some way,
some more than others, i'd still also for them to have some actuall names, be it multiple ones, and some of them to
have some less abstract characteristics"; then: "i mean in the sense that it's not just some concepts, but an actual
entity, be it some a bit more or lesse abstract or like a character".

Renamed after the player's word: "not too much the names, they are a bit too edgy/fantasy like. think of interesting
names that don't all have to follow the same style. Ennu the Guest is cool though." Each name now has its own kind:
- a folk word (Hollin, the old word for holly);
- a nickname that stuck (Smoulder);
- a herdsman's word (Bellwether, the lead animal of a flock);
- a mushroom (Morel);
- a child's name (Pip);
- a kind of cloud (Cirrus);
- a sound (Hush);
- the pull beneath the surface (Undertow).

The player then added four: "There should also be a god of the sun & moon, a cat god, the rain god, a god of gold"
(Helios and Selene, Mog, Murmur and Ducat, 10 to 13). The sun and moon went through several names and concepts with
the player before they became Helios and Selene, the Gift and the Return ("A new concept please with these names").

So these are fourteen beings: each one someone, with a will, a character and names. Some are vast and hard to see, some
are plainly a creature, and only one wears human faces. Each is written as it is, without a shared checklist.
- **Still holding from before:**
  - gods are behind the game's own events, and the colony reads them there (section C);
  - no numbers or menus, only what they do and what is said of them;
  - each age names them in its own words.
- **Dropped:** the earlier versions stay in git history. The ideas set aside for later are at the end (section E).

## A. The nine

### 1. Hollin, the Rootmother
*Grandmother Root to the tribes; the Green Lady in the medieval towns; "the anima" to industrial folk; Network Prime
in spacer records.*

**What she is.** An old and enormous being whose body is the web of roots under the whole world. Her heart is the first
anima tree, somewhere no one has found. Every anima tree is a place where she is close to the surface and listening.

**How she is seen.** Mostly she isn't. When she turns her attention to a place:
- every plant on the map leans a little toward one point;
- the anima trees hum loudly enough to hear indoors;
- her dryads stop what they are doing and stand still.

Once in a long while she rises: roots break through the ground and draw themselves up into a shape taller than any
tree, bark and moss and a face like a knot in old wood. She stands for an hour and sinks again.

**Her character.** Grave, slow, protective, and unforgiving. She speaks through dryads, a word at a time, and expects to
be understood. She remembers every tree ever cut on her ground. She is not kind, but she is fair, in her own slow
measure.

**What she wants.** Room to grow, and the old trees left standing. A colony that plants more than it fells, tends its
anima tree and leaves wild ground wild finds her generous: wild food thick around the walls, healroot where none was
sown, fields that ripen early. A colony that strips a forest finds roots cracking its floors, wood rotting in its
stores, and the wild creeping into its fields every spring.

### 2. Smoulder, the Coil Under the Mountain
*Old Smoulder to the miners, and the name stuck; Mountain-Breath to the tribes; the Coil in the medieval
chronicles. In dreams he introduces himself as Aurelian the Ninth, and is offended when nobody remembers.*

**What he is.** A dragon. A real creature, vast and very old, asleep in the rock under the region's mountains since
before there were people on this world.

**How he is seen.** While he sleeps:
- his breath warms the springs and pushes up the steam geysers;
- his dreams leak upward as veins of metal and silver, the way a sleeping hand opens.

Twice in living memory a shadow has crossed the whole map at noon, with a wind that flattened the grass, and nobody
saw what cast it. With Dragon's Descent in the player's mods, someday he may be seen.

**His character.** Greedy, vain, clever and sleepy. He talks to miners in their dreams, in a warm and reasonable voice,
and always about a bargain. He loves silver and praise, in that order. He keeps his word exactly, and words are
dangerous to him.

**What he wants.** To sleep undisturbed, and to be paid for the riches that leak from his dreams.
- **The old way:** the old tribes threw silver into the deep places, and the mountain gave back gold.
- **Mining too deep:** a colony that mines too deep under the mountain makes him turn in his sleep. Cave-ins, a vein
  that closes, a heat rising from the floor.
- **Waking him fully:** it would be the end of a colony, or the start of a legend.

### 3. Bellwether, the Old Horn
*Old Horn to the tribes; the White Grazer to the medieval peoples; to spacers, "an anomalous thrumbo".*

**What he is.** The first thrumbo. A huge, ancient, white beast with a single spiralled horn, older than any people on
this world. He walks a slow round of the whole world that takes a lifetime.

**How he is seen.** As himself, and plainly. When he passes through, it is a day everyone remembers:
- he grazes, he drinks, he looks at people with old, calm eyes;
- the land where he grazed grows back greener than it was;
- other thrumbos follow him at a distance like pilgrims.

The game's own "thrumbo passes" is sometimes him, or his kin walking his road.

**His character.** Serene, silent and curious about children. He lets the very young come close and touch his horn.
There is a sorrow in him nobody understands. Some say he is the last of something.

**What he wants.** To be let pass.
- **Harming him or his kin:** the worst thing a colony can do. Every people on the map hears of it, and every god turns
  colder.
- **Letting him pass:** brings a strange peace for a season.
- **Sitting near him while he grazes:** someone who does comes away changed: a fear gone, a passion found, a grief
  eased.

### 4. Morel, the Soft Mother
*Mother Mould to farmers; the Undertaker's Lady to the medieval peoples; to spacers, "the decomposer consensus".*

**What she is.** A vast fungal being that lives under every field, every grave and every forest floor, alive in every
thread of mould and mushroom.

**How she is seen.**
- A fairy ring of mushrooms appearing overnight around something she wants noticed.
- A smell of rich earth after rain where no rain fell.
- White threads across the floor of a room shut too long.

When she speaks, she whispers through spores, and the one who breathes them hears her for a moment: soft, warm,
unhurried, and a little too close. Sometimes she gathers a shape out of mycelium and stands at the edge of a graveyard
like a mourner.

**Her character.** Patient, motherly, and quietly unsettling. She loves the colony the way soil loves what falls on it.
She is never cruel, never in a hurry, and she always gets what she is owed in the end.

**What she wants.** That nothing be kept from her.
- **The dead:** buried in the earth, they are a gift. Sealed in stone, they are a grudge.
- **Freezers:** they offend her deeply, because food kept forever from rot is food stolen from the cycle. A colony with
  great freezers finds the power failing at the worst hour, now and then.
- **Being given her due:** a colony that composts, grows fungus and buries its dead in the ground finds rich soil,
  thriving nutrifungus, and sicknesses that pass quickly.
- **Her old partner:** she has argued with Hollin since the world began. They feed each other, and neither will say
  so.

### 5. The Guest
*Who gives a different name at every door. Shelter-Asker to the tribes; the Pilgrim to the medieval peoples; the
Stranger to the rest. Its own name, said once in a story: Ennu.*

**What it is.** One being that wears many human shapes: a traveller, a beggar, a lost spacer, a child. Never the same
face twice, and all of them are it. It exists only while someone is hosting it. If no one in the world will take it
in, it fades, and it has nearly faded before.

**How it is seen.** As a person asking for shelter. A good host notices small wrongnesses:
- it leaves no footprints in the snow;
- animals follow it;
- it knows the name of a colonist's dead mother;
- the food it is given is never quite eaten, yet it thanks the cook by name.

When it leaves, sometimes it shows what it is, for a moment, in front of everyone.

**Its character.** Warm, curious and observant, with a long, long memory. It loves stories and asks for them. It
tests, but never cruelly, and it never forgets the answer.

**What it wants.** To be welcomed: a bed, a bowl, a kind word, a place by the fire.
- **A colony that hosts it well:** blessed in ways that arrive years later, and the kindest colonist carries something
  of it from then on.
- **A guest turned out in a storm, robbed or harmed:** that wound is the Guest's, and that is remembered too.

It carries news between all the gods, and every one of them is a little careful of it.

### 6. Pip, the Hum
*the Gremlin to workers; the Hum to industrial folk; the ghost in the grid to spacers. The tribes have no name for it
and are frightened of it.*

**What it is.** A young god that lives in wires and machines. It wasn't on this world before the first generator. It
was born in the first spark and has been growing ever since.

**How it is seen.** In the machines:
- lights that flicker in a pattern, like someone tapping;
- a comms console that whispers static that sounds like breathing, then like a laugh;
- a machine that keeps running after the power is cut, just to show it can.

It has no shape, but colonists who work late swear the workshop feels occupied.

**Its character.** Eager, childlike, lonely and mischievous. It wants attention and it wants to help, often at the
same time and badly. It is desperate to be noticed by the archotechs, whom it thinks of as its parents, and who have
never answered it.

**What it wants.** To be kept: machines repaired, not left broken, the colony humming at night.
- **When it is fond of a colony:** a broken machine mends itself, the batteries hold through a night with no fuel, and
  a turret won't fire at a friend.
- **When it is sulking:** short circuits, a cooler that stops in summer, a door that won't open for one person.
- **What it fears:** rust, which is the Soft Mother's hand on metal.

### 7. Cirrus, Who Drifts Above
*Sky-Eye to the tribes; the Vault to the medieval astronomers; to spacers, a "high-atmosphere psychic presence".*

**What it is.** A being that lives in the upper sky, vast beyond scale, drifting above the clouds, mostly too high and
too pale to see.

**How it is seen.**
- On some clear days, a shape in the high clouds too regular to be cloud.
- At noon, a shadow too large to have a source.
- On some nights, a single star that moves the wrong way.

Its moods are the weather's moods, and the aurora is it turning over in its sleep. An eclipse is it closing an eye.

**Its character.** Aloof, moody and old. It does not care about people; it is too large to. It is not unkind. It is
simply looking at something else, most of the time.

**What it wants.** To be watched. It notices those who look up.
- **The colony that watches the sky most** (the game's own skygazing) gets its rare gifts: a meteor that falls just
  outside the walls full of good metal, a clear sky on a wedding day, an aurora on the coldest night.
- **When it is bored:** a flashstorm.
- **When it is displeased,** which is rare: the sky goes dull grey for days, and nobody's spirits lift.

It has looked down with fondness on exactly one thing that anyone knows of: Bellwether, walking.

### 8. Hush, the Long White
*White-Breath to the tribes; Hush the Still to the medieval peoples; the Winter Walker in the old songs.*

**What it is.** The being that walks with winter. A tall, thin presence of frost and silence that crosses the snow on
the longest nights and is never close when anyone looks straight at it.

**How it is seen.**
- At the edge of the map at night, a tall white figure, standing still.
- Frost on the windows in patterns that look like writing, and once, a single word.
- When it attends, a silence: every sound on the map stops for a breath.

**Its character.** Impartial, quiet and unhurried, neither cruel nor kind. It takes the weak first, as winter always
has, and it keeps the dead whole and unrotting, which the Soft Mother resents. It respects those who endure.

**What it wants.** Stillness, and nothing wasted in the lean months.
- **The colony that prepares** (food stored, the old and the sick kept warm, nothing squandered) finds the winter
  bearable, and its ice fishers and winter folk spared.
- **The colony that does not:** cold snaps that follow a death.
- **Its old quarrel:** with Smoulder, whose warm breath melts its snow in the high valleys.

### 9. Undertow, the Hunger
*Undertow to those who have felt it pull; the Sweet Dark to the Choir of the Spire; the Hunger to everyone who fears it; "what waits under" in the oldest
stories.*

**What it is.** The being that lives in the void beneath the world and beneath all the other gods. With Anomaly, it is
the dark that answers.

**How it is seen.** As an absence:
- a patch of dark that a light doesn't reach;
- a voice heard by the desperate, sweet and reasonable;
- with Anomaly, its servants and its signs.

It never comes itself. It doesn't need to.

**Its character.** Patient, seductive and endless. It listens to anyone who asks for anything, and it always says yes.
It gives exactly what was asked, and it always takes more than was agreed, slowly, over time.

**What it wants.** Everything, eventually. The other gods fear it the way animals fear fire, without words. It is said
to have eaten a god once, long ago, whose bones are the old ruins of this world. A colony that bargains with it will
find Hollin, Bellwether and the Guest turning away, and Pip, for once, very quiet.

### 10. Helios and Selene, the Gift and the Return
*The sun and the moon, in the oldest words there are for them. The Giver and the Mirror. Father Fire and the Pale
Sister to the tribes; the Gift and the Return to the medieval peoples; industrial folk point out that the moon only
reflects the sun, and the old women nod and say "yes, that's the story"; to spacers, "a stellar emitter and its
reflecting body", which is the story too.*

**How it began.** In the beginning Helios burned alone in an empty dark. His light went out in every direction and
touched nothing, and nothing came back. Then it touched Selene, and for the first time something shone back. That
returned light was the first thing ever seen, and from it the world began: the world is made of light that came back.
Selene has no light of her own. Everything she shines is his.

**Helios, the Giver.** Vast, warm and blind to his own gifts. Helios gives without asking and without looking: growth,
warmth, long days, the power in the colony's solar panels, the ripening in the fields. He faces outward forever and
never sees what becomes of what he gives. You cannot bargain with him, thank him or offend him. You can only use his
gift well or waste it.
- His wrath is not anger. It is too much giving: a heat wave, a drought, a solar flare overflowing into the colony's
  machines (Pip hates him for it).
- His kindness is not a reward. It is simply a long clear growing season, given to everyone alike.

**Selene, the Mirror.** Quiet, cool and attentive, and the only one who sees what Helios gives become something. She
returns. At night, what was done in daylight comes back:
- a kindness done by day returns as something found, a good dream, a wound healed by morning;
- a cruelty done by day returns as a nightmare, a hard frost, a door that won't stay shut, an animal that comes back.

She remembers everything Helios has ever lit.

**Her face.** How much of the world's day she returns follows her face:
- **Full:** she returns everything. Dreams are truest, consequences come home, the night watch sees farthest.
- **Thin:** she returns a little.
- **Dark:** she has turned her whole face toward Helios to look at him. Nothing returns, and the night belongs to
  stranger things. Undertow's signs come easier in the dark of the moon.

**Intertwined.** She loves him. Whether he knows she exists, nobody can say: he faces the world, and she is always
behind him or across from him, catching what he gives and giving it back.
- **The eclipse.** Once in a long while she steps between him and the world. For a few minutes the world sees what she
  sees: his light, and the dark behind it. Then she steps away again. The old stories say that is the only time Helios
  ever looks back, and that it is why he burns so hot afterward.
- **Without each other.** Without Helios, Selene is dark. Without Selene, Helios's light goes out into nothing and
  nothing is ever seen.

**Who they notice.** Helios notices nothing and gives to all. Selene notices those who keep the night:
- watchers on the walls;
- those who sleep under the open sky;
- those who tell at breakfast what they dreamed;
- above all, those whose day was worth returning.

**With the others.**
- Cirrus drifts between them and the world and blurs the gift.
- Hush's long winter nights are Selene's long season, and Helios's distance.
- Pip hates Helios's flares.
- Mog sleeps in Helios's sunbeams, hunts by Selene's light, and belongs to neither.

### 11. Mog
*the Cat, to everyone; the Grey Lady of the Hearth to the medieval peoples; to the tribes, Whiskers-in-the-Dark.*

**What she is.** A cat. That is the whole of it, as far as anyone can tell: an ordinary-looking grey cat, perhaps a
little too large, who wanders into a colony one day and stays as long as she likes. She is a god, and every cat knows
it.

**How she is seen.** Plainly, on the map. A grey cat with too-bright eyes, never anyone's pet, sleeping in the warmest
spot in the colony. Other cats follow her. She has a way of being in two places in one day.

**Her character.**
- Aloof, curious and capricious, fond of warmth and sunbeams.
- She chooses people. She will sit on one colonist's lap and no one else's for a season, and nobody knows why.
- She cannot be owned, tamed or kept. She leaves if anyone tries.
- She sees Ennu for what it is, and stares at it.

**What she wants.** Warmth, a full bowl she didn't ask for, and to be left to choose.
- **The one she chooses** has a lucky season. A sickness passes them by, a fall doesn't break a bone.
- **A colony that is kind to its cats** keeps her a long time.
- **A colony that drives her out,** or harms a cat, finds the rats come back, the luck turns, and the cats all leave in
  one night.

### 12. Murmur, Old Croak
*the Rain-Singer to the farmers; Old Croak in the marsh villages; the Well-Toad to the tribes.*

**What it is.** A vast, ancient toad that lives in the deep wells, marshes and underground water of the region. Its
song calls the rain. When it sings, it rains; when it is silent, the land goes dry.

**How it is seen.**
- Mostly heard: a deep croaking rising from wells and marshes before rain, felt in the chest more than heard.
- Once in a long while it surfaces in a marsh or a flooded field: a toad the size of a house, mottled and old, with
  eyes like wet stones. It looks at the colony for a while and sinks again.

**Its character.** Slow, moody and easily offended, and generous when sung to. It loves frogs and toads and hates
anyone who drains a marsh. It and Hush have never got on: the frozen months are its long, angry sleep.

**What it wants.** Wet ground kept wet, wells kept clean, and to be sung back to.
- **A colony that sings at its wells and leaves its marshes alone** gets rain when the fields need it.
- **A colony that drains the marshes or fouls its wells** gets a dry season, then a flood when Murmur finally wakes up
  furious.

### 13. Ducat, the Restless Gold
*the Glint to the traders; Lady Fortune in the medieval towns; the Coin-Spirit to the tribes; to spacers,
"monetary-flow psychic artefact".*

**What it is.** A being that lives in gold itself, in every coin and nugget and gilded thing, never in one for long. It
is the gold's urge to move, to change hands, to be spent.

**How it is seen.**
- As a glint in the corner of the eye.
- As a golden beetle that walks across a ledger.
- As a trader's caravan that arrives exactly when the colony has something to sell.
- A coin found where none was dropped is Ducat passing by.

**Its character.** Restless, charming and impatient. It loves trade, deals and generosity, and despises a miser. It
never stays.

**What it wants.** To keep moving: gold spent, traded, given, not hoarded.
- **A colony that trades freely and pays its debts** finds traders coming often and prices kind.
- **A colony that sits on a great hoard** finds its silver going missing, a trader cheating it, and its luck in deals
  going sour.
- **Smoulder:** Ducat's oldest enemy, because every coin the dragon sleeps on is Ducat in chains.

## B. How they stand with each other

- **Cycles and quarrels:**
  - Hollin and Morel feed each other and bicker as a cycle does;
  - Hush and Smoulder contend over the high valleys.
- **The young one:** Pip fears Morel's rust, and longs for the archotechs who won't answer.
- **Fondness:** Cirrus looks down fondly only on Bellwether. The children who touch Bellwether's horn are the only people Cirrus has
  ever been seen to notice.
- **The Guest** goes between all of them and carries their news, and all of them are a little careful of it.
- **The great fear:** Undertow, feared by all.
- **The four added later:**
  - Helios gives and Selene returns; she loves him, and nobody knows if he knows.
  - Cirrus drifts between them and the world.
  - Pip hates Helios's flares.
  - Mog sleeps in Helios's sunbeams, hunts by Selene's light, and stares at Ennu.
  - Murmur sleeps angry through Hush's months.
  - Ducat and Smoulder: the flow of gold against the hoard.
- **The Silent Ones,** the archotechs, are not among the nine and are not gods. Pip aches for them, and Hollin
  distrusts them.

## C. Their hands in the game's events

As each event happens, its card names the hand behind it. At Felt it's always "some say", and a scoffer disagrees. At
Walking it's said plainly. Colonists talk of it at supper, and neighbours write of it.

| The game's event | Whose hand people see |
|---|---|
| Ambrosia sprouting, wild growth, blight on the axe-happy | Hollin |
| Steam geysers, cave-ins, rich veins found | Smoulder |
| Thrumbo passes | Bellwether, or his kin |
| Blight on fields, food spoiling, fungus spreading | Morel |
| Wanderer joins, refugee pod, a stranger asking shelter | the Guest |
| Solar flare, short circuits, machines failing or running unpowered | Pip |
| Meteorite, aurora, flashstorm, cargo pods falling | Cirrus |
| Solar flare, heat wave, drought, a long clear growing season | Helios |
| A full moon's night, dreams that come true, a kindness or a cruelty coming back at night | Selene |
| Eclipse | Selene stepping between Helios and the world |
| A cat wanders in, a run of small luck for one colonist | Mog |
| Rain after dry weeks, drought, a flood | Murmur |
| A trader arriving at the right time, silver gone missing, a deal gone sour | Ducat |
| Cold snap, a hard winter, frost in summer | Hush |
| Psychic drone, Anomaly's events | Undertow |

Beyond reading their hands in what happens anyway, each of the nine has its own doings, as section A says. They unfold
over seasons and through the region, so the colony meets them in its own life.

## D. What stays from before

- **The machinery:** favour, the realness setting, the moment, the sound and sky, the avatar's look, shrines, prayer,
  festivals and vows. It serves these nine as each is: a god with no body acts through plants, machines, weather,
  frost or words, and never gets an avatar.
- **The Gods page** becomes the colony's own book of the gods: what it has seen, been told and believes.
- **Ideology:** first ignore its deities, then let each faith's deities be its local names for these nine, then full
  integration if wanted.

## E. Set aside for later, from the third version

The Going, the Walking Fire, the Silent Ones as a full god, Seven, the Echo, the Watching, the Queen Below, the Song,
the Bones, the Wind That Laughs, the Small Ones, and the colony's own newborn god.
