# Rimfolk changelog

What changed in each update of Rimfolk, newest first, written for players. The same list is the "What's new" page at
the top of the Almanac in the game, and a letter shows the newest round once when you load a save after an update.

## Role clothes the colony can reach

*9 November 2026, version 0.192.0*

- When the game picks the clothes for your ideoligion's roles, it now leaves out pieces that need a dear research off the usual path, such as VFE Classical's togas, unless your colony has them or your ideoligion calls for them.
- The mood for a role's clothes names each missing piece and how to get it: whether the colony has one, where it can be made, or which research it needs and how far along it is.
- A new button, Choose other clothes for the role, shows the clothes the game allows for that role, those you can make first, and replaces the one you pick once you confirm, just as the game's own editor would.
- Factions' ideoligions are left as the game makes them; a setting, Role clothes the colony can reach, turns the first part off.

## Foraging is for food

*9 November 2026, version 0.191.3*

- Foraging days and the foraging decree now mark only wild plants that give food, such as berries, agave and mushrooms; wild healroot is left for you to pick when you choose.
- The decree's description says so.

## Foraging leaves the bees alone

*9 November 2026, version 0.191.2*

- Foraging days and the foraging decree now mark only wild plants whose harvest is food or medicine, such as berries, mushrooms, agave and healroot.
- Trees with a wild beehive, and other plants harvested for something you can't eat or treat with, are left as they are.

## Colony news only on days that matter

*9 November 2026, version 0.191.1*

- The evening's Colony news comes as a letter only when one of the day's lines is important or bad news.
- On a quiet day, a wish granted, a new friend, someone back at their work, the lines go to the message log in the History tab without a letter; the setting Only on days that matter brings back a letter every evening.

## The Story tab at a glance

*9 November 2026, version 0.191.0*

- Under a colonist's name, one line says who they are: the name the colony knows them by, their office, their age and where they come from.
- The top of the Story tab shows their life goal with a progress bar, the story they're living, their wish with Show me and Promise, their quirks as tags, their standing on a small trust bar, and the newest line of their history.
- Their closest friends and rivals sit beside it as small portraits with their opinion, and a click selects them.
- Everything else follows in sections that fold and remember their state, from their whole story and every wish to family, dreams, secrets and the journal; nothing the tab showed was lost.

## Rimfolk's letters open as event cards

*9 November 2026, version 0.190.0*

- Rimfolk's letters still arrive on the right as the game's do; opened, they show as a card in Rimfolk's own look that you can move and size.
- The card shows who or what it is about with their portraits, the people it comes from with their stance, and a button to see it on the map.
- Each answer is its own card with what it does, its costs and risks, and why it can't be chosen when it can't; a colony custom is marked as such.
- Who is involved is shown with their mood and how strained they are, and where the council has voted, which way it leans.
- Every answer still does exactly what the game's letter did; a setting, Rimfolk's event cards, brings back the game's letter window.

## A full kit for every colonist

*9 November 2026, version 0.189.0*

- With Simple Sidearms, every colonist who can fight should carry a gun and a melee weapon, and with Dual Wield a weapon in the off hand where it adds; an empty slot is filled before anyone gets an upgrade.
- A gap is filled from storage, from a weapon they already carry, or from another colonist's spare, choosing what suits the slot: a light quick blade for a shooter, a light gun for a melee fighter.
- The gear tab names what's missing and offers the right button, says plainly when nothing in storage can fill a gap and why, and the Defenses page shows every colonist's kit with a button to fill them all.
- Colonists fill an empty slot themselves in their free time, at home and safe, at most once a day, never replacing what they hold; a setting turns this off, and then they only ask.
- Restrictions always win: no kit for those who can't fight, no guns for brawlers, and their faith, heavy weapons, bonds and the mods' own rules are kept.

## Wood is not a weapon, and every book has its title

*9 November 2026, version 0.188.2*

- The trade window no longer lists wood under weapons; only what can be held as a weapon counts as one.
- Books Rimfolk gave the colony without a title, shown as only "(normal)", are named the game's own way as soon as they are on the map, including those already in your stores.

## The heart said once on the Mood tab

*9 November 2026, version 0.188.1*

- The Heart section under the Mood tab's summary no longer repeats the bars, what the band does to them, the advice or the day off; those stay at the top.
- It now holds only what the top leaves out: what built strain lately and what eased it, the weight under it, who close wore on them or lifted them, and what filled the good side.

## Strain and the good side as bars on the Mood tab

*9 November 2026, version 0.188.0*

- The Mood tab shows strain and the good side as two bars under the mood bar, in the colonist bar's colours, with marks where each band begins, the lasting weight under strain, and how far it moved today.
- Hover a bar for the band, what it does to them now, what built it and what would help; hover a mark for what begins there.
- The day off now has its own button beside the bars, greyed with the reason when it can't be given, and showing a day off or a bad afternoon under way with a way to end it early.
- The day off button on the colonist's command bar is gone, unless the new setting puts it back; the colonist's card and the heart's section use the same bars.
- RimHUD's strain line fits its cell again, and its strain bar shows the day-off mark.

## Every piece of armour and apparel in your mods rated

*9 November 2026, version 0.187.0*

- Every piece of armour and apparel your mods add has a rating from 1.0 to 10.0, from the game's own armour roll layer by layer over the parts it covers, against guns, blades, animals, mechanoids and fire, with cold, heat, what it costs the wearer and what it adds counted.
- Pieces made for one kind of fighter are rated for them: a shield belt for a melee fighter, a gunlink for a shooter, with both ratings kept side by side.
- Each comes with the weapons' colour-coded tags and a plain line, and its rating in the common materials; the review is in the docs/apparel folder beside the mod's files, and the card in the game and suggestions for each colonist come next.

## Planning starts at a glance, as it should have

*9 November 2026, version 0.186.1*

- The planning window now really opens on Rooms, at a glance, every room plan in columns; it had opened on the list of room plans instead.
- The Rooms tab also starts at a glance until you choose the list, whichever page the window opens on.

## The weapon in the picture, and trades for the whole colony

*9 November 2026, version 0.186.0*

- Rimfolk's weapon card shows the weapon itself, large and framed in its quality's colour, in its own material and style, and small pictures wherever it lists other weapons.
- A suggested weapon now sits as its own row in the equipment box, on Nice Inventory Tab and on the game's gear tab, with its picture, rating, gain, reason and buttons; when nothing better is stored, a quiet line says so.
- Clicking a weapon's rating badge opens Rimfolk's card for it.
- Two colonists can be asked to trade even when one gives up some, if the colony gains: the best weapons go to those who fight most, judged by their skill, how often they are drafted, their kills and their work, and the line names both sides.
- Colonists sometimes propose such a trade themselves, the one who would gain asking or the one who rarely fights offering, when both are near and both weapons in hand.
- Giving up a lucky weapon or a family heirloom is shown as a warning with its mood, never a reason to hold back the suggestion, and nobody offers up their own.

## The heart counts: stronger moods, a day off, and what would help

*9 November 2026, version 0.185.0*

- Strain now weighs -4, -8 and -14 on mood by its band and the good side lifts +3, +6 and +10; a setting chooses mild, felt or strong.
- Beyond mood, a worn colonist works and learns more slowly, needs a little more sleep and heals a little slower, while one in good heart learns and recovers a little faster; illness is fought off at most a twentieth slower.
- From worn down, a colonist may have a bad afternoon: a few hours off work to cry beside their bed, look out over the land, have a drink or visit a grave, easing a good share of their strain.
- Hearts spread a little: someone worn down wears on those close to them, someone in high spirits lifts them, never enough on its own to wear anyone down.
- Each strained colonist gets advice on what would help most, from a proper meal or a better bed to a party, a friend sent over or a decree that eases strain, with a button wherever Rimfolk can do it.
- Give a strained colonist a day off from a button: their work hours become free time with a lie-in, it eases far more than an ordinary day, once in five days each, and danger ends it.
- The Mood tab shows mood, the heart, the three things weighing on them and lifting them most, what their state does to them and the best advice at the top, with every detail below in sections that fold; RimHUD's lines match it.

## The best weapon for each colonist, and the weapon card first

*9 November 2026, version 0.184.0*

- The gear tab suggests a better weapon for each colonist from what the colony has, with a line on why, weighing their skill, passions and traits, their work, the weapon's abilities and effects, and what your last raids brought.
- One click sends them to pick it up, or to take it as a sidearm with Simple Sidearms or in the off hand with Dual Wield; two colonists can trade weapons when both gain, and "not now" puts a suggestion aside for ten days.
- The Defenses window lists every colonist's weapon beside the suggested one, and can give all the orders at once; the weapon card says who should carry each weapon.
- Suggestions respect your colonists: never a weapon their faith despises, never another's bonded or biocoded weapon, no guns for brawlers, heavy weapons only for those who can carry them.
- Now and then a colonist mentions a clearly better weapon lying in storage, by name and where it lies, rarely and never in danger.
- Opening the info of a weapon now opens Rimfolk's weapon card first; its button "All the game's stats" opens the game's own card, and a setting turns this off.

## Weapon ratings in the game, and Rimfolk's weapon card

*9 November 2026, version 0.183.0*

- A weapon's info card now opens with its rating, how it does at each distance and against each armour, its colour-coded tags with the reason for each, a plain line on what it is for, and its effects.
- Rimfolk's weapon card, from a button on the info card, a button on a weapon on the map or the small button on the gear tab, adds a chart of damage by distance, its situations and its numbers.
- The card rates the weapon for a chosen colonist, by their own skill, traits and body, says why it differs, and compares it with what they hold now.
- The weapon in hand counts: its quality, its material and the traits of a unique or persona weapon change its rating.
- A rating badge sits beside each weapon on the gear tab, and on Nice Inventory Tab's rows when you use it.
- Simple Sidearms' carried weapons and Dual Wield's offhand are rated as a pair; the card notes RunAndGun's shooting on the move and Melee Animation's executions for the weapon. Three settings under Weapons turn each part off.

## Every weapon in your mods rated

*9 November 2026, version 0.182.0*

- Every weapon your mods add has a rating from 1.0 to 10.0, worked out by the game's own rules of hitting, armour and timing against plain, armoured and heavily armoured raiders and mechanoids, with its abilities and its effects on the wielder counted.
- Each comes with colour-coded tags for its strengths, weaknesses, risks, specials, role and needs, and a plain line on what it is good and bad at; the review is in the docs/weapons folder beside the mod's files.
- Weapons that only load with a mod you don't have are left out, and so are the Kiln Guild's arms that named such a weapon kind; the weapon card in the game and recommendations for each colonist come next.

## The heart is felt: strain that stays, joy beside it, and both shown on the map

*9 November 2026, version 0.181.0*

- Heavy things weigh for days: losing a partner, kin or a friend, being downed, a deadly sickness of their own or of someone close, a fight at home with colonists down; rest no longer washes it away overnight, and strain eases less while it runs high.
- The good side grows beside strain, more slowly the harder things are, so someone worn down by the plague can still be in good heart thanks to their friends; both moods count at once.
- Colonists show how they are: sitting a while or looking out when strained, pacing at night or a sharp word when worn, humming at work or lingering at the table in good heart, singing and dancing in high spirits, each with a word of their own.
- Strong feelings act: grief at the grave or by the empty bed, fear seeking company indoors after dark, anger slamming a door, love sitting with a partner, homesickness talking of home by the fire, joy shared with a friend.
- A sign over the head shows a strong feeling, a thin line under each name on the colonist bar shows strain and the good side, and a message says when someone is worn down or in good heart, and why.
- A calm colony comes to be steady in about a week and in good heart in two or three; each new sign, act and message has its own setting.

## Rimfolk's illnesses share the game's rate more gently

*9 November 2026, version 0.180.0*

- A serious illness of Rimfolk's still makes the game's next disease likely to pass the colony by, but three times in four rather than always, so different sicknesses can each be met now and then.
- After one of the game's diseases, Rimfolk's serious ones rest for about a week, then come back slowly over the following weeks instead of all at once.
- Rimfolk's own serious illnesses come a little more readily while the setting is on, so in an ordinary colony they are a third or more of the serious cases, while serious illness in all stays about a tenth above the game's own.
- Someone already fighting a serious illness rarely catches a second one, and a healthy colonist is more likely to be the one who catches it from the sick.
- The illness letter and the setting's note now say the game's next disease will most likely pass the colony by, rather than promising it.

## The mailbag gets a picture of its own

*9 November 2026, version 0.179.2*

- The mailbag by the gate showed a red cross where its picture should be; it now shows a wooden post with a leather satchel on a peg, a brass post-horn plate and letters sticking out, one sealed in red wax.
- It is drawn in the game's own style and colours, so it sits beside the caravan spot and the other markers without standing out.

## Planning opens on every room at a glance

*9 November 2026, version 0.179.1*

- The planning window now opens on Rooms, at a glance, every room plan in columns, whenever you open it with its button; a link to another of its pages still opens that page.
- A new setting, Planning window opens on, beside the council window's, can keep it on the page you last looked at instead.

## All of Rimfolk's windows move, size and wear its own look

*8 November 2026, version 0.179.0*

- The council's windows, Planning and Defenses now float like the region window: moved by the title bar, sized from any edge, made large and back, left open while you play on the map, one of the four at a time, each opening where you last left it.
- The Almanac and Rimfolk's dialogs, from the letter window, the journey planner and the camp to the market, the founding window, the hand-over and the small naming dialogs, have the same frame and remember their place and size.
- Every page and dialog of Rimfolk's now draws its buttons, fields, switches and lists in Rimfolk's own look, and wider windows show more, such as three columns of This week or a longer list of who goes on a journey.
- Leaving a page for the map, research, quests or work closes the floating window as the old tab did; previews and the search take the look of the window they come from.
- The on-screen panels, the room and defense planners docked above the architect, and the game's own windows keep the game's look; turning Rimfolk's own look or moving windows off puts everything as before.

## Rimfolk's own look, and windows you can move and size

*8 November 2026, version 0.178.0*

- The region window now floats: drag it by its title bar, size it from any edge or corner, make it large with the button or a double-click on its title and back, and it stays open while you click on the map or pick a colonist; it opens where you last left it.
- The trade window moves and sizes the same way, and both remember their place between games, fitted onto your screen if it changes.
- Both wear Rimfolk's own look: a calm dark ground, rounded cards, tabs that read as tabs, flat buttons with clear hover and pressed states, a lit row for what is chosen, one amber accent, allied in blue and hostile in orange always with a word beside, and text kept easy to read.
- A wider window shows more: two columns in Here and away and Faces, a wider list of peoples, more of each people's acts side by side.
- Settings: Rimfolk's own look and Rimfolk's windows move and resize, both on, and Put Rimfolk's windows back; off, the windows look and sit as before. The council's windows, the Almanac and Rimfolk's dialogs follow next.

## Serious illness shares the game's own rate

*8 November 2026, version 0.177.0*

- Rimfolk's serious illnesses no longer come on top of the game's own diseases: each one Rimfolk gives holds back the game's next disease on that home, and after the game's own disease Rimfolk waits a month, so serious illness in all comes about as often as in the game without Rimfolk, some of it simply Rimfolk's kinds.
- Crowding and a dirty kitchen still make the catching illnesses likelier, but they now change which illness comes rather than how many; Rimfolk's own stay a minority of the cases, about one in seven in an average colony.
- The colony's news says when a disease passed the colony by and what took its place; a new setting, Serious illness shares the game's own rate, under Serious illnesses, turns it off. Everyday ailments, frailty and dirty wounds are unchanged.

## Dealings with a whole town, pen friends, and the world writing first

*8 November 2026, version 0.176.0*

- A town's card and the Peoples page now offer dealings with its council as a whole, sent as a letter by the post, your rider, a relay or radio, and answered by their regard and their ways: a supply contract delivered each season at your gate, relief for a town in hard times, a fair day or friendship proclaimed, your trade terms posted, and market rights for a stall in their town with better terms.
- You can hire harvest hands or a guard detail from a town for a season, who camp and work for you and are paid at their fire, and petition a council about its raiders, for amends, or to settle refugees with them or take theirs in.
- Joint works with a neighbour: a real road between you that caravans and the post travel faster, a well or a bridge that raises their fortunes, or a watchtower that warns of raiders from that side; and you can invite a whole town to your feast or send your entertainers to their festival.
- A steady exchange of letters with one of their notables makes a pen friend, who warns of raids from near them, eases trade, introduces you further off and may do you a favour; peoples now also write to you first now and then with news, a request or an invitation, answered when the next carrier passes.
- A town beyond your reach can be reached through a people at peace with both, a little slower, and the map rings it so.

## The last four peoples: the Helix Works, the Lattice, the Choir of the Spire, the Sunken Vault

*8 November 2026, version 0.175.0*

- The Helix Works, gene-crafters in clinic mint, buy a blood sample from your colonist with the rarest genes, write a gentle gene into someone for a price, leave a case of medicine at your gate, and now and then ask your doctor to see to their vat-grown when a batch goes wrong.
- The Lattice, mechanitors in indigo, mend your hurt mechs, buy subcores and chips, can raise a small turret at your gate, and when one of their mechs wanders onto your land you may give it back or keep it.
- The Choir of the Spire, calm psycasters from beside an archotech spire, sit in a ring and listen to your colony, which leaves some calmer and others uneasy; they trade psychic and archotech goods for art, and a listener may stay a few days to watch your people work.
- The Sunken Vault came up from a shelter with spacer gear it cannot make: its people slip back an age every few years unless someone keeps them supplied, trade their last spacer goods for components and food, ask you to teach a lost craft, and a vault-born child who has never seen the sky may ask to stay.
- Each has its story, look, gear and gifts from your mods, a faith, town, letters and its own voice; with them every age has peoples of its own, and peoples arising in play can take their ways too.

## Answers say how they would land, not what they change

*8 November 2026, version 0.174.0*

- Under each answer of a council question, an event or a world event there is now one short line in the story's own voice about how it would land, such as "Avila would take it hard; Guido would see the council kept faith." or "Guido won't like it; it may come to a scuffle between Guido and Avila.", in place of the list of spirits, trust and sizes.
- The line never says the opposite of what happens, names who it touches, and keeps to the weightiest one or two things, a risk to someone always among them; the wording varies from letter to letter but stays the same each time a letter opens.
- Hovering an answer still shows the plain hint of what it does, and the setting What each answer does keeps hints in words, exact numbers or nothing for anyone who prefers them.

## Your silver at a glance in the trade window

*8 November 2026, version 0.173.1*

- The trade window shows your silver in large at the top of its side panel, now and after the trade with an arrow between, and says plainly whether you can afford it: green with what you pay or are paid, red with how much you are short.
- Under it, smaller, the trader's silver and whether they can pay you all they owe; the whole box lights up as the game's own does when the silver won't do.

## A new diplomacy screen, and letters written for a reason

*8 November 2026, version 0.173.0*

- The region window's Peoples view is now a list of every people beside the chosen one's page: grouped allies, at peace and at war, with a search, filters for neighbours, mail and dealings, and a sort by closest (the default, in days on the road), regard, name or age.
- Each people's page shows its age and which way it is heading, its regard with this season's trend and why, what they want and fear as far as you know it, and how to reach them now, with tabs for their towns (closest first), their people, your dealings and your history together.
- A Do something with panel aims at the whole people, one of their towns or one person, and lists every act that fits, each saying what it brings, how it travels and what it costs, greyed with the reason when it isn't open.
- Letters now wait for a reason: condolences after a loss you heard of, a warning when you know of a threat to them, an invitation when a feast or fair is near or guest beds stand empty, asking for help when food or medicine runs short, greetings at the turn of the year or after a gift; asking for news is always open. A setting, Letters by occasion, opens them all again.
- Choosing a people lights its towns on the region map, and a small web shows who stands with whom.

## A camp far off meets you at the gate

*8 November 2026, version 0.172.1*

- Paying a camp, sending a supper or meals, or anything else your colonists carry to a camp no longer sends them across the map when the camp is far: one of the camp walks to your gate to meet them, and the hand-over happens there.
- Whoever of the camp is already at the gate, such as the woodcutter bringing the load of wood you bought, takes the payment there; a near camp is still met at its fire, and going over to learn from them or to help stays a walk to the camp.

## Four more peoples: the Signal Line, the Deepwell Union, the Last Republic, the Hollow Fleet

*8 November 2026, version 0.172.0*

- The Signal Line keeps radio masts across the region: a lineman may raise one on your high ground, and while it sings your letters travel twice as fast and a raid's warning comes a few hours before it; storms wear it down, and the linemen climb it to mend it.
- The Deepwell Union drills the deep seam under your land for a share, its assayer leaves a crate at your gate, and now and then it strikes, stopping its trade until a shift-captain is given food or a good talker; the drill can hurt your miner, on the shared budget.
- The Last Republic counts your people bed to bed, offers citizen's papers with better trade and a duty owed, may call a colonist up for a season's service who comes home with pay, and praises or denounces you on its radio.
- The Hollow Fleet, a grounded colony ship's crew in ship's grey, lands a shuttle by your gate to trade, buys salvage and ship chunks, and a sleeper woken after two hundred years may ask you for shelter.
- Each has its story, look, gear and gifts from your mods in every age it can reach, a faith, town, letters and lines in its own voice; and every visitor at your gate now walks at the normal pace, not only the newest.

## Couriers and visitors walk at a normal pace

*8 November 2026, version 0.171.1*

- Rimfolk's couriers, post riders, envoys and parties, visitors and camps coming and going, and anyone sent on an errand now move at the ordinary pace your own colonists work at; before, most of them walked at the game's slow walk, half that speed.
- A stroll, a wander about a camp, browsing a stall, herds and pets on their own business, a group keeping together and someone carrying another still go slowly, as they should.

## The post: letters travel with someone

*8 November 2026, version 0.171.0*

- Letters you write now wait in a mailbag by the gate (a new spot under Misc, or the gate itself) until someone going that way takes them: traders, visitors and guests, camps breaking up, pilgrims, travellers, messengers or your own caravan, one of them stopping at the bag on the way out. No messenger appears from nowhere any more.
- A post comes by the age: none for a tribe, a rider on one of their beasts every week or two in the medieval age, a mail run every few days in the industrial age, a shuttle for spacers. It brings your mail and takes what goes its way; how often is a setting.
- For something that can't wait, send it with your own rider: a colonist sets out as a small caravan, delivers it, waits for the answer and brings it home. Letters coming to you arrive the same way, with whoever comes from that direction, and only if nobody comes for a while does a messenger bring them.
- Writing takes a colonist who can read, and a tribe gives its words to the carrier aloud. Your comms console still calls peoples with radio at once.
- Peoples now have a range: how far a letter can go is the real travel time over the land, slower over hills, swamps and snow and quicker on roads. Far towns are reached by relay through a friendly people's town, the furthest only by radio; each town's card says how and how soon, and the region map rings the far ones.

## A camp's farewell says what it means

*8 November 2026, version 0.170.1*

- A camp's goodbye letter no longer offers "Let them go.", which read as sending them off at once: the plain answer is now "Wish them well on the road.", and its tip says they pack up and go when their stay is done, by daylight.
- Nothing else changes: a camp whose stay ends at night waits for first light, packs for a few hours on the map, and walks off.

## More camps, bands against them, and old camp sites

*8 November 2026, version 0.170.0*

- A new setting, How many camps, under the camps: Rare, Usual (as before), Many or Crowded sets how often camps come, how long the land rests after one leaves, and how many may stand at once, up to four on a big map; a small map or one mostly mountain or water takes fewer.
- Several camps live side by side, each on its own site well apart, never two peoples at war together, and the World page shows a card for each and why no new one came.
- Now and then a band of a people at war with a camp's people comes for that camp from the map's edge away from you: you are warned, and may send help, send a runner or a radio call to warn them, or stay out; they stand or flee, and the fight is the game's own.
- If you fought beside them they thank you with goodwill and a gift carried to your gate, and the band's people remember it; a warning alone is remembered too.
- What a camp leaves standing becomes a named site with a history of who held it; a later camp may move in, mend what stands and build only what is missing, and a site held by rivals becomes contested, where old holders come back to take it.

## Peoples whose age changes

*8 November 2026, version 0.169.0*

- Rimfolk's peoples no longer stay in the age they began in: each season every people moves a little toward the next age, at a pace set by its ways, the learned society and the Stormglass Conclave soonest, the herders and the Mossborn slowest, and each up to the highest age its ways accept.
- The colony speeds them along: trade with them, gifts of books, techprints, components or tools, their people learning at your colony or teaching yours, and an alliance or pact can make a people rise up to three times faster.
- A people cut off from the world, with no trade for years, its towns burned or at war with most of the world, stops rising and can slowly fall back an age.
- When a people reaches a new age or falls back, a letter in its own voice says what it learned or lost and your part in it, and its page shows its age and which way it is heading and why.
- Their people from then on dress and arm in the new age, the hospitallers' guards in flak with guns, the league with charge rifles as spacers, and their camps, words, messengers, ransoms and traders' goods follow; a setting, Peoples' ages change, keeps every people in its first age.

## A pet bitten on the hunt still eats

*8 November 2026, version 0.168.1*

- A pet hunting for food no longer gives up the moment it is bitten or its prey turns on it: a wound that wants tending or the prey fighting back is part of a hunt, and once the prey is dead it eats before going home.
- It still breaks off for your word, being downed, badly hurt or bleeding out, or another danger on the map; setting out still needs it well and the map quiet.

## Bed rest only when it makes sense

*8 November 2026, version 0.168.0*

- Colonists now go to bed rest only for something serious: a deep, bleeding or vital wound, wounds that bring strong pain or leave them barely able to walk or work, an illness that can kill until they are immune, or one that badly hits consciousness, moving or breathing.
- Everything minor is worked through with its pain, slower hands and bad mood: sunburn, lice, a sprain, small tended cuts, a head cold. Some of them make a colonist sleep a little longer at night, never all day.

## Weather that changes like weather

*25 October 2026, version 0.16.0*

- The game still chooses each weather from the land's own, Seasonal Weather's and other mods' included, but Rimfolk weighs the choice by the land and the last days. Spells of rain and of dry weather hold and then break, a long dry spell in the warm with a storm.
- One weather follows another as it would: a storm seldom comes out of a clear sky except on a hot afternoon, fog comes after a cold clear night or rain, and a cold front brings snow, or a storm where it's still warm. A morning fog lifts by late morning.
- Each land keeps its character. A desert's dry spells run longer and its wet ones end sooner, a tropical land Seasonal Weather doesn't cover gets a wet and a dry season, and over the years every land keeps about the rain it had. Special weathers and anything an event forces are left alone.
- Rimfolk's events move the weather after them: the long rains bring more rain, a thirsty herd comes in a dry spell that holds, and the sky clears after hail. Heat waves, warm spells and cold snaps bring clear skies, and no drought ever brings more rain.
- The weather moves the temperature a few degrees: cooler under rain on a warm day, colder on clear nights (much colder in the desert), milder under cloud.
- The colonist who reads the weather best says what's coming a few hours ahead, and the Overview's Weather card shows it. The weather that comes is held to, and the forecast is as good as the one who makes it; a weather station from Stormproof or Weather Control makes it nearly certain.
- Each year leans a little wetter or drier, warmer or colder, and the annals say so when it was. Each part has its own setting under "The weather" on the Events page.

## Faith in the colony

*20 October 2026, version 0.8.0*
