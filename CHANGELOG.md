# Rimfolk changelog

What changed in each update of Rimfolk, newest first, written for players. The same list is the "What's new" page at
the top of the Almanac in the game, and a letter shows the newest round once when you load a save after an update.

## Fealty and vassals

*5 November 2026, version 0.97.0*

- The colony can swear fealty to a stronger neighbour at peace: someone of theirs comes to the gate, anyone who objects has their say before the council, and the speaker swears by their rite, in each age's form.
- A liege takes tribute at the gate each season, 150 to 600 silver's worth by the colony's wealth; short, it is owed, and still owed a season on, the oath breaks.
- A liege's band comes when a home is raided, nine times in ten, and its letter calls the colony's levies when its town is attacked; renouncing costs their grudge unless they failed the colony that season.
- The hamlet once sworn and up to two struggling towns that ask for protection are the colony's vassals: produce at harvest as the council sets it, levies when called, and asks for help, a judgment or a camp broken.
- The quartermaster's collect the dues task is open: a vassal's season of dues by letter and carter, or the colony's tribute to its liege seen paid in full.
- Faction Territories' vassals get a head of their first people who writes, thinks of the colony and asks for help, including against the mod's invasions; their tribute, purchases and points stay that mod's.
- 31 conversation pieces, 27 letter lines and 16 spoken lines speak of fealty and vassals.

## The keeper of secrets and council tasks

*5 November 2026, version 0.96.0*

- A new council seat, the keeper of secrets (Social and Intellectual, from six voters): while it is filled the colony's own secrets slip half as often, and the keeper walks with guests after dark.
- Eight officers can take a task with a neighbour: the speaker wins over a town's head, the quartermaster opens a trade, the marshal patrols at dawn or drills a pact partner's fighters, the physician takes medicine to a sick town, the warden brings the colony's own home for less, the chaplain keeps or speaks for the faith, the steward prepares a feast.
- Officers visit by real caravan, planned in the journey planner: the officer and a companion, one away from a home at a time, each office once a season.
- The keeper can learn what a notable keeps quiet through talks, a paid informer or a visit, and the colony can press it: they pay to keep it quiet, or refuse and have it told. One step in ten the keeper is found out, and the notable says so.
- Every task shows its last step and the next on the officer's card, under its seat on the Decrees page, in Here and away and in This week, never as a time left.

## Guests, wards and in-laws

*5 November 2026, version 0.95.0*

- Guests, wards, hostages and in-laws have far more to say: 127 new conversations in every age, from the feast's weather and seating to a forfeit hostage's last word and a grudge handed down.
- Neighbours write about the feast they came to, the ward you keep, the hostage they hold and the marriage between you, and they answer invitations, wards and matches in more of their own words.
- A wedding's guests greet the colony at the gate as kin come to a wedding, and in-laws who spot a raiders' camp write to the colony as family.
- Letters told often have more wordings: the feast day, the champion, a ward's letters home in each age, a hostage forfeit, the kin's gift each season, a broken alliance.

## Weddings, checked over

*5 November 2026, version 0.94.1*

- Fixed: a wedding begun late in the day no longer leaves its guests without the evening's feast. The feast waits for the couple's party after the vows, and the ceremony is not begun once the feast is due.
- Fixed: the wedding letter speaks of kin among the guests only when they came, and of a defense pact only when one stands.
- Fixed: the agreement letter promises the wedding's guests only when some of their kin can come.
- Fixed: no match letter promises a defense pact with the Empire, or with any people while pacts are off.
- Fixed: a spouse carried off by raiders who in time goes over to them is no longer taken for one the colony sold.

## Marriage alliances

*5 November 2026, version 0.94.0*

- Marriage alliances: a match between one of the colony's and one of a people at peace, by "Speak for a match" face to face, a letter from a people's or a notable's card, or their own offer once a year.
- The colonist is asked first: one who loves someone else here, would leave children behind or can't stand the one chosen is against it, and the council hears them before anything is settled.
- Where the couple lives is chosen each time: theirs comes with their kin as the wedding's guests, joins at the gate and is married by the game's own ceremony before the evening's feast, or their kin come for the colony's own, who leaves with them for good.
- While it holds their notables think better of the colony, their kin send a gift each season, they write when a raiders' camp is set against the colony, and it stands as a defense pact (never with the Empire, whose titled nobles marry only the titled).
- A child of the two keeps the alliance after a death; a spouse wronged by the colony breaks it, and their head's grudge passes to the next.

## Turrets where they stand

*5 November 2026, version 0.93.1*

- A faith that scorns automated turrets now minds only the turrets where its people are: a turret handed over by a hacked terminal in a hatch's dungeon, or one at another home, no longer sours everyone at home. A new setting, on at first, can turn this back.
- Fixed: babies no longer carry the council's colony-wide effects (militia drills, mourning, a steady marshal, a good physician) in their health.

## Wards and hostages

*5 November 2026, version 0.93.0*

- Wards: a people at peace may send a youth of a notable's house to live at the colony for a year, or the colony asks by letter; they join as the game's lodger, a guardian teaches them an hour a day, and how they were kept goes home with them when their kin come to the gate.
- The colony can send a youth of its own to live with another people for a year, writing home every ten days and coming back with that people's skill and a friend there; with the Empire, a page in a titled household.
- Hostages, adults only, can be a truce's price or a defense pact's term either way: if their side breaks a truce, their hostage is the colony's to decide about, and if the colony breaks its word, its own is held in the game's own captivity.
- Fixed: a pact the colony ends by letter is no longer answered with words meant for a refused offer.
- Fixed: a trap sprung at an exchange of captives is remembered as its own deed, not as a debt refused.
- Fixed: a truce sworn by word, with no people to vouch for their envoy, no longer promises an envoy at the gate.

## Feasts and contests with guests

*5 November 2026, version 0.92.0*

- Feasts with guests: once a season, invite up to three notables of peoples at peace by letter or call, from a people's card, the calendar or the steward's suggestion; each answers by their regard and whether their people's hopes are offered.
- On the day the guests walk in with one or two of their people and wait by the gate, and up to four contests are held on the map: wrestling, a race, a hunt, a game at the colony's tables, and a shooting match where a mod gives targets.
- The feast is the colony's gathering with the guests at its table; the champion of the day is named and the prize is carried to them, and the guests stay the night and go home at first light.
- Their welcome moves their regard and their people's memory: a meal, a fine one for the Empire, what they hoped for, the prize, a bed; an empty plate is a slight.
- The risks are named before the choice: a wrestling blow goes in hard about one time in 25, a hunted beast may turn, and about one guest night in 15 ends in a fistfight.
- Each feast has its Event card, a row on the calendar, a feast day in This week and its guests in Here and away; 27 conversation pieces, 24 letter lines and 47 spoken lines speak of it.
- The colony's own at a neighbour's feast tell what they won when they come home.

## Who to bring and what to pack

*5 November 2026, version 0.91.0*

- The journey planner shows everyone at home as a row: health, mood and heart, the skills a journey uses with the colony's best in gold, weapon and armor, whether their clothes cover the weather on the way, the drugs they need, what they carry, and what the home loses while they are away, grouped Recommended, Can go and Better not with the reason in a line.
- The planner reads what a journey is for from where it goes (a fight, trade, talks, an escort, a mission's errand) and how dangerous the place looks, and recommends whom to send by it: fighters and a doctor for a fight, the best negotiator for trade, the speaker or best talker for talks, never the home's last doctor or cook while someone else could go.
- Recommended for this journey: medicine by the danger, silver and goods for trade, a town's trade request, a weapon for each traveller who has none (taken up before they set off), bedrolls, and pack animals and mounts by the load. Pick the recommended fills who goes, the animals and the things in one press, and nothing is picked but by your click.
- Bars at the planner's foot for the load against the room, the caravan's own speed, and the food's days against the road's.
- With RimMarket, what lies on the colony's stalls is listed in the planner beside the stores, the stores' taken first, and a traveller takes the rest out of the stall's stock before setting off.

## What lies between peoples

*5 November 2026, version 0.90.0*

- Visitors have about 150 new things to say about debts of honor, pacts, truces, captives and envoys, each in their own people's way: an envoy at your table through the rain, a raid and a funeral, a former captive back as a guest, a band that buried its own after your fight.
- Neighbours answer letters about debts, pacts, truces, ransom and envoys in many more words, by how they took it and by their ways, and letters of any kind now mention a debt, pact, truce, captive or envoy that stands between you.
- The letters and news of diplomacy have more than a hundred new wordings: debts called in, truces offered, sworn and broken, a band on its way, the ransom at the gate, the envoy's arrival, letters home and leaving.
- Whoever lights a signal fire for a defense pact says something as it catches, and peoples of the middle ages and the Empire no longer talk like a tribe's elders in letters and at the gate.

## Diplomacy, checked over

*5 November 2026, version 0.89.1*

- Fixed: an envoy downed in a fight who then dies, is taken prisoner or is carried to a bed now gets the right ending, letter and regard.
- Fixed: the colony's envoy ambushed on the road carries on to the town once the caravan sets off again.
- Fixed: Needs you no longer lists a truce offer, pact offer or call to arms that can no longer be answered.
- Fixed: with Faction Territories, a caravan that joins the fight at a partner's town through Join fight keeps the call instead of breaking the pact.

- Fixed: after loading a save, a war already answered brings no second call to arms, and the council isn't asked an avenger's objection twice.
- Fixed: a partner's fighter killed by a stray shot while their band fights on the colony's land no longer breaks the pact.

## Ransom and the envoy

*5 November 2026, version 0.89.0*

- Ransom: some days after one of a people is taken prisoner, their messenger (a go-between when they are at war, or a call at the comms console) may come to the gate with an offer. Accept, ask for more, let them go for nothing, or keep them; refused three times, they hold a grudge.
- Without a powered comms console, the game's own ransom demand for one of the colony's comes by their messenger at the game's rate and fee. Paid, the speaker carries the silver out, and the colonist walks home some hours later.
- Exchanges: offer one of theirs for one of the colony's by letter, from the cards or face to face, and both walk at once at the gate. With a people at war an exchange can be a trap, at the game's peace talks' rate by the negotiator, and the letter names the chance first.
- Their envoy for a season: a guest who eats at the colony's table, sleeps in a bed set aside, talks with its people, sits in at a council meeting and writes home every five days; how the stay goes travels home with them.
- The colony's own envoy: a colonist good with people lives at their town for a season, sends its news every five days, and comes home better with people, with a parting gift when their hosts think well of the colony.
- Captives and envoys show on the cards, in Here and away and in the inbox, with 16 conversation pieces, 24 letter lines and 23 spoken lines. The settings Ransom and Envoys are in the Diplomacy section, and the Almanac's diplomacy page has five new sections.

## Pacts and truces

*4 November 2026, version 0.88.0*

- After beating a raid, holding one of their notables or kin, or with a friend's word for peace, the colony can offer a people at war a truce for a season or a year, with a price either way; they now and then offer one themselves after a defeat.
- Their envoy walks to the gate under their sign of parley and the colony's speaker walks out to swear it there; a colonist sworn to vengeance on them has their say before the council first.
- While a truce holds, their raids keep away from that home and their raiders' camp breaks up; once per truce their firebrands may raid anyway, and at its end it is a peace if their goodwill has come round.
- Defense and trade pacts with friends, for a year and renewable: by letter, face to face ("Speak of a pact"), or offered by them; the Empire makes truces but no pacts.
- During a threat, call a defense partner by the comms console or a signal fire by the gate, and their band walks in some hours later; when their town is attacked, send fighters by caravan and fight beside them there.
- A trade pact brings their caravan each season and takes a cart of the colony's surplus for silver.
- Debts of honor can now be called in for fighters, both ways.
- Attacking a partner breaks a pact or truce, and the peoples in reach hear of it; it all shows on the people and town cards, the inbox and the map's lenses.

## One look across every window

*4 November 2026, version 0.87.0*

- Every window reads the same way now: one set of colours by meaning, each beside its word or icon (friendly green, neutral blue, hostile red, needs you soon orange, harm now a brighter red with the warning sign, done a muted green, the colony's own gold), and the pages' own colours gave way.
- Section headings carry their counts, tags are a dot and a word, filters are small chips with counts, numbers line up on the right, and an empty list says in one sentence what will show there.
- An act that can't be done now is greyed with the reason as its tooltip and, on a card and on the defense lines, in a line under it, instead of saying no after the click.
- The overview's numbers and the Moods page show a short line of the last week, rising, falling or steady.
- Names are links in more places: the on-screen panels (a click on a colonist's name opens their card, the rest of the row still takes you to them), the hover card in the corner, This week's journeys, and the council's pages (decrees, offices, wishes, dilemmas, the chronicle, bonds, faiths, the calendar).

## One inbox, and search

*4 November 2026, version 0.86.0*

- This week is now the one place where everything waits on you: what you follow, what needs you, what is under way, what is coming, how people are and the plan, each line one sentence with its names leading to their cards and its act on the right.
- A raiders' camp's terms, a hamlet's ask, a debt a people calls in and settlers at the gate now wait in This week with the rest, and count with them.
- What is under way shows the step it is at (written, on the road, arrived, answered), never a countdown, and what is coming shows the day it comes.
- One count of what waits on you, the same on This week's tab, on the inbox in every window's corner and on the council button (a setting, on at first).
- The pin on any card follows it, up to twelve: a colonist, someone out in the world, a town, a people, a family, a debt. Each shows at the top of This week with its newest change, in the corner's list and in an optional Following panel on the screen.
- A search in every window's corner, and a key you can bind, finds colonists, people outside, places, peoples, houses, the windows' views and the Almanac's pages by name. Enter opens the first.

## One look, the first part

*4 November 2026, version 0.85.0*

- The defense window reads like the other windows: a "?" beside its title to its Almanac page, sections that fold with their counts, buttons greyed with the reason when they can't be used yet, and the card of a colonist or a people named there in a drawer at its right.
- Names in the Story, Culture and Mood tabs and in the Social tab's column are links: a relative, a bond, a nemesis, a people or the one a mood is about shows a preview after a pause and opens its card on a click.
- A name inside a preview shows one more preview beside it, two at most, and both stay while the mouse moves between them; the setting that turns previews off covers both.
- A new setting, Window size: Standard keeps Rimfolk's windows at 1010 by 720, and Large lets them grow up to 1280 by 860 on a screen with the room, so the map, the tables and a card beside them get more space.

## The region in four views

*4 November 2026, version 0.84.0*

- The region window has four views instead of ten tabs: Map, Peoples, Here and away, and History. Every old page name, key and link still leads to the right place.
- The region map reads six ways through its lenses, Land, Stance and ties, Fortunes, Threats, Trade and Ours, each with its legend at the map's foot and a List of what it shows, nearest first.
- Stance and ties draws a line from home for each tie, with debts of honor dotted, protection double and a grudge in red; Threats draws a raiders' camp's line to the home it raids, and Trade each post's line to its town.
- Trading posts, raiders' camps and hamlets are the Trade, Threats and Ours lenses now, with their lists, Pay on a camp's row, and how the last ones went.
- Peoples shows what is owed each way and a card for each people with its ties and acts; its Faces mode has everyone the colony knows by name, its old foes and those who left.
- Here and away gathers who is here from outside, what is on its way, letters on the road, who of the colony's is away, missions and the colony's houses.
- History looks at this colony, the region or the whole world, shows the world's events running in a strip at its top, and lists the colony's letters lately.

## The council in four views

*4 November 2026, version 0.83.0*

- The council window has one row of four tabs: This week, People, Council and Colony. What were pages under them are now modes, small buttons under each view's title.
- People is one table of everyone. A click on a row opens that colonist's card beside it, with their whole story, their dreams and their journal.
- People, then Houses shows the colony's families, from the ties of blood and marriage among its people, and the houses it knows of in the region.
- Every dream and journal is in the Chronicle under its Dreams and Journals filters, and a colonist named in the chronicle is a link to their card.
- The planning window has four views: Plan, with the advisor's priorities as its second mode, Base, Making, and Rooms, as a list or at a glance.
- The Almanac opens in a window of its own that you can move and size beside what it explains: from the book in every window's corner, or from the "?" on every view, which opens that view's page. A page you come back to opens where you left it.
- A colonist's portrait in the council window opens their card in the drawer at the window's right, and the old shortcuts and buttons still lead where they went.

## Names that lead somewhere

*4 November 2026, version 0.82.0*

- Every name in the region window now leads somewhere: click a town, a person, a people, a family, a debt or a line of the history and its card opens in a drawer beside what you were looking at.
- Every card has the same parts in the same order: what is happening now, its people, its ties, what was said, its story with a link to its whole history, and what the colony can do, each act greyed with the reason when it can't be done.
- On the region map the drawer is the map's panel: picking a town opens its card, and the small world button on a place's card still shows it in the game's world view.
- Rest the mouse on a name for a moment and a small preview says who or what it is; move into it to keep it open. The settings under Names and cards set the pause or turn previews off.
- Back and forward arrows in every Rimfolk window's corner retrace the pages, filters and cards you opened; two keys can be bound, and a mouse's side buttons work where the system reports them.
- Show on the trading posts, hamlets, raiders' camps and journeys pages and on This week now opens the place on the region map with its card, instead of the game's world view.

## More than one place to gather

*4 November 2026, version 0.81.1*

- Fixed: gatherings, walks and fires held out of doors always met on the same patch of ground, and a fire laid for a gathering always went to the same spot by the middle of the colony.
- The colony now knows its few good places in the open (a terrace, the yard by the kitchen, the ground by the river) and each gathering chooses one, the nicer ones more often and not the place used last time while another good one is there.
- A gathering keeps the place it chose: its walk, its fire and everyone in it meet there.

## Debts of honor

*4 November 2026, version 0.81.0*

- A good turn big enough leaves a debt of honor between the colony and a people, either way, and the letter that tells of it says so in both sides' words by their age. A notable helped in person owes the colonist who did it.
- Call a debt in by letter, by call, from a town's panel on the region map or face to face ("Ask a favor of their people"): a load of goods brought by their porters, a word with a third people, a colonist they hold sent home, or their help toward peace. Their head's regard decides whether they honor it.
- The peoples call in what the colony owes them when the world shows a need: medicine in a sickness, food in a famine, hands at their harvest, a roof for their homeless, the colony's word in a feud. Refused or left unanswered, they remember it.
- 14 conversation pieces and new letter lines speak of debts of honor, and the Almanac has a new page, diplomacy. The full view of what is owed each way comes with the region window's redesign, so it adds no tabs.

## What stands near

*4 November 2026, version 0.80.0*

- A raiders' camp's raid comes in from the camp's side, or from the nearest side that lets it in, and its letter says which.
- A raiders' camp breaks up once its leader is dead, taken or one of the colony, and the warlord's son no longer names or weakens a camp he can't lead.
- A trading post given up in hard times closes in the posts' history, and a family at the gate from a town given up is always of that town's own people.
- Visitors talk about what stands near the colony: a raiders' camp, a ruin within reach, a town given up lately by name, a town raised on old walls, and what is happening at their own town (60 new conversation pieces).
- Travelers say in conversation what brought them: a relic, a landmark, ruins, books, ore, a rush, grazing, the road or the distance.

- Fixed: "building work by my bed" no longer comes to colonists far from their beds, or for an unfinished building behind a wall that nobody is working on.
- It now comes only to someone in their own bedroom while a colonist is building in that room or on its walls, as its words say.

## The region's voices

*4 November 2026, version 0.79.0*

- Visitors now talk about the region's new places: trading posts, hamlets, raiders' camps their neighbours remember, a town being given up, a ruin settled again, the town that grew and the warlord's son, each people in the words of its own age.
- A visitor who has never met one of your colonists may already know their name from home, for good or ill, and say what for.
- The region's letters and news read differently from one time to the next: a post opening or closing, a hamlet's harvest and its asks, a town given up or settled again, a new town begun and built, each raid from a raiders' camp and how the camp ended.
- Travelers who came for the ore or the ruins talk while they work out there, and news of far towns starts in more than one way.
- A town given up, the town that grew and the warlord's son have more endings.

## More to say about the day

*4 November 2026, version 0.78.0*

- Colonists have more to say when a talk winds down: every topic has about fifteen new closing words, and many everyday replies now fit what the speaker is doing, at work, over a meal, late at night or out in the rain.
- Comforting someone sounds less the same: about a hundred new lines for the one who comes over, the one who is low and the last word, in every voice.
- Gifts carried over, stashes burned, the soup taken round and the dead carried out after a raid have more to say along the way.
- Forecasts, record weather, snowfall, sunrise, the birds, the colony's named beast and the herd's new leader get new lines, and some belong to their age: banked fires in a tribal or medieval colony, heaters, conduits and turbines in an industrial or spacer one.
- Gossip has three more ways to say what it was about for every deed, and the social log has more for a friend back on their feet, a visit to the sick, where someone grew up, a grumble about the menu and the light left burning at bedtime.
- In all, about 1,150 new lines, so the everyday ones you hear most come round about half as often.

## The region, checked over

*4 November 2026, version 0.77.1*

- Fixed: the game no longer slows down with the world map open: two of the region's hooks looked at every town many times a second, and now look only at a town under attack.
- Fixed: with raiders' camps switched off, a camp already set up in secret is no longer found, so no letter warns of raids that will never come.
- Fixed: a trading post no longer stays tied to a colony that moved far away by gravship or settled anew; the new home can get posts of its own.
- Fixed: settlers who could not found their hamlet because the place was taken no longer say their people are at war with the colony.
- Fixed: a settler you take prisoner, enslave or take in no longer vanishes from your cells when the caravan takes the others to their hamlet, and never founds it from a cell.

## A hamlet under your protection

*4 November 2026, version 0.77.0*

- Settlers may ask to found a hamlet a few days off under your protection: a holding after a year on your land, families of a town given up, or settlers from a struggling town. The letter shows the place, and you can choose another on the world map.
- Take them under your protection and they camp by the colony with their carts until a caravan of two grown colonists sets out for the place. They travel beside it, named in its panel, and light their first fires after a night there. Let them go alone, and a third are turned back.
- On the road the founding may bring a demand or an ambush, and the settlers take up arms beside your caravan.
- The hamlet is a town of their own people with its founder and families. It brings some of its harvest to your gate and asks for food, a doctor or tools in hard seasons. Refuse it twice in one season and it stops counting on you.
- In time it asks to swear fealty: as a Faction Territories vassal when that mod's vassalage is on, otherwise sworn to the colony. If its people turn on you first, its families choose whether to come to you or stay.
- Small towns look small: a hamlet, a trading post or a town in its first year has a smaller base with fewer defenders on its own map, through Vanilla Base Generation Expanded's layouts too.
- The region window has a hamlets page, This week shows settlers waiting at the gate, and two new settings sit in The region: Hamlets and Small towns' maps.

## The warlord's son, and those remembered

*4 November 2026, version 0.76.0*

- The warlord's son comes back 60 to 180 days after his father's story ends, in the way it ended: at the head of a raiders' camp if the colony killed his father, at the gate for his father, his grave, his weapon or a peace if the colony took him, as an exile asking shelter if others hunted him down, or with a few riders looking for work.
- A raiders' camp's leader is behind its walls when you attack it, and the peoples it troubled remember everyone who fought there.
- A hidden raiders' camp can be found by a visitor who passed its fires on the road, or by word from a neighbouring town.
- Peoples remember single colonists for good and ill: who broke a camp, fought beside them, traded at their post, killed one of theirs, or executed or cut open a prisoner of theirs. Their notables think better or worse of them, and their talk and letters name them.
- A trader won't deal with a colonist their people hold a death against: the trade option says why, and a caravan sends someone else to do the talking.
- After a friendly year, the other settlers' steading grows into a town: its leader heads it, you may name it, and its carter comes for a load for its first market.
- Fixed: a raiders' camp's leader could be let go while his camp still stood.

## Trading posts

*4 November 2026, version 0.75.0*

- Friends who trade with a far colony may open a trading post a few days off: a real town of theirs on the world map, named by their age (a trading camp, a market, a depot, a ground station), with its keeper and trader known by name.
- Caravans trade at a post as at any of their towns, and while it stands their trader caravans come half again as often. Goods on the road draw robbers, so the bandit road is likelier too.
- When relations sour, the post's keeper writes that it will close unless things mend. If they don't, it is packed up and leaves a ruin, and war closes it within a day.
- Raiders of a people at war with them may sack a post, and a post the colony razes is a wrong its people will not forget.
- The region window has a trading posts page, This week shows a post that may close, and visitors speak of the posts in 14 new conversations.
- A new setting, Trading posts, under The region. The Almanac's region page explains how posts work.

## Faith in the colony

*20 October 2026, version 0.8.0*

- People → Faiths (with Ideology): every faith in the colony on one page. It shows who believes what and how firmly, which faiths have no leader or moral guide here, what each faith asks that is weighing on someone right now, and who could sway whom. Each faith's card can call an evening of faith or name it the colony's faith, and the page explains the game's own tools.
- Talks about faith matter: a devout, well-liked colonist who comes out of a friendly talk well sways someone of another faith a little. The open-minded and friends are swayed more, the proud less. An argument makes both dig in, two of one faith steady each other, and a faith that dislikes preaching takes it badly.
- Evenings of faith: the council can call one from the faiths page or the calendar, or say yes when the devout ask. The faith's moral guide or most devout believer speaks, at its altar if one stands. Others choose whether to come, and a good evening can sway them and lifts those who came.
- A wavering colonist may ask to spend an evening with a devout believer of another faith, to learn about it. Granted, the two sit together, and it moves them.
- When the colony's faiths are at odds, the council decides: share the altar, give each faith its own corner, or name one faith the colony's. Each answer costs trust or certainty somewhere, and plays out on the map.
- New decree, one faith for the colony: you name the faith. The wavering are drawn to it, its believers speak of it more often and meetings open with a word from it, while everyone else trusts the council a little less until it's lifted.
- Crises of faith: losing someone for all the rites, being saved by someone of another faith, a faith's demands carried too long, or a brush with death can shake a colonist's faith for real. It shows as a turning point on their character page and in their journal.
- Every conversion is the game's own: someone takes a new faith when their certainty runs out, and Rimfolk tells you who brought them round. The advisor speaks up when the colony is badly split. Two Almanac pages, "faith in the colony" and "faith and the council", explain it, and the "Faith in the colony" setting turns it all off.

## People with a character of their own

*20 October 2026, version 0.7.0*

- Every colonist has a character on eight sides (warmth, nerve, temper, humour, trust, pride, openness, outlook). Traits, the Social skill and age set where they start, and life marks them, with 48 kinds of mark: a raid held or a nerve broken, a friend lost, being nursed through an illness, a craft mastered, a hungry season. The Story tab opens with it: eight bars, and what shaped them, with dates.
- Character shows in how they speak, in talk, gossip and the journal alike: a hothead snaps, a joker jokes, the cold are curt, the gloomy see the worst. It shows in what they do too: the warm have more warm moments, the hot-tempered more frictions, and the trusting confide their secrets sooner.
- Conversations: two colonists who are together now and then really talk, a short exchange over their heads, taking turns. They talk about the last raid, the council, the food, the newcomer, faith and 14 more topics, and what comes up follows the news. Everyone holds a view on each topic, which moves slowly with what happens and with who talks them round, so a talk ends in agreement, a friendly debate, a heated argument, teasing or comfort.
- Pairs keep shared memories: fighting side by side (only those who really stood together), a funeral, a feast, a caravan, a quarrel made up. Small mishaps become in-jokes once everyone's fine, funny for a while and then worn thin.
- Groups grow out of something real (friendship, the same work, a fight, the night hours, faith, where they came from) and keep their name: the hunters, the night shift, Hector's lot. Each has roles, ways and habits, takes newcomers in or closes ranks, falls out with other groups, and can split or merge. People → Bonds shows each one.
- Reputation and gossip: colonists notice what the others do (31 kinds of deed), and the rest hear it in gossip. Who tells it colours it, and the listener believes it as far as their nature lets them. What the colony knows becomes a name (brave or a coward, kind or cruel, reliable or idle), and a name leans votes and opinions.
- Traits can change, rarely, after a long stretch of the same kind of life. Raid after raid held can make someone steadfast, a year of graves a pessimist, many kindnesses kind, and a beaten addiction a teetotaler. A psychopath or a cannibal comes only from an extreme history, once in a colony's life each. A letter tells what led to the change, with dates.
- All of it gives opinion and company, not mood. "Personalities and social life" and "Traits can change" in the settings turn it off, and a new Almanac page, "Social life: how it fits together", follows two colonists through a year.
