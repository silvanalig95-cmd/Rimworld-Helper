# Rimfolk changelog

What changed in each update of Rimfolk, newest first, written for players. The same list is the "What's new" page at
the top of the Almanac in the game, and a letter shows the newest round once when you load a save after an update.

## The new stories, seen at a glance

*30 October 2026, version 0.36.0*

- "Rimfolk: check myself" (development mode) has a new part for the slower stories: the regulars the colony keeps up with, each with their visits, the bond and when they are due back, and whether one could come back now; the herd's leader, its line and whether its last season has been told, or what the herd still needs for a leader; the beast that stays, or the animal being watched toward a name, or the rest before the next one, and how many wild animals of size are on the land; the shiny rumour and its days left; and whether traveling trainers can come at all.
- The Overview page has a card, "The herd and the land": who leads the herd and of which line and generation, whether it is in its last season, the beast the colony watches and what it has made of it, a shiny rumour on the wind, and the neighbours settled for good.

## Two fixes from a read-through of the new rounds

*30 October 2026, version 0.35.1*

- The other settlers' "sell it to them at a fair price" could never be chosen: the sale had no one kind of thing to sell. It now goes as the famine's does: the food is carried out to the settlers at the gate, and they pay the usual price in silver.
- A camp a world event calls for (the road wardens come to build their post) arrived with no letter. It now gets its kind's own letter, as a camp the storyteller sends does, naming the colonist their people remember.

## A Pokémon that keeps coming, a shiny rumour, and traveling trainers

*30 October 2026, version 0.35.0*

- With PokéWorld, the beast that stays can be a wild Pokémon: one seen on the land three days running is named by a colonist, whoever sees it says so, and until it is fed or caught it gets into the stores now and then and makes off with a few of what it would eat. The council's question about it has its own answers: leave food out (carried out and left), keep the stores shut with a watch at the gate, let the namer try to catch it (a walk out to it with a ball at the belt; the throw is the player's, with PokéWorld's own balls, and the catch is told), let it be, or drive it off.
- A shiny rumour: a trainer at the gate has heard of a shiny Pokémon of some species seen in the region. Keep an eye out and the rumour holds for twenty days; while it does, the shiny may show itself on the colony's land, where it is named like any beast that stays, and the one who named it knows the rumour was true. Or it was a story after all.
- Traveling trainers, a new kind of camp (only with PokéWorld's Pokémon in play): two or three trainers of a friendly people with their Pokémon at their heels, who trade, teach training to whoever asks, and put on a friendly contest with the colony's best Pokémon as a show at their fire (a gathering there; the higher level is likelier to win, never surely; nobody is hurt). They come back like any remembered camp, and the score comes back with them: a rematch, with words for who won last time.
- Under the hood: a camp kind can be about Pokémon (possible only with PokéWorld's Pokémon in play), a camp's animals can be Pokémon of the roaming kinds, and a camp's lines know whether its people are back and how the shows have gone.

## The herd's year, and a beast that stays

*30 October 2026, version 0.34.0*

- The herd has a leader. With four head or more, the eldest of the many (and later the old leader's own blood) leads the colony's herd and is named by its keeper or the herdmaster, who speaks to it at the pens; a letter and the chronicle tell it. Its line is counted from then on: a calf born of the leader's blood is the second generation, the third, and so on, each new generation told the first time it is reached. When the leader grows old its last season is told and the keeper sits with it at the pens of an evening; when it dies the herd has lost its leader, and in a day or two follows one of its own line if there is one, else a new line begins.
- The first calf of each spring is a moment: a letter, a glad thought for the colony, and the keeper goes down to the pens to see it stand. The first foal ever is still kept on the calendar as before.
- A beast that stays: a wild wolf, bear, stag or the like seen on the colony's land three days running is given a name by a colonist (a child first, else someone with a love of animals), and from then on whoever sees it says so. A few days later the council is asked what to make of it, and again every so often while it stays: leave food out for it (carried out and left where it is, meat or fodder by its diet), keep everyone close with a watch at the gate, send the hunters, let the namer try to tame it, let it be, or drive it off with torches. After a season it is part of the land, and the story closes; hunted, tamed, driven off or moved on, it closes sooner, and the chronicle keeps how.

## Other settlers, the road over a year, and people left behind

*30 October 2026, version 0.33.0*

- Other settlers: a party of a friendly people founds a steading a few days from the colony, a new town on the world map, and their leader comes to the gate. Over nearly a year (the longest world event yet) the steading grows household by household, their hunters work the colony's woods, they ask for food in a hungry month or, slighted, set their traps on the colony's side of the stream. At the turning of the year the leader comes back in person: bind the two settlements as allies, invite the settlers to join the colony (they walk over and the steading is left empty), agree a boundary, or tell them to keep to their side. Warm answers and slights are weighed against each other all year, and the year ends in an alliance, in new colonists, in bad blood, in the settlers giving up, or in plain neighbours.
- The road, over a year: when "a new road" was brought past the colony's gate, it is used (merchants, travelers, a carter robbed on it, the people's road wardens at the gate); the wardens made welcome come back to ask for ground for a post, and their camp arrives to build it and then settles as the colony's neighbour, fighting whatever comes up the road; and at the post a crossroads town grows, which the colony may name after itself. Each follows the one before, so the road's story takes a year or two.
- People a world event leaves behind: in a plague, an old woman brings two children orphaned by it to the gate (take the children in, send them on with food, or not); deserters of a war, a holy war, a levy or a mercenary company camp out of sight at the edge of the colony's land, ask for steel to mend their gear, and can be asked to stand guard, taking up arms against whatever threatens the colony while their camp stands. Any camp asked to stand guard fights as a war party does.
- Under the hood: a world event can follow an earlier one by a flag it ended with, a beat can bring a camp of one kind or settle the camp that is here as the colony's neighbour, a letter's visitors can include children and bring the event's own person back to the gate, and taking in the children alone leaves whoever brought them free to go home.

## Faces that come back, visitors who notice, guests in the colony's stories

*30 October 2026, version 0.32.0*

- The tinker, the scholar, the doctor, the schoolteacher, the bonesetter, the wandering cook, the scribe, the players' storyteller and the kindred spirit are remembered when they go (up to five of them). From twenty days on, one of them comes back as "a familiar face at the gate", asks after the colonist they dealt with, and teaches a lesson in their trade for a welcome. Each warm answer deepens the thread, a cold one frays it, and someone not seen for half a year is forgotten. On their third visit or later, if the thread holds, it turns: they ask to stay for good, or ask their friend to come along on the road. The World page lists them under "Out there".
- Visitors notice the colony. A guest or a visitor at the gate says a line about a great hall, the art on the walls, the graves, a shrine, the walls and the guns, the many animals or the wide fields, once per thing seen. Word spreads: a colony whose halls, art, graves or walls travellers keep remarking on becomes famed for them (four new colony traits), and the tourists, the celebrity, the players, the notable guest, pilgrims, scholars and scribes come to it more often. "Travellers speak of..." on the World page says what the colony is known for.
- With Hospitality: a guest already staying can be the stranger of a story, about half the time when one of the guests fits. The old flame, the one asking to learn a trade, and the tinker or the teller who becomes a regular can be someone sleeping in the guest beds rather than a new face at the edge of the map; an answer that has them join hands them over through Hospitality, so their people are told the same way. The setting "Guests in the colony's stories" (Guests page) turns it off.

## Neighbours for good

*30 October 2026, version 0.31.0*

- A refugee family or a hermit whose stay went well asks, at their parting, to stay on the colony's land for good. Let settle, their camp becomes their home at the edge of the colony's land: they live off the land around it, trade (their stock is new every so often), come over to the gate every few days, ask and are asked (the same questions the council gets about any camp), and the council can still ask them to move on. Or take them all into the colony, camp and goods and children. One such neighbour at a time; camps still come and go beside them.
- Neighbours have their troubles: one of them falls ill now and then and asks for the colony's doctor; raiders on the land may kill one of them, and the rest may bury their dead and move on. Their unease eases with time rather than growing. The World page has their card under "Our neighbours".

## Camps that come back, a stay with a shape, and help in a fight

*30 October 2026, version 0.30.0*

- A camp that stayed and left in peace is remembered, with up to four of its people. A month or more later, when a camp could come again, it may be the same faces coming back (likelier after a warm stay or when the colony asked them back, less each time), with news of what became of them since. They settle in quicker, and the colonist who made a friend of them is glad to see them. The World page lists the camps the colony knows that may pass this way again.
- Every stay has a shape. A day and a half in, they ask something of the colony or do it a good turn. Midway through a longer stay trouble comes: one of the council's questions about them, or one of theirs falls ill and they ask for the colony's doctor, or their cart breaks and they ask for the makings. In the last day whoever speaks for them comes to say goodbye, and the colony can ask them back or send them off with meals for the road.
- Help in a fight. When enemies have been on the colony's land for an hour, a people at peace with the colony and at war with the attacker may send a war party to fight them: a letter asks whether to welcome them, hold them at the edge or send them away. They hunt the enemy alongside the colony's own; when the fight is over they make camp to rest a day or two, have their wounded seen to and sell what the fight left them, and their people remember that the colony owes them. A new threat while they rest sends them after it.

## The Decrees page organized, and why no camp has come

*30 October 2026, version 0.29.0*

- The Decrees page is organized rather than one long list. The decrees stand in their kinds (work, food and stores, order and health, rest and spirit, faith, the wider world), each kind with how many are in force or ready now and an arrow that folds it away. A row of filters shows only what you're after: ready now, in force, asked for, needed now, waiting. The order can be by kind, most wanted first, or soonest ready. The page remembers all of it.
- "At a glance" above the cards shows every decree as a small chip with a bar of how the colony stands on it (green for those who love or like it, grey for those who don't mind, orange and red for those who dislike or hate it) and a stripe for its state; a click goes to its card.
- Each card keeps all it showed, what the decree does, who loves, likes, dislikes and hates it, how long it has left, who asked and why it's needed, with the same support bar under the name and the portraits beside the effects rather than under them, so a card is shorter.
- When no camp is on the colony's land, the World page says why none has come (danger on the map, the weather, no friendly people, no room, the days since the last) or that the way is open and how many such mornings have passed, so a quiet stretch isn't a mystery.

## The planning pages drawn on the map

*30 October 2026, version 0.28.0*

- The Base page has a little map of the base on the left, like the defense page's: the rooms coloured by their use, the walls and doors, the rock, and every finding marked in its part's colour (orange for rooms and walks, red for doors, blue for stores, yellow for conduits and pipes), with a legend. Hover a finding on it to pick it out in the list, and a finding in the list to frame it on the little map; click to go there.
- While the Base page is open, its findings are drawn on the real map too: the rooms a finding is about outlined, the room or store they're far from in white with a line between and the distance at it, the doors, stubs and dead runs marked, each with a short word. The finding under the mouse is drawn in full. "Show on the map" keeps them drawn with the window closed.
- Hovering a step on the Plan page shows on the map what it points at; a step from the base review shows the review's own drawing of it. Hovering a batch on the Making page outlines the bench that would make it, and a harvest nothing uses outlines where it lies.
- Rearranging a room, each piece that moves has a line from where it stands to where it goes; the piece under the mouse in the window is drawn in full with its name at its new place.
- In the room planner, after "Find a good spot", the two other spots are outlined faintly and all three carry their number from the panel, so they can be compared on the map.
- On the ground plan, the room whose row the mouse is over is drawn in full while the others fade a little.

## What you could make

*29 October 2026, version 0.26.1*

- A Making page under Colony reads the recipes of every bench you have against the stock, so any mod's benches and recipes count by themselves. It lists what a batch could be made of right now with no bill for it, bench by bench: the staples first, and of clothes and weapons only the dearest. "Set a bill, until N" adds the bill to the bench.
- It names the harvests and finds you have a fair amount of that nothing here puts to its own use, and the bench that would (hops for a brewery, psychoid leaves for a drug lab, chunks for a stonecutter), with the research it waits on and a button to open it or to build the bench.
- Benches standing without bills are named at the bottom.
- An Almanac page, "a base that grows well": which rooms belong together, growing in rings, doors and corridors, the works under the floor, stores, and keeping it tidy, with the council's tool for each.

## The stores, looked over

*29 October 2026, version 0.26.0*

- The Base page now looks over the stores too: stockpiles that take everything (goods land in whichever is nearer), goods lying about that no store accepts, a bench far from the nearest store of what its bills ask for, one kind of goods accepted by stores in three rooms or more, and stores whose filter allows nothing. Shelves of any mod count like stockpiles.
- "Make a stockpile for them" lays out a stockpile by the store room (or the colony's middle) that takes exactly the goods nobody stores. "Make a store beside it" lays out a little stockpile by a bench, set to prefer its materials, so they're at hand. Both are ordinary zones afterwards.
- A stove or a butcher table gets no store beside it: raw food and corpses belong in the cold, and the kitchen's distance from the freezer is a finding of its own.

## The ground plan

*29 October 2026, version 0.25.1*

- A Ground plan button on the Plan page draws the next rooms on the map together: the rooms in the plan in your order, then the proposals, each at the spot that fits best with the ones before it kept clear. How the base grows if the plan is followed.
- Each room is outlined in its own colour with its name, and listed in the window with its reasons: the walls it shares, the rooms it goes with, the gate, the fields, the quiet. "Plan it here" opens the planner at that spot.
- A room whose size can't be built yet is drawn at the size that can; one with no spot near the colony is listed as such.

## The base, looked over

*29 October 2026, version 0.25.0*

- A Base page under Colony: once an hour, or when you ask, the council walks the base and lists what stands where it shouldn't, what leads nowhere, and what a click can put right, most pressing first.
- Rooms: a kitchen far from the freezer or the dining room, a workshop far from its store, a hospital far from the gate, a barn far from the fields; bedrooms or the hospital right by a workshop or a barn, a prison cell by the bedrooms; and long walks from the bedrooms to meals.
- Doors: one with the same room on both sides or no wall beside it, and a freezer door that opens into a warm room or the open air with no airlock between.
- Walls: short stubs left over from rebuilding, never a pillar on its own or a wall holding up a roof.
- Conduits and pipes: runs that lead nowhere, conduit joined to nothing at all, and the same for Dubs Bad Hygiene's and Vanilla Expanded's pipes. Something still being built beside them counts as their use.
- Doors, stubs, conduits and pipes have a "Take them down" button that gives the deconstruct orders. Every finding has Show me, and the findings are proposed as steps on the Plan page.

## Shorter walks, fuller workshops

*29 October 2026, version 0.24.1*

- Rearranging a room now minds the walk from the door: a bed goes to the near end of the room rather than the far, benches and tables lean toward the door too, and lamps and plant pots keep out of the way at the far end. The head still goes to a wall first.
- In a big workshop benches stand in rows facing the same way and, when the walls run out, back to back in islands with the work spots outward. No two work spots share a cell.
- A tool cabinet goes where it reaches the most benches, a dresser near the beds, a multi-analyzer by the research benches: anything that serves other pieces counts what it reaches. Shelves go near the benches, so the materials are at hand.
- A stool at a bench sits on the bench's work spot, facing it.

## Planning the base with the council

*29 October 2026, version 0.24.0*

- A Plan page under Colony: the colony's next steps in your order. Each hour the council proposes steps from what it watches (the priorities, the defense advice, an idle research bench); you take them into the plan, let them go, add your own, and order them. Each step opens what does it: the room planner, the defense plan, the research.
- The first step shows on This week and the council panel. A step is done when you say so, or when the need behind it has passed for a few hours.
- Where should this go: the room planner now suggests three spots for a room, each with its reasons. A hospital goes near the gate and away from the workshops, a barn by the fields, a kitchen beside the freezer and the dining room, a bedroom away from the noise. Click a spot to see it, or Here to place the plan there.
- Rearrange a room: click a built room and the council shows where it would put the furniture. Beds get their head to a wall and room to get in, bedside tables go by the beds, benches stand with their back to a wall and their work spot free, the table in the middle with the chairs facing it, shelves along the walls in runs, lamps and plant pots in the corners. The new places show on the map before you say go.
- The moves are carried out with the game's own reinstall orders, a few at a time as the cells free up, so nothing is set down in the way. Only what can be picked up moves; a mod that makes everything minifiable widens that. Walls, doors, pipes and conduits, plumbed fixtures, growing plants, filled graves and ritual pieces stay, and shelves with goods in them stay unless you tick the box.
- The window says why each piece goes where it goes, why the rest stays, and which pieces will want a conduit at their new place.

## Recruiting, spelled out

*29 October 2026, version 0.23.3*

- With Hospitality, the guest tab now says under its own lines whether a guest can be recruited and how: now, only by force, not yet, or never, and why.
- A checklist shows what it takes to have them join of their own will, each condition met or not: friends here, what ties at home ask for more, a reason to stay, room, and what they want of a home.
- It also explains Hospitality's own rules. A death acidifier keeps a guest from joining of their own will unless they're related to someone of the colony, and the titled can't be recruited by force, so a titled guest with an acidifier can't be recruited at all.
- It warns that recruiting someone with a death acidifier sets it off: their weapons and clothes dissolve and they're left with chemical burns.

## Slots kept just so

*29 October 2026, version 0.23.2*

- A reserved kind can be kept to its slots with "no more than that": one slot of meals, and never a second stack, however much room is left.
- "Per stack" sets the most of a kind in one stack on the shelf, like four meals. Haulers top it up to that and bring no more.
- "When" can keep a kind only while a patient lies in the shelf's room. With the beds empty it isn't kept there, and what's there is carried elsewhere.
- Each reserved kind now shows its slots, its limits and when it's kept on a second line in the shelf's list.

## More to reserve slots for

*29 October 2026, version 0.23.1*

- "Reserve a slot for..." opens a list with a search box: what's on the shelf now, groups such as any meal, and everything the shelf's storage settings allow, also what the colony has none of yet.
- Food and medicine groups also offer "best at hand": the slot goes to the best kind the colony has, a lavish meal before a fine one, and a better kind takes it over when it turns up.
- A kind can keep several slots, set with the minus and plus beside it. Each slot needs a stack of its own, so a fine meal and any meal reserved together keep two meal stacks.
- "Keep what's on it now" reserves a slot for each kind the shelf holds at the moment.

## Reserved slots, and drinks out of the freezer

*28 October 2026, version 0.23.0*

- A "Reserved slots" button on the colony's shelves keeps one slot for each kind you pick, an item or any of a group like meals, so the hospital shelf always has herbal, medicine and glitterworld medicine.
- Haulers fill the other slots with anything the shelf allows, and keep a slot free for each reserved kind the colony has on the map.
- When a reserved kind turns up and the shelf is full, a stack of what it holds most of is carried elsewhere to make room. It works with LWM's Deep Storage.
- Nobody stands in the freezer to drink a beer or have a smoke: with no seat close by, they take it to a table within 40 cells, or step out of the store room first.

## A tidier bottom bar

*28 October 2026, version 0.22.0*

- A "more" button, three dots just before the menu, holds the bottom bar's buttons you don't use. Right-click any button on the bar to put it away under it.
- Click the dots for the buttons put away: click one to open it, or the pin beside it to put it back on the bar. A button put away still opens with its key.
- "Arrange the bottom bar", in that list and in the settings (Interface), ticks every button on or off the bar. What you put away is the same in every save.
- Other mods' buttons can be put away too, and a mod's own switch for its button still works, as with Dubs Mint Menus. Switching "more" off puts every button back.

## Wedding plans

*28 October 2026, version 0.21.0*

- A couple engaged a couple of days or more plan the wedding with the council: a feast for the whole colony after the ceremony, a small wedding with a toast among their closest friends, or a wait of a fortnight or so for a calmer time.
- The wedding is the game's own ceremony, started on the next fine evening when both are ready, and the feast or the toast follows once they're married.
- A plan not kept within five days lapses with a note, and the game marries them in its own time as before.

## What the words say, done

*28 October 2026, version 0.20.2*

- Work parties do what their words say: rebuilding the woodpile fells trees and carries the logs to a heap by the gate, hauling stone moves the chunks, patching the roofs marks every unroofed room for roofing, and the clean-up after the butcher's work is at the butcher's table.
- The unflattering likeness is a sculpture its maker really made, and comes up only while one stands in the colony. Smashed, it goes to rubble when the visit to it is over, and the one it mocked sweeps up the pieces.
- Old bones reburied get turned earth and a stone at the head, the evening of headstones has the stonecutter cut and set a stone at every grave one after another, and the buried journal lies under a flat stone where the walk ends.
- Smudge fires burn along the field edges against the locusts, and against the marsh lights the watch on the move carries torches after dark and the lanterns hang at the doors.
- The camp's lost child is really out there with one of the camp's beasts, and comes home as the answer says: found where the search ends, found by their own people, or walking in toward the bell before first light.
- Soup rounds carry meals out from the stores and hand them over, the children run their race in laps round the fields, and the mushrooms for the mushroom supper are picked, cooked and eaten.
- The story seeds are sown in a small patch of a crop the colony grows, with a fence marked out round it. The hill's pilgrims hand over their silver, eat and bed down by the wall, and the robbed merchant eats and sleeps by the gate.
- Fixes: a friendly talk now and then runs to a fourth line, so its parting words can be said, and a confession or a secret to be confided that is waiting for the right moment is kept over a save and load.

## Ties weigh, they don't bar

*28 October 2026, version 0.20.1*

- What ties a guest to home no longer bars them from joining. Each tie asks for more friends in the colony than Hospitality does: two for a partner or young children there, one for parents or a faith held fast.
- Each reason to stay beyond the first makes up for one of those friends, so a guest in love here with a very good stay needs fewer.
- The Recruit button's tooltip counts the friends out, and where the game allows it Hospitality's own friends bar shows the higher number too.

## Guests who would stay

*28 October 2026, version 0.20.0*

- With Hospitality, a guest joins of their own will only when they would. Otherwise the guest tab offers only to recruit them by force, and the Recruit button's tooltip says what's missing.
- A partner or young children at home, their parents while they're young, their people's leader or a faith held fast hold a guest back. Love or family here, hard times at home or a very good stay give a reason to stay.
- There has to be room for one more: a free bed, a room of their own for the greedy, jealous and titled, and four days of food.
- What they want of a home goes by their passions and nature: a passionate cook wants a decent kitchen, a grower fields to work, a gourmand fine meals.
- Taking someone costs their people by who they are: less with no family among them, more with family there, twice for their leader's family.
- Before a guest is recruited from the guest tab, a colonist who objects has their say at the council: someone who can't stand them, the devout, or the cook when food is short.
- Weeks after someone joins, their family may walk in to take them home. Let them go together, send silver, or keep them at a price in goodwill and longing.

## What a life goal leaves behind

*28 October 2026, version 0.19.0*

- A life goal reached is toasted that evening: those close to them gather at the table, and a friend raises a glass.
- It always brings an inspiration that fits the goal: surgery for a healer, taming for a tamer, a work frenzy otherwise.
- The skill it was about never slips again and comes a little faster, for goals already reached too.
- A younger colonist with a passion for that skill spends some hours learning at their side.
- The Story tab remembers them as the one who did it, and the colony tells it round in gossip.

## Kept at a price, and faith that holds

*28 October 2026, version 0.18.4*

- Keeping a colonist who asked to move on now always costs: trust, by how much they wanted to go, and a longing that grows over weeks until what they wanted comes to them.
- A relative can be sent for to come and live here, for silver and a little goodwill. A partner asked to join costs goodwill too.

## Habits that grow from a life

*23 October 2026, version 0.11.0*

- Each quirk on a colonist's Story tab, under Heart, now says how it came, what it does and how long it lasts: "can't stand a mess (from all the cleaning). Cleaning speed +10%, mood -2 in a messy room, tidies up in their free time. Stays while they keep at cleaning." One that does nothing to play says so.
- Habits come each in their own way. Most grow out of what someone does: Rimfolk notes once an hour what each colonist is doing (their kind of work, early mornings, late nights, time outdoors) and remembers about three weeks of it. - The superstitions that were only flavour do something small: knocking on wood gladdens after a fight come through unhurt, talking to the dead comforts near the graves, counting everything warns when the food won't last the week, salting the doorway keeps nightmares away, and a sleep talker's roommate sleeps a little worse (a lover finds it endearing).

With Vanilla Traits Expanded, its neat and animal lover are given instead. Tough and fast walker can now be earned too, never lost.
- The character page shows "where life is taking them": the trait changes a colonist is heading toward, with how many of the things that lead there have happened. Hover one for the list.

## Talk in their own voices

## Faith in the colony

*20 October 2026, version 0.8.0*

- People → Faiths (with Ideology): every faith in the colony on one page. It shows who believes what and how firmly, which faiths have no leader or moral guide here, what each faith asks that is weighing on someone right now, and who could sway whom. Each faith's card can call an evening of faith or name it the colony's faith, and the page explains the game's own tools.
- Talks about faith matter: a devout, well-liked colonist who comes out of a friendly talk well sways someone of another faith a little. The open-minded and friends are swayed more, the proud less. An argument makes both dig in, two of one faith steady each other, and a faith that dislikes preaching takes it badly.
- Evenings of faith: the council can call one from the faiths page or the calendar, or say yes when the devout ask. The faith's moral guide or most devout believer speaks, at its altar if one stands. Others choose whether to come, and a good evening can sway them and lifts those who came.
- A wavering colonist may ask to spend an evening with a devout believer of another faith, to learn about it. Granted, the two sit together, and it moves them.
- When the colony's faiths are at odds, the council decides: share the altar, give each faith its own corner, or name one faith the colony's. Each answer costs trust or certainty somewhere, and plays out on the map.
- New decree, one faith for the colony: you name the faith. The wavering are drawn to it, its believers speak of it more often and meetings open with a word from it, while everyone else trusts the council a little less until it's lifted.
- Crises of faith: losing someone for all the rites, being saved by someone of another faith, a faith's demands carried too long, or a brush with death can shake a colonist's faith for real. It shows as a turning point on their character page and in their journal.
- Every conversion is the game's own: someone takes a new faith when their certainty runs out, and Rimfolk tells you who brought them round. The advisor speaks up when the colony is badly split. Two Almanac pages, "faith in the colony" and "faith and the council", explain it, and the "Faith in the colony" setting turns it all off.

## People with a character of their own

*20 October 2026, version 0.7.0*

- Every colonist has a character on eight sides (warmth, nerve, temper, humour, trust, pride, openness, outlook). Traits, the Social skill and age set where they start, and life marks them, with 48 kinds of mark: a raid held or a nerve broken, a friend lost, being nursed through an illness, a craft mastered, a hungry season. The Story tab opens with it: eight bars, and what shaped them, with dates.
- Character shows in how they speak, in talk, gossip and the journal alike: a hothead snaps, a joker jokes, the cold are curt, the gloomy see the worst. It shows in what they do too: the warm have more warm moments, the hot-tempered more frictions, and the trusting confide their secrets sooner.
- Conversations: two colonists who are together now and then really talk, a short exchange over their heads, taking turns. They talk about the last raid, the council, the food, the newcomer, faith and 14 more topics, and what comes up follows the news. Everyone holds a view on each topic, which moves slowly with what happens and with who talks them round, so a talk ends in agreement, a friendly debate, a heated argument, teasing or comfort.
- Pairs keep shared memories: fighting side by side (only those who really stood together), a funeral, a feast, a caravan, a quarrel made up. Small mishaps become in-jokes once everyone's fine, funny for a while and then worn thin.
- Groups grow out of something real (friendship, the same work, a fight, the night hours, faith, where they came from) and keep their name: the hunters, the night shift, Hector's lot. Each has roles, ways and habits, takes newcomers in or closes ranks, falls out with other groups, and can split or merge. People → Bonds shows each one.
- Reputation and gossip: colonists notice what the others do (31 kinds of deed), and the rest hear it in gossip. Who tells it colours it, and the listener believes it as far as their nature lets them. What the colony knows becomes a name (brave or a coward, kind or cruel, reliable or idle), and a name leans votes and opinions.
- Traits can change, rarely, after a long stretch of the same kind of life. Raid after raid held can make someone steadfast, a year of graves a pessimist, many kindnesses kind, and a beaten addiction a teetotaler. A psychopath or a cannibal comes only from an extreme history, once in a colony's life each. A letter tells what led to the change, with dates.
- All of it gives opinion and company, not mood. "Personalities and social life" and "Traits can change" in the settings turn it off, and a new Almanac page, "Social life: how it fits together", follows two colonists through a year.
