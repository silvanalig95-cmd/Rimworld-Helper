# Rimfolk changelog

What changed in each update of Rimfolk, newest first, written for players. The same list is the "What's new" page at
the top of the Almanac in the game, and a letter shows the newest round once when you load a save after an update.

## Habits that grow from a life

*23 October 2026, version 0.11.0*

- Each quirk on a colonist's Story tab, under Heart, now says how it came, what it does and how long it lasts: "can't stand a mess (from all the cleaning). Cleaning speed +10%, mood -2 in a messy room, tidies up in their free time. Stays while they keep at cleaning." One that does nothing to play says so.
- Habits come each in their own way. Most grow out of what someone does: Rimfolk notes once an hour what each colonist is doing (their kind of work, early mornings, late nights, time outdoors) and remembers about three weeks of it. A habit of work never comes without its work, and the more of it, the likelier. A few are simply who someone is and come to anyone they fit.
- Seven new habits: steady hands (doctoring), measures twice (building), sharpens every blade (smithing), knows every trail (hunting), carries more than they should (hauling), keeps late hours (being up after eleven), and can't sit still (by nature).
- A habit lived doesn't fade while it is. Lived on sixty days, it takes root and its knack and good mood grow a little (cleaning 15% faster instead of 10%, say).
- Every habit shows on the map: a moment of its own in their free time (tidying a dirty room, watching the sun come up, looking for pretty stones, looking in on the sick, wishing someone a happy birthday) or a word now and then at the work.
- The superstitions that were only flavour do something small: knocking on wood gladdens after a fight come through unhurt, talking to the dead comforts near the graves, counting everything warns when the food won't last the week, salting the doorway keeps nightmares away, and a sleep talker's roommate sleeps a little worse (a lover finds it endearing).
- A habit lived for months grows into a trait: harder work, kindness, night owl, or one of six traits of Rimfolk's own that are only ever earned (tidy, green thumb, born cook, animal friend, healer's hands, trail-wise). With Vanilla Traits Expanded, its neat and animal lover are given instead. Tough and fast walker can now be earned too, never lost.
- The character page shows "where life is taking them": the trait changes a colonist is heading toward, with how many of the things that lead there have happened. Hover one for the list.

## Sandbags further from the gate

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.16*

- The gate with sandbag lines put its sandbags three cells straight behind the gate, which a defense line's own review calls too close: the first raider through was on the shooters at once, and raiders outside could see the nearest of them through the gap. Its sandbags now stand five cells back and off to either side of the way through, like the sandbag line's, with three firing positions behind each.
- The perimeter wall's sandbags moved the same way, five cells back from its inner gate. A plan that closes a gap can no longer bring cover closer than a line's review allows, and the advice for a gate with too little cover says how far back to put it.

## Drawn to another ideoligion

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.15*

- Each colonist now leans toward the other ideoligions around them, shown as bars under their certainty on the Culture tab, growing, fading or holding. Believers they like, love or are family to pull hardest, and a leaning fades when nothing feeds it.
- The strongest leaning wears on their certainty against the game's own daily recovery. When it passes their certainty, they take that ideoligion up through the game's own conversion, and the one they leave becomes a leaning they may drift back to. A leaning passing half is told once.
- The one-ideoligion decree now draws everyone of another ideoligion, not only the wavering, who were almost never there because content colonists stay at full certainty. With friends among its believers, a content colonist comes round in about a month; a lone believer of a minority never pulls the rest over.
- Talks, evenings, crises and council answers that sway someone toward an ideoligion feed their leaning toward it as well.

## Messages you can read

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.14*

- The game's messages at the top left are drawn over the on-screen panels, and were hard to read over a panel's own lines. A message that touches a panel now sits in a box of its own (a dark fill with a thin light edge, behind the whole line), fading with the message; over the open map it looks as it always did.
- A decree used lately says on its card in how many days it's safe to enact again, and "used once" or "used 3 times" instead of "1 time(s)".

## Talk in their own voices

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.13*

- Everything colonists say over their heads was reread one line at a time: council talk, wish asks and reminders, everyday talk, gossip, visitors' lines and group talk. Each makes its point in its first sentence, and a speech bubble now shows the whole line when it fits instead of only its first sentence.
- The social log reads like the game's own. Moments, frictions, conversations and small social events are one sentence about what was said, at any hour and wherever the two stand. Nobody tidies a grave, fetches a meal or walks the walls when the game shows no such thing, and a friendly contest's opening line no longer gives away a different result from the winner's.
- Conversations hang together: replies answer whatever was said, closing lines fit either side, and wish talk fits every wish it serves (health and healing, the lost and the dead, animals, safety, what children need).
- Calendar letters, eulogies, the annals, group journals, personality marks, trait changes and quirks are written for the moment they come: no grave before a burial, no rite claimed that wasn't held, no children, pens or walls the colony may not have, and no sentence opening on a lower-case name.
- A beast or pet nobody named is "the warg", not "warg", and talk about a disaster says "the cold snap" instead of "Cold snap" in the middle of a sentence.
- Only a joyful ritual (a feast, a dance, a festival) is talked about afterwards as a party; an execution or a sacrifice no longer is. A Pokémon's evolution is congratulated aloud instead of turning up as a quote with no speaker in the log.
- Moods from small social events hold for every event that gives them ("made fun of", "made to feel small"), and British spellings that had slipped in are American now.

## A lesson at the table

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.12*

- Words said to a gathering (a lesson, a story, an apology, a letter read out) are no longer a party where everyone wanders about. The speaker stands at the head of the table, the listeners sit in the chairs round it facing them, and the rest stand close by; with no table near, they stand in a ring round the speaker.
- A visiting scholar who is asked to teach now comes to the table and gives the lesson there, instead of walking about while the colony follows, and the lesson is held at the dining table when there is one.
- What a lesson teaches goes to those who sat through it, when it's over, and the closing message says who learned what ("Hector, Harper and 3 others learned from it: intellectual +800 xp each"). The scholar's lesson teaches 800 xp instead of 350, since it no longer goes to everyone.

## A clean sweep that cleans

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.11*

- During a clean sweep, everyone who can clean does it before their other work, even those not set to clean, until the home area is clean. The decree's card says so.
- Fires, the sick and wounded, children, prisoners and cooking still come before the scrubbing, and so do sleep, meals and washing. It's one of the deeper game hooks: with them off, the work order is your own.

## Rest for the wounded

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.10*

- Colonists still healing from a wound or an illness now like a clean sweep (unless they're lazy) and resting when ill (unless they're industrious or tough), and the Decrees page shows them among those who like it.
- When many are hurt (two colonists or more, and at least a third of the colony, with real wounds still healing, not a scratch), colonists petition the council for either decree. Before, resting when ill was only asked for when someone was ill, and a clean sweep only when the colony was dirty.

## Diaries and dreams

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.9*

- Every dream was reread and many retold: some are a bare image, some a half-memory or a feeling that stays into the morning, fewer start with "I dreamt" or end with waking up, and a dream of a raid or an evolution never reads as if it really happened.
- Every journal line was reread on its own. Entries are shorter and uneven, in each colonist's own voice, and true to the moment they're written: no funeral before anyone is buried, a blessing told by the one who received it, no snow promised in a dry winter.
- Journal entries go by the hour the moment happened (a loss, a fight, an arrival, an everyday thing), so night and morning lines show when they should, instead of always or never depending on where the colony lies.
- Comrades from a fight who drift apart now each write a line about it; before, nothing was written at all. Two lines about kept promises now show, and the yearly day of a fight where an old enemy came back is remembered as such.
- When a nemesis dies to a trap, a turret or an illness, the one they hurt no longer writes about thanking themselves for it.
- The dates on this page are corrected: rounds from 0.8.0 on had drifted up to a week ahead of when they came out.

## Plain materials

*23 October 2026, version 0.10.8*

- What colonists plan on their own (a light or a bed for themselves, a personal touch in their room, a floor, a grave, the thing a wish of theirs asks for) is only ever of plain materials: wood, stone blocks or bone. They no longer spend the colony's steel, silver, gold, plasteel, cloth or components; something that needs those isn't planned at all.
- What the council plans for a promised wish is made of wood or stone blocks too when it can be, and of the colony's finer materials only when nothing plainer will do.

## Moods in their own words

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.7*

- About 1,600 moods were reread one at a time. Each now says what happened in this colony with one concrete thing from the moment ("I still get up at feeding time"), and holds for every answer, variant or risk that gives it, including half-hearted customs and verdicts the council reuses.
- Moods no longer claim what the game didn't do: a sold animal leaves with its buyer instead of running loose, a reburied grave doesn't moralize, and a story's mood fits however the story went. About 35 mood names were clarified or shortened to fit the mood list.
- The "what helps" tips were checked against the game's own numbers (how long things last, who is spared, what really helps) and use the interface's own names.

## A health panel at a glance

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.6*

- The health panel speaks the Health tab's language: each condition carries the game's own icon (tended well, tended poorly, needs tending, the blood drop), a tiny bar for how far a wound has healed or how an illness's immunity race stands, and its tending in red, yellow or green.
- Conditions are small chips that wrap onto a second line instead of being cut off ("Bruise ×2", then "+N"), each with its own tooltip. Each colonist gets a small health bar by the name, a coloured word (healing green, getting worse orange, bleeding red) and their pain on hover. "Icons and bars" in the panel's options gives the plain look back.

## Panels that stay on the screen

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.5*

- A panel taller than the screen (the inventory with every row shown) now stops at the game's button bar and scrolls: the mouse wheel over it, or the thin bar at its right edge, which you can drag or click above and below. The inventory's search box scrolls with it.
- The panels' options show all of their settings again. The inventory's were cut off after its first look, so its rows, order, pins and the rest couldn't be set.
- The inspect tabs over the colonist's pane (Story, Mood, Gear and the rest) take your clicks again where a panel lies behind them, also when another mod such as RimHUD draws them wider or from the left edge.

## Turn the line

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.4*

- Picking a spot for a new defense line by hand can now turn the line: the rotate keys (or the game's rotate buttons above the menu) turn which way it faces from the way the helper worked out, and the cursor says which way it faces and how to turn it.
- Placing a defense plan by hand ("Place it myself") shows the same rotate buttons beside its keys.

## Wishes in their own words

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.3*

- Every wish, life goal, decree and council ambition was reread line by line. Colonists ask in more ways (a hint, a demand, a bargain, a complaint), the cold voice bargains or unsettles instead of reciting costs, and thanks can be a single word.
- Wish lines fit every way a wish comes true: thanks are said the moment it's granted, a lapsed wish no longer claims the trouble passed or that the council said no, and a room of one's own fits someone with no bed as well as someone in the barracks.
- Life goals read right in a letter ("let go of an old shame", "find faith again"), each line fits whatever set the goal off, and the letter when a goal or an ambition is reached is a plain sentence about who did it and how long it took.
- Decree lines hold whether they're said in a petition, when the decree is declared or while it runs, and the curfew's grumbling counts the hours.

## The people in the letters

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.2*

- The people a world event's letter speaks of are there. The rider waits by the gate until you answer and takes what you send; the family asking for shelter waits with their dog or goat, and the ones you take in are the ones you met. Only people at peace with the colony come, only when it's safe, and they go home if the weather turns on them.
- Guests given a night stay it, by the wall or in a corner of the dining room on a bitter night, and go on at first light. Drovers graze their herd on the far meadow overnight, and mourners, a clan's band or a lord's progress pass along the road.
- More of what answers only told now happens: a night watch on the walls, a shearing day that shears the herd, a grieving colonist sitting at the grave, the long road told by the fire, a family supper at one table, and visitors shown round, taught from and sung with.
- What answers name now exists: the apology carving and a repaid debt's gift are real sculptures signed by their maker, guests who cut wood leave real stumps and logs, the children's trees are saplings in the ground, and a staked-out room appears as building plans.
- A visitor's dog only comes up when there is one, the traveling healer only for an old injury, and the animal in decline only when it's old or ill. A few event lines that could never show now can.

## Goods carried by hand

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.1*

- Goods that events move between the colony and the world now move on the map. Sell corn to a village and their porters walk in some hours later, go to your stockpile and carry it off; the silver comes back with a runner. A big load brings one of their pack animals. Gifts, payments, rewards and bought goods are carried in and set down by the gate.
- Errands to the gate are handed to someone who really waits there. A prisoner you sell is led off by the buyer's riders, a runaway animal by its owners' herder, lost cargo and a crashed drone by their owners, who pay the finder's fee on the spot.
- Letters about a venture, a market or a fair now arrive with the goods. Couriers come by day, only when the map is safe, and say a word or two in their own people's way; if nobody can come, or the setting "Goods and people come and go on the map" is off, everything happens at once as before. The Almanac page "goods carried by hand" explains it.

## Every event in its own voice

*22 October 2026, version 0.10.0*

- About 1,250 events, questions and stories were rewritten one at a time: storyteller events, world situations, council dilemmas, encounters, personal stories, missions, camps and secrets. Each now has the length its moment deserves, from a single line for a passing sight to a full letter for a disaster, and opens its own way.
- Where the story gives a reason, people speak for themselves: a burned town's shaky message, a toll-keeper's terms chalked on a board, a plague doctor's printed form passed through the gate, an officer's summons, a hermit's six words, a reeve's hurried letter, a child's petition in charcoal.
- Some events now arrive as what they are: the list found under a hoarder's bed, an elder's ledger read out column by column, a funeral plan in a colonist's own hand, a warlord's price, a work song, a playbill nailed by the road.
- Outcomes say what happened instead of how everyone felt about it, and stop on an image rather than a moral. Each camp's people sound like themselves: terse hunters, a tired refugee family, cocky prospectors, theatrical players, precise surveyors.
- When a personal story ends, its letter reads as one short tale (an opening, a turn, an ending) instead of a list of stages.
- About sixty texts that contradicted the game were fixed: outcomes that told of days passing when they show at once, letters that showed an unfilled place name or began a sentence in lowercase, gifts said to be at the gate when they land elsewhere, carts, oxen and goats that never appear, and a default answer that claimed the council had ruled when it never answered.

## Panels that read right

*22 October 2026, version 0.9.9*

- The health panel's summary line no longer runs words together ("Bruisetended 27%"): each condition and how it stands are spaced apart again, and so are the pieces of the mood line in the Needs tab. Something that can kill but isn't a wound or an illness (a gene's chemical dependency, hypothermia) shows once it's a tenth of the way, not from the first hour, and says "towards deadly".
- Click "and 15 more" under any panel to show every row; "Rows before 'and more'" in the panel's options sets the limit again.

## The game's own words

*21 October 2026, version 0.9.8*

- Rimfolk now calls the game's belief system what the game does: the ideoligion, and how firmly someone holds it, their certainty. The People page shows "Ideoligions", the setting is "Ideoligions in the colony", the council calls an ideoligion evening or names one the colony's ideoligion, and the colonist bar warns of wavering certainty. It was always the game's own Ideology system, not a religion of Rimfolk's.
- Colonists still speak of their faith in their own voice: in what they say, in their journals and in a crisis of faith. The council's buttons on an ideoligion's card take a second row when they don't fit side by side.

## A defense helper that reads your line

*21 October 2026, version 0.9.7*

- The defense helper knows a pass your line already holds: a gap just in front of it, or the same pass measured further out, now reads "covered by the south-west pass" and is no longer offered as a new line. Your gate, the doors in the line's wall and a door across its gap count as its way through even when shut, so a closed gate never makes it look for another way in; the line's card says to hold the gate open during a raid.
- "Plan this step" lays the trap corridor on the raiders' own route to your gate, in front of the line, using the rock for its sides where it can, and says why it chose that spot, or why nothing fits and what would make room.
- Before it proposes walls, the helper checks whether they would send raiders another way, and offers to leave the corridor's sides open or to close the other gap first. It reads the map again when you build or take down walls, doors, traps or cover near a line ("Survey again" does it at once), and the line cards say when raiders' way in has moved. A new Almanac page explains how raiders pick their way.

## Panels in the background

*21 October 2026, version 0.9.6*

- The on-screen panels now sit beneath the game's own interface: windows, the colonist bar, letters, alerts and the inspect tabs draw over them, and your hover and clicks reach the game there. They stay visible behind open windows instead of disappearing ("hide them under big windows" is off now, still there as an option).
- An Inventory panel shows what you have with the game's own icons, in four looks to try: classic, grouped by category, an icon grid and essentials only. Pin what matters, search, see the change since about this time yesterday, and click to jump to the stacks. The game's own resource list steps aside while it shows (a setting keeps both).
- The health panel shows how each condition stands: tended and how well, how far a wound has healed and roughly when it will be gone, an illness's immunity race and whether it's being won. Right-click any alert in the Alerts panel to hide it there; the wishes the Council panel already shows are hidden by default.

## Fungus that lasts

*21 October 2026, version 0.9.5*

- The fungus meal is part fungus again, the rest any raw food, so the fungus lasts longer: a meal with some fungus in it is all a fungus diet asks. Stoves get a "cook 4 fungus meals" bill too, like the game's own.
- Someone whose faith allows only one kind of food (a fungus or insect diet) no longer tires of the same meal: they have no choice to tire of.

## A wedding you can call

*21 October 2026, version 0.9.4*

- The wedding wish no longer depends on the game choosing a day. Promise it and the council holds the wedding, the game's own ceremony, as soon as both are ready and the day is calm, trying again every few hours until it happens.
- The wish says so: the game still marries an engaged couple on its own sooner or later, and a marriage spot gives the ceremony a place.

## Alerts, health and work on screen

*20 October 2026, version 0.9.3*

- Your alerts in a panel you can move: the game's and every mod's, coloured by urgency, with the game's explanation on hover and its click to jump to them. The game's own list steps aside while the panel shows (a setting keeps both).
- Health panel: only what will pass or needs seeing to (wounds, bleeding with the time left, anything untended, illnesses and whether they're winning), never scars, implants, chronic conditions, lost limbs or magical effects. Work and idle panel: what everyone is doing, who's idle and for how long, food, rest and recreation bars, and work nobody is set to do.
- The panels always stay behind other windows. The game's own tooltip makes way for the hover card, which now puts long advice on its own line instead of cutting it, the mood trend's labels no longer overlap, and the "drag to move" tooltip is gone.

## A fungus diet that holds

*20 October 2026, version 0.9.2*

- The fungus meal is now made from fungus and nothing else. When a colonist's faith requires fungus, Rimfolk makes a "Fungus diet" food policy once (fungus meals, raw fungus, insect jelly and insect meat only) and tells you who needs it. A policy that still allows simple meals or raw vegetables is what keeps such a colonist throwing up.
- The small frictions between colonists (a borrowed thing not returned, a taken seat, a joke that fell flat) now say what they are and that they fade in a day or so, instead of pointing to the council.

## Families that really come

*20 October 2026, version 0.9.1*

- World events that bring a family now bring one: a couple who share a name, sometimes with a child, and the dog or goat the text speaks of, tame and the colony's. Before, "the family" could be one person, and the dog never came. "The exiles" are always more than one, and no line assumes the colony already has a dog.
- "The favorite" only comes when the same colonist has had a second wish granted since the other's own last one, within 30 days, while the other's request still waits. One wish granted is just good news.

## What matters, on screen

*20 October 2026, version 0.9.0*

- On-screen panels: a mood board (every colonist's mood with their own break lines, where it's heading and the biggest thing dragging them down, most at risk first), a mood trend graph over 3 or 7 days, the colony's vitals (food days, medicine, power, the weather ahead, days since a raid, research) and the council at a glance (wishes, promises, questions, decrees, the next day or meeting, trust). Click through to the right place.
- Every panel can be dragged, snaps to edges and other panels, and has its own options: rows and columns, sort, who's listed, compact or full, size and background. Toggle all of them with the button among the game's icons at the bottom right or a key, and find every option under Interface → On-screen panels.
- Hover card: rest the mouse on a colonist on the map to see their mood and the two or three things that matter most right now, from bleeding and breaks to the biggest mood drag and what helps. Delay, modifier key, position and lines can all be set.
- Badges on the colonist bar: a ring warms from amber to red as someone nears a break, and small badges mark someone about to leave, a wish waiting long, strain near breaking, wavering faith or a promise at risk. Hover for what and how much, click to go straight there. Each kind, the ring, size, corner and count can be set.
- One shared ranking puts what matters most first everywhere: life and limb, then what's about to become a problem, then what's worth knowing; the biggest and most fixable first, and "and 3 more" rather than silently cutting a line.

## Moods at a glance

*20 October 2026, version 0.8.2*

- The Mood tab opens compact: moods sorted into "You can fix", "Will pass", "Faith and who they are" and "Lifting", with chips for each group's total. Each mood is one line with its size and what helps ("Build a dining table") or how long it has left. Hover for the full explanation, click for its card with the advisor, council and Almanac buttons. "Details" brings back the old cards.
- The game's own Needs tab gets one line above the moods with the same totals, so you can see how much is in your hands and how much will pass. Click it to open the Mood tab.
- Every mood hint now has a short action, and the council's Moods page sorts the colony's moods the same way.

## Social and Culture in the colonist's tabs

*20 October 2026, version 0.8.1*

- Rimfolk now sits in the game's own Social tab: a column beside the relations list with a colonist's bonds, groups and role, what people say about them, shared memories and in-jokes, recent talks and the secrets the colony knows. Hovering someone in the list adds a line on what is between the two of them. The "Rimfolk in the Social tab" setting turns it off.
- A new Culture tab next to Social: faith with a certainty bar and any role, the views they hold, how they feel about the colony's ways, what they think of each decree in force, and where they come from.
- The Story tab is lighter and keeps who they are and what they've lived through. The council's People page still shows everything.

## Faith in the colony

*20 October 2026, version 0.8.0*

- People → Faiths (with Ideology): every faith in the colony on one page. It shows who believes what and how firmly, which faiths have no leader or moral guide here, what each faith asks that is weighing on someone right now, and who could sway whom. Each faith's card can call an evening of faith or name it the colony's faith, and the page explains the game's own tools.
- Talks about faith matter: a devout, well-liked colonist who comes out of a friendly talk well sways someone of another faith a little. The open-minded and friends are swayed more, the proud less. An argument makes both dig in, two of one faith steady each other, and a faith that dislikes preaching takes it badly.
- Evenings of faith: the council can call one from the faiths page or the calendar, or say yes when the devout ask. The faith's moral guide or most devout believer speaks, at its altar if one stands. Others choose whether to come, and a good evening can sway them and lifts those who came.
- A wavering colonist may ask to spend an evening with a devout believer of another faith, to learn about it. Granted, the two sit together, and it moves them.
- When the colony's faiths are at odds, the council decides: share the altar, give each faith its own corner, or name one faith the colony's. Each answer costs trust or certainty somewhere, and plays out on the map.
- New decree, one faith for the colony: you name the faith. The wavering are drawn to it, its believers speak of it more often and meetings open with a word from it, while everyone else trusts the council a little less until it's lifted.
- Crises of faith: losing someone for all the rites, being saved by someone of another faith, a faith's demands carried too long, or a brush with death can shake a colonist's faith for real. It shows as a turning point on their character page and in their journal.
- Every conversion is the game's own: someone takes a new faith when their certainty runs out, and Rimfolk tells you who brought them round. The advisor speaks up when the colony is badly split. Two Almanac pages, "faith in the colony" and "faith and the council", explain it, and the "Faith in the colony" setting turns it all off.

## Easier to keep spirits up

*20 October 2026, version 0.7.2*

- Hard times: as a colonist nears breaking, Rimfolk's own small letdowns (a joke that fell flat, being talked about, a sticking door) weigh less, down to half at their break line, and its everyday pressures down to three quarters. It eases back as they recover. Low spirits no longer double the nightmares (sweet dreams still come rarely).
- On the normal setting, everyday pressures and the colony's small frictions press at 85% of their old strength. The same work starts to wear after 4 days instead of 3, and a passion for the work takes it a step lighter.
- Good moods add up a little faster: the third, fourth and fifth on a colonist's mind count for 60%, 45% and 35%, and every one after that a quarter.
- "The favorite" now only comes to someone who really has a request of their own still waiting. Before, it could come to anyone when a wish was granted.

## Eating well is no rut, and lean saves

*20 October 2026, version 0.7.1*

- Fine and lavish meals no longer count towards "the same meal again". A colonist who eats a fine meal every day enjoys it, and doesn't sour on it. Simple meals, pemmican and the like still count, and a varied table is still rewarded.
- Saves stay lean: everything Rimfolk keeps now has a limit, about 2 MB after five years in a large colony. Curfews no longer keep notes on people the game has forgotten, and a removed map is no longer held on to for the defense line shown on it.

## People with a character of their own

*20 October 2026, version 0.7.0*

- Every colonist has a character on eight sides (warmth, nerve, temper, humour, trust, pride, openness, outlook). Traits, the Social skill and age set where they start, and life marks them, with 48 kinds of mark: a raid held or a nerve broken, a friend lost, being nursed through an illness, a craft mastered, a hungry season. The Story tab opens with it: eight bars, and what shaped them, with dates.
- Character shows in how they speak, in talk, gossip and the journal alike: a hothead snaps, a joker jokes, the cold are curt, the gloomy see the worst. It shows in what they do too: the warm have more warm moments, the hot-tempered more frictions, and the trusting confide their secrets sooner.
- Conversations: two colonists who are together now and then really talk, a short exchange over their heads, taking turns. They talk about the last raid, the council, the food, the newcomer, faith and 14 more topics, and what comes up follows the news.
