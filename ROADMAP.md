# Rimfolk roadmap

A living plan. Each phase ends with a build, the XML validator, a review pass and a push. Phases can overlap;
content work runs alongside code work.

## Phase A: Faster and sturdier (light pass done)
Performance is good in play (big medieval modlist, no complaints), so this stays a light pass: apply the cheap,
safe wins from an audit of the per-frame interface and the hourly simulation, and keep every system skipped
entirely when switched off.

## Phase B: The colony's character (done)
The council's choices add up to a reputation. The colony becomes known as merciful or harsh, generous or
thrifty, open or wary, keeper of traditions or restless for change, easygoing or hard-working.
- Council answers, decrees, granted wishes and how strangers are treated shift the colony's ways; old deeds
  slowly fade, so the character can change.
- Colonists approve or disapprove by nature: the kind like mercy, the greedy like thrift, the lazy like ease.
- Visitors talk about the colony's reputation; some dilemmas and encounters only come up for a colony with a
  certain name. Crossing a line is news ("The colony is becoming known as merciful").
- A page in the council window shows the colony's ways, who approves, and what changed them.

## Phase C: Medieval life
More of what makes a long medieval run feel lived-in.
- More decrees (feast days, tithes, curfews, militia drills, alms for the poor), more quirks, more social
  moments, seasonal festivals that can become traditions, more dreams and secrets.
- Era-aware writing throughout.

## Phase D: Reacting to the world (mostly done)
The colony notices what the game does to it. Done so far (Harmony hooks): weddings, births, homecomings, breakdowns,
exact nemesis detection, victories (who fell, who stood out), quests won and lost.
- Weddings, births, breakdowns, victories, caravans coming home with stories, quests won and lost.
- Journals, the chronicle, friends' reactions and life-goal sparks for each.
- Done: Rimfolk's context in the game's own letters (the nemesis riding with a raid, what weighed on a colonist who
  breaks, who someone was when they die); nemeses now arrive with the raid itself.
- Done: everyday journal entries drawn from what the game says is on a colonist's mind that day.

## Phase H: Rimfolk events (first batch done)
Things that happen to the colony from outside, drawn by the storyteller (or on Rimfolk's own timer), written for
every era. Called world events until the world situations below took that name.
- Done: 31 events in five groups (pasts catching up, challenges, old enemies, the land and sky, visitors with a
  purpose), fights behind their own switch, era-appropriate loot and visitors, ties into nemeses, secrets,
  mentorships, journals, the chronicle and the colony's character.
- Done: events that build on each other (a deserter's old unit, the lost child grown, a rematch, the great beast's
  young, fed pilgrims' thanks) and the World page (what's coming, who's out there, what happened lately).
- Done: a second batch for industrial and spacer colonies (each with a tribal and medieval version too): lost
  cargo, a talent scout, a journalist, a travelling doctor, music in the air, a famous visitor.
- Done: a third batch of twelve everyday events with no fights: stranded merchants, a neighbor's order (collected
  five days later, shown on the World page), a traveling tinker, neighbors in need of medicine, a quarrel between
  two neighbors, growing weather, a warm spell, an old grave, a box of old letters, runaway lovers, a family visit
  and a fortune teller.
- Done: a fourth batch of seventeen that remember and unfold: follow-ups to the third batch (a regular buyer, the
  runaway lovers' family, a letter from a relative who visited, the fortune teller's bad fortune spent, a quarrel
  remembered), events drawing on epithets, life goals, vendettas (the one fight, behind the battles switch) and the
  colony's founding day, seasonal ones (a harvest fair, a winter-night traveler, the thaw), a caravan to a far
  market, and four that need a mod (A RimWorld of Magic, Alpha Animals, Dubs Bad Hygiene, Hospitality). Things on
  their way show on the World page.
- Done: a fifth batch of twenty-six for the storyteller's pool: the land (rockslide, warm spring, lightning-struck
  tree, wild bees, fallen star, high water, a sign at dawn), people passing through (old adventurer, mapmaker, silent
  guest, limb-maker, merchant's venture, a prisoner wanted, wounded stranger, envoy), each era's own (lord's tithe,
  crashed drone, signal), pasts knocking (recognized, old commander, former patient, old map), small finds (child's
  find, pet's present, sealed crate) and an arcane tome (A RimWorld of Magic). Outcomes ride on who is sent and what
  the colony has; two can end in a fight, only as the risk of an answer; several leave memos for later events.
- Done: camps. Seven kinds of group (hunters, refugees, prospectors, pilgrims, traders, a smith, a hermit) settle
  part of the map for days to a season: they build their camp from blueprints, live there, hunt and fell trees
  around it, trade, help, ask to join, and bend terms; the colony sets terms, hires, gives gifts, asks them to go or
  drives them off. Tension, letters, chronicle lines, journals and the World page follow it.
- Done: five story chains told in stages over days and weeks (the door in the hill, the fugitive's debt, bad blood,
  the preacher, the lost expedition), branching on the colony's answers and luck, each with several endings in
  letters and the chronicle; pending stages on the World page. Three missions (goods for a neighbor's funeral or
  wedding, a runaway animal to tame, a shrine for pilgrims) offered as real quests in the Quests tab (or letters),
  tracked by Rimfolk with progress on the World page.
- Done: a sixth batch of twenty-six written as data (one worker reads where, when and for whom each may come): the
  land and the season (mushroom flush, white nights, desert bloom, long rains, ice fair, birds returning, the rut,
  first snow, the longest night, an eclipse), callers for each era (scribe, photographer, tourist, spirit singer,
  wandering cook) and for the newer systems (a bonesetter for an ailment, a fiddler for strain, guests for a council
  meeting, a peacemaker for two at odds, a feast or a quarrel with a camp), and old lives (a taste of home, amends,
  headstones, bridge work, a sick herd). Many answers call on someone with the right skill or trait.
- Done: three more story chains as data (the lost letters, the stranger's seeds, the play), and two mission kinds as
  data (rebuilding a town after a fire, storm or flood; provisions for a hard winter, a dry year or a sickhouse).
- Done: four more camp kinds (traveling healers, a procession of the faithful, traveling players, surveyors), each
  with its own good turn; camps follow world events (refugees from a famine or a war say so) and name their creed
  with Ideology. Older events with one title, text or answer got more (a letter from home and the lost child grown
  got new answers).
- Next: more events per group; camps that use tent mods' tents when present; more chains (hooking the storyteller
  batch's memos: the peace bought, the survivor, the star-iron blade) and missions (an apprentice to train, a dignitary to host, refugees to see safely through).

## Phase H2: Scenarios with stakes (done)
- Done: six scenarios (Hot Money, Marooned, Deserters, Bad Blood, Borrowed Time, The Fallen Tyrant) built from six
  scenario parts anyone can add in the scenario editor: a sworn enemy, a debt, deserters, feuding families, a
  reputation, a secret.
- Done: gentler stakes (later, smaller and fewer attacks; smaller debts; a softer feud; a strength setting) and
  four small, gentle starts with one or two colonists (The Inheritance, Master and Apprentice, Runaway Hearts,
  The Lone Hunter), built from two new parts: a bond from the start, and an event around a set day.
- Next: a tribal and a medieval scenario or two, once the parts have been played with.

## Phase H3: World events (first batch done)
Rare happenings across the whole region that last a while and change what the colony meets, on Rimfolk's own slow
rhythm (about one every 30 to 60 days), at most two at a time.
- Done: the framework (a def format most can be written in without code; modifiers on Rimfolk events and, through the
  deeper game hooks, the game's own; beats, questions and endings that read what the colony did; the region
  remembering who helped), the "In the world" block on the World page, the chronicle, settings, dev tools.
- Done: 22 of them. A warlord rises (hunted by the neighbors, a bounty, an ally's march, a truce, waited out, or
  brought down at home), a plague, a famine, a hard winter, locusts, a flood downriver, a neighbor's leader dying, two
  claimants for a seat, a war of creeds (Ideology), peace talks, a mercenary company, a bandit road, a new town, a town
  abandoned, a trading season, a great fair, a pilgrimage, a silver rush, a famous healer, the great herds, strange
  herds (Alpha Animals) and a season of wild magic (A RimWorld of Magic).
- Done: six more, with follow-ups: a long drought upriver (a famine can follow), a new road (a trading season can
  follow), a holy year, a bandit lord's fall (a warlord or sellswords can follow), sickness in the herds (pen the
  animals; a famine can follow), and a ship coming down (industrial and later).
- Next: more of them, and Rimfolk events written to follow up on how one ended (the warlord's son, the town that
  grew from the settlers' camp).

## Phase E: The council at a glance (done)
- Done: an Overview page (the colony's numbers and how they moved this week, graphs of mood, trust, wealth and
  people over the last weeks, and one line from the advisor, the World page, the colony's character and the
  chronicle), and "how often" sliders for encounters, colonist moments, social events and dreams.

## Phase E2: The mod almanac (done for the main modlist)
The Almanac learns about the mods in the user's modlist, and Rimfolk uses that knowledge for new ideas.
- Almanac articles on the mods in play: what each adds, how to get the most out of it, and how it touches
  Rimfolk (medieval factions, hospitality, outposts, quality of life, and so on).
- A reference in the repository (`Modlists/`): each modlist the user shares, plus a list of mods with the
  hooks Rimfolk can use (their pawns, buildings, items, events and settings). New features check it first.
- New or alternative modlists from the user go into the same folder and update the articles.
- Done: 104 Almanac articles covering all 216 mods of the main medieval modlist; notes and `Modlists/HOOKS.md`.
  Next: build the best ideas from HOOKS.md into Rimfolk, a few at a time.

## Phase G: Integrations, the careful way
Built from `Modlists/HOOKS.md`, following the rules in DESIGN.md ("Playing nicely with any modlist"): soft, only
when a mod is active, only what the colony has unlocked, never harder without a mod, no per-run tweaks.
- First: more moments between colonists (conversations that show in the social log and as Interaction Bubbles):
  shared grief, talking shop, praising the cook, old stories, friendly contests that hurt nobody, faith, magic and
  hygiene moments when those mods are present.
- Then: people, magic and beliefs (RimWorld of Magic classes, traits and skills mods, memes); hygiene and cooking
  (bathroom plans, better bath wishes, processors and aging); medieval life (Medieval Overhaul production, wine and
  cheese) when those mods are active.
- Not planned: diplomacy and faction-war features (Diplomatic Marriage, Factional War); weapon duels.
- An audit of existing content against these rules (unlocked-only, mod removal, era fit) comes first.

## The longer road (plan, October 2026)
Rimfolk is now large (about 63,000 lines of code, 3,000 defs, more than a hundred settings) and the game has moved
to 1.7. The next stretch is less about adding systems and more about making what's there run lean, read well and
play deep. In order:

### 1. Steady on 1.7 (first)
- Done (October 2026): builds against the 1.7 reference assemblies (`-p:RimRef=1.7`: no errors, no new warnings);
  every Harmony target, every game, Unity and runtime member the DLL uses, every override, constant and by-name
  lookup checked with `Tools/ApiDiff`, and nothing changed; the XML validates the same against 1.7. 1.7 is in the
  supported versions, and one DLL serves both (on 1.7 the game uses the 1.6 folder; no LoadFolders.xml needed). How to
  check the next version: DESIGN.md, "Checking a new game version". Still to see in game on 1.7: that the patches take
  effect on the new runtime, and that the Needs tab's mood hints join the game's own tooltip (else they show in a box
  of their own).
- Done (October 2026): a dev action, "Rimfolk: check myself" (debug actions, Rimfolk), listing the game, Rimfolk and
  Harmony versions, which patches applied and which didn't (with the reason, now kept by the per-patch bootstrap),
  which switches are off or changed from their defaults, which of the mods Rimfolk works with it found, and its own
  log errors this session, so a log isn't needed to tell what's wrong. It copies to the clipboard for bug reports.
- Done (October 2026): a short in-game test checklist, the Almanac page "Checking that it works": the Good times line,
  ailments, the visual touches, meetings with attendance, the heart in RimHUD and the material counts.

### 2. Lean (optimization)
- One scheduler for everything hourly: today the heart, pressures, nuisances, ailments, camps, world events,
  encounters, wishes and others each run their own hourly pass. One clock that spreads colonists across the hour,
  plus one shared per-map snapshot (rooms, beds, food and medicine, threats, power) worked out once and read by all.
- Def lookups by name in hot paths moved to cached statics; LINQ and string building out of anything drawn per
  frame; a pass with Dubs Performance Analyzer in a long save to find the real hot spots.
- Save size: caps and pruning for every history (chronicle, journals, pair tones, dreams), measured on a five-year
  save.

### 3. Clear (UI/UX)
- Settings presets (light touch, story-rich, demanding) with a search box and grouped pages; each feature's
  switch shows a one-line "what you'll notice".
- A news digest: minor happenings gathered into one "colony news" letter a day instead of many messages; letters
  kept for decisions.
- Done (October 2026): a first-run welcome letter (once per colony; saves that already had Rimfolk skip it without a
  setting) and a "Start here" Almanac page; the council window remembers its page, across sessions too.
- Done (October 2026): Rimfolk's pawn tabs fit the space above RimHUD and other tall inspect panes (checked by tests for
  768 to 2160 pixel screens and interface scales up to 2x), scroll when short, and their sections fold away.
- A small visual pass with the game's own effects: the comet tints the sky, fireflies glow, camps smoke, a warlord's
  raid comes with banners of dust.
- Translation-ready: every string keyed and a guide for translators.

### 4. Deep (features and variety)
- Personal arcs: a colonist's goals, feelings, secrets and history woven into a few personal stories a year (a
  homecoming, a reconciliation, an apprenticeship that ends in a masterwork).
- The colony calendar: player-named holidays, harvest and founding days, traditions that grow from what the colony
  actually celebrates.
- The long view of the world: factions that remember the colony over years (debts, favors, grudges), envoys and
  missions that follow from it, more mission kinds (an apprentice, a dignitary, refugees to see through).
- Health and recovery stories: ailments (started), convalescence, a doctor's reputation, a midwife's.
- Rooms with character: furniture sets per era and mod, room themes, upgrades suggested as the colony grows.
- Variety checks: a dev report of the lines shown most often, so the pools that repeat get new lines first.

### 5. Wider (integrations)
- Deeper with the always-on mods: Hospitality guests as visitors with stories, Vanilla Expanded factions and
  Empire storylines in world events, Medieval and Tribal expansions for era flavor, RimWorld of Magic schools in
  moments and goals, Alpha mods' creatures in encounters.
- New where it pays: PokéWorld (started), tent mods for camps, Big & Small, character editors that change people
  mid-game.
- A compatibility table in the Almanac, generated from what Rimfolk detects.

### 6. Shared (release)
- The Workshop page (private first), preview art from the game's own sprites, and a changelog written for players.
